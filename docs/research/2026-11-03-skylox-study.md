# A 24-day-old blocky channel at 178K subscribers (study, 2026-11-03)

The owner pointed us at a new blocky story channel. The owner's Chrome extension studied all 7 of its Shorts (titles,
transcripts, stills, comments). We take lessons, never its stories, characters or titles.

## The numbers
- **Channel:** 178K subscribers and 7.9M views, from 7 Shorts and 1 long video in its first 11 days of uploads (a Short about every 1.5 days).
- **Views per Short:** 0.38M to 3.1M. The fastest grower is about 600K views a day.
- **Length:** every Short is 50 to 57 s. (Ours: 130 to 145 s. The trend scan's fastest 40: median 106 s.)

## What they do every time
1. **Title:** "What If [your MOM / DAD / BEST FRIEND / you] ... ?" plus one emoji, 6 to 9 words, one or two words in caps.
2. **First second:** the title is spoken as a question with one simple rule, over a close-up of the key character or object.
3. **Escalation:** "Day one ... day five" escalation, then a reversal twist, then often an ending that loops into the start (the top Short ends where it begins and asks viewers to comment LOOP: 2.6x the views of the next).
4. **Pace:** the picture changes every 1 to 2 s (cuts and punch-ins), bright saturated colour, and a counter on screen the whole time (the rule's timer or currency).
5. **Captions:** 1 to 2 words, white with a black outline, key words colour-coded by meaning (family words pink, money green).
6. **Comments:** "Comment WORD if..." at the end, and the channel's own comment is that word (pinned). 15.5K comments on the top Short.
7. **Mid-video subscribe line:** "everyone who subscribes gets [a thing from the story]". We don't copy this. It's aimed at kids, it reads as a false promise, and our channel isn't made for kids.
8. **Shared world:** a recurring "you" character, Mom and a villain, and Shorts that set each other up.
9. **Tone:** comedy, a relatable home conflict (Mom and screen time), warm twists. The warm one grows fastest.

## What fits us
- **Length:** much shorter. Make 60 to 90 s the core and keep the 20 to 45 s experiments. Over 110 s needs a reason.
- **Rule first, spoken as a question or a flat rule in the first second**, over a close-up of what the rule is about. Already a rule, now with the close-up.
- **A rule meter on screen:** a counter or meter tied to the rule, visible the whole time (our story's own number, timer or mark).
- **Loop endings:** the last line should lead straight back into the first.
- **One-word comment prompt:** the end of the description and our posted comment ask for one easy word tied to the story ("Comment KNOCK if you'd open the door"). Our comment job already posts the comment. We can't pin it.
- **Faster picture:** a cut or punch-in every 1 to 2 s, never a still moment over 2 s.
- **Mood:** brighter, more saturated grades on the closer-to-Roblox look. Dark, murky grades are fine for one scene, not a whole Short.
- **Relatable home conflict:** a parent, sibling or best friend with a wish or a fight anyone has had. A creepy or fun rule on top. The warm turn is a sting, not the whole story.
- **Recurring cast (try it):** a series with a recurring "me" and family, where Shorts can mention each other. Each Short still stands alone.

## What doesn't fit
- The game's name, its currency or its avatars in titles, tags or the picture (our hard rules).
- Aiming at children or "subscribe to get" promises.
- The same template every time: the "superficial differences" rule (hard rule 5). They can afford it. We vary the skeleton.

## Risks they show
Scam spam near the top of their comments, viewers calling it "AI", and mockery of a bad sound effect. Our harsh-sound checks and the critic's sound pass cover the last one.
