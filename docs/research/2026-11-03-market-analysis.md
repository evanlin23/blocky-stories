# Market analysis: what the fastest blocky Shorts do (2026-11-03)

Data: every trend scan so far (`docs/trends/2026-11-02.json`, `2026-11-03.json`, 212 unique Shorts from 139
channels, published Oct 20 to Nov 3) plus the first deep pass (`docs/trends/deep/2026-11-03.json`: the 40 fastest,
with channels, tags, descriptions and 794 top comments). Numbers came from short scripts over those files. Re-run them
on each new scan.

**How far to trust this.** It's two scans one day apart, so it's one sample, not a trend. The sample comes from
YouTube search sorted by views on ten Roblox-worded queries, so it shows winners and misses the many small Shorts
that never surfaced (survivorship). "Views per day" favours Shorts in their first big push. Confidence levels below:
**high** means a big gap on a decent sample that fits earlier research; **medium** means a clear gap on a small or
biased sample; **low** means a lead to test.

The bar: the **top 10%** is the 21 fastest (median 1.51M views/day). The **median band** is the 40th to 60th
percentile (43 Shorts, median 174K/day). The bottom half (106) median is 21K/day.

## 1. Length: two winning shapes, and the middle loses
| Length | Shorts | Median views/day | In the top 10% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 45 s or less | 81 | 114K | 5 |
| 46 to 75 s | 28 | 119K | **0** |
| 76 to 110 s | 32 | 238K | 3 |
| 111 to 150 s | 51 | 185K | 7 |
| 151 to 180 s | 20 | **419K** | 6 |

- **The top 10% has a median length of 140 s** (middle half 100 to 155 s). The median band's is 55 s, and the bottom
  half's 53 s. The deep 40's median is 110 s.
- **The winners come in two shapes.** Full stories at 100 to 175 s (16 of the top 21) and quick gags or POV clips at
  13 to 45 s (5 of 21: a family meme, a sibling gag, a "Pt 2" power premise). **Nothing between 46 and 75 s made the
  top 10%** (0 of 28).
- Our one hit, back-row, ran 138 s with 84% average viewed and 67% still watching at the end.
- **This contradicts the reset's "60 to 90 s is the core".** That came from one channel (seven Shorts at 50 to 57 s).
  Across 139 channels, story Shorts that win run long. **Confidence: medium-high** (the gap is big and holds in both
  scans, but the sample is search-biased, and a fast-growing new channel shows 50 to 57 s can work too).

## 2. Title shape
| | Top 10% (21) | Median band (43) | Bottom half (106) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median words (hashtags removed) | **5** | 7 | 7 |
| Ends with or has an emoji | **14 (67%)** | 19 (44%) | 47 (44%) |
| Hashtags in the title | **6 (29%)** | 27 (63%) | 69 (65%) |
| A word in CAPS | 4 (19%) | 11 (26%) | 32 (30%) |
| Question | **0** | 2 | 6 |
| Starts "In this town/world" | 1 | 4 | 3 |
| "What if" | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| A number | 2 | 6 | 35 (33%) |
| First person (I, my) | 2 | 5 | 8 |

Among story-length Shorts only (90 s and up, 96 Shorts), the fastest fifth (19) are the same: 5 words, 13 of 19 have
an emoji, 5 of 19 hashtags, 0 questions, and 6 of 19 are **"The [thing] ..." noun titles** ("The Knocker Copies Your
Voice", "The Tooth Fairies Project", "The Greeter In The Woods") against 9 of 77 in the rest.

- **Short titles win: about 5 words, one emoji at the end, no hashtags, no question.** Confidence: medium (it holds
  inside the story-length group, so it isn't only a length effect, but a few channels with house styles drive it: Gneef
  has 4 of the top 21 and GreenBroRants 3).
- **Caps are neutral to slightly negative** (19% of the top against 26 to 30%). The reset's "one word in caps" isn't
  supported, and isn't contradicted strongly either. Confidence: low.
- **Questions and "What if" never appear among the fast ones** (0 of 21, and "what if" is 0 of 212). Our queries
  don't search for "what if", so that's a blind spot, not proof. Confidence: low for "what if", medium for questions.
- **A number in the title is a bottom-half sign** (33% of the bottom, 10% of the top). Confidence: low to medium.

## 3. Format and the opening
- **Format by query** (median views/day, and how many of the top 21 it holds): "roblox story" 986K, 14 of 21; rule
  premise 407K, 4; POV 252K, 3; horror 227K, 3; comedy 209K, 1; riddle 57K, 1; series / Part 2 38K, 1; sad /
  emotional 68K, **0**; eerie lore 251; unbranded "blocky animation story" 4. The top is broad stories with a
  premise, not a sub-genre. Confidence: medium.
- **An unbranded blocky search finds almost nothing** (12 Shorts, median 4 views a day). We can't use the brand (hard
  rule 4), so reach has to come from the look and the feed, not search. Confidence: medium.
- **Descriptions don't tell us the opening line.** 10 of 40 descriptions are empty and 10 more are the same block of
  five hashtags. Only one channel (GreenBroRants) opens its descriptions with a teaser in the narrator's voice ("I
  always knew there was something off about her"). 19 of 40 have no tags. **Descriptions and tags don't separate
  winners.** Confidence: high.
- **The same five-hashtag description shows up on six channels' Shorts** (Gneef, LuckyDuck, studby, Baseplate,
  Laftty and others). That looks like a shared studio or network. Part of the top of this market is a few production
  houses, not lone creators.

## 4. What commenters react to (794 top comments on the 40 fastest)
Themes from keyword coding, so read them as rough shares:

| Theme | Share of comments | On how many of the 40 |
|---|---|---|
| **Quotes a line from the video back** | **25%** | 34 |
| Laugh reaction (😭💀😂, lol) | 24% | 40 |
| Names a family member | 11% | 26 |
| Praises the format or lore ("in this world never gets old", "deeper lore than movies") | 6% | 21 |
| Emotional, cried, or vents about their own life | 6% | 18 |
| Like-bait, spam, "edit: thanks for the likes" | 5% | 22 |
| Self-insert ("this is me", "my mom does this") | 5% | 19 |
| Compares with another of the channel's stories ("same as the hug one") | 3% | 12 |
| Loophole or plot logic ("just tell Simon...", "how did the bird land") | 3% | 13 |
| Spots a detail or reference | 2% | 14 |
| **Scared** | **1%** | 6 |
| Asks for a part 2 | 1% | 4 |
| Answers the story's question about themselves | ~0% | 1 |

- **People comment on lines, not on scares.** A quotable, funny or odd line is the most common thing in the top
  comments, and the laugh is the most common reaction even on horror-tagged Shorts. Fright almost never gets a
  comment. **Write one quotable line per story** (a strange rule detail said flatly, a funny aside). Confidence:
  medium-high (big gap, 34 of 40 videos, crude coding).
- **Family is in a quarter of the videos' comments** (26 of 40), so a relatable family conflict gets talked about.
  This backs the reset's premise rule. Confidence: medium.
- **Our "answer about yourself" pin prompt has no organic match** (1 comment in 794). The reset's "Comment WORD"
  prompt is better supported (skylox study: 15.5K comments on its top Short), but no Short in this sample uses it,
  so it's untested here. Confidence: low.
- **Comment rates are tiny everywhere:** median 0.01% of views (top 20: 0.019%, the rest 0.010%). Back-row's 7 on
  22K (0.03%) is normal. One outlier at 0.74% is a "say my name" power premise whose comments are viewers typing
  their own names. **A premise that makes the viewer type something specific is the one proven comment engine
  here.** Confidence: medium (one Short, a huge gap).
- **Like rate:** top 20 median 2.3%, the rest 1.8%. Back-row's 2.4% is at the top level. Confidence: medium.

## 5. Can a new channel still break in?
| Channel age | In the deep 40 | Median views/day |
|---|---|---|
| under 6 months | 5 (3 channels) | 802K |
| 6 to 13 months | 13 (6 channels) | 1.03M |
| 13 months to 3 years | 5 (5 channels) | 982K |
| over 3 years | 17 (9 channels) | 1.17M |

- **Yes: 18 of the 40 fastest are from channels under 13 months old,** and they're about as fast as old ones. The
  scope research found the same (PurpleHat joined June 2026, Baseplate February 2026), and the skylox study found a
  24-day-old channel at 178K subscribers. Confidence: medium-high.
- **But the young winners are already big.** The young channels in the 40 have 57K to 2.9M subscribers, and only 5
  of the 40 come from channels under 100K (51K to 89K). Subscriber count doesn't predict a Short's speed (under 100K:
  median 1.03M/day; 100K to 500K: 1.29M; over 1.5M: 1.52M), so the feed tests each Short on its own. Our 144
  subscribers aren't the block. Confidence: medium.
- **The young winners post a lot:** Xenost 249 videos in 119 days, GreenBroRants 156 in 391, studby 258 in 269,
  Baseplate 209 in 261. That's one to two a day. Our one a day is in range, no more. Confidence: medium.

## 6. Publish hour
| Published (Central) | Shorts | Median views/day | In the top 10% |
|---|---|---|---|
| midnight to 4 AM | 18 | 185K | 3 |
| 4 to 8 AM | 44 | **234K** | 5 |
| 8 AM to noon | 51 | 116K | 5 |
| noon to 4 PM | 42 | 167K | 2 |
| 4 to 8 PM | 33 | 155K | 4 |
| 8 PM to midnight | 24 | 160K | 2 |

- **No hour clearly wins.** 4 to 8 AM Central (10:00 to 14:00 UTC) has the highest median, but the top 10% is spread
  across every block, and per-hour samples are 1 to 15. Our 4 PM Central publish time is fine. **Don't change it on
  this.** Confidence: low.
- Weekday: no pattern (Monday is lowest at 110K, n=25; the rest are 127K to 216K).

## 7. Which formats are rising or falling
- **We can't say yet.** The "vs last scan" column compares scans **one day apart**, not a week. The reset's "sad /
  emotional falling 14% in a week" is a one-day move in the median of ten Shorts. By the 2026-11-03 rerun it was
  171K against 203K (-16%), and rule premise went +6%, roblox story +24%, riddle +17%, horror -8%. All are inside the
  day-to-day noise of a ten-video median.
- **A rough within-scan check** (median views/day of Shorts under 7 days old against 7 to 14 days, all formats 1.37x):
  riddle 11x (n=6), sad 4.3x (n=12), rule premise 3.8x (n=3), series 2.8x, POV 2.6x, horror 2.4x, comedy 1.5x, roblox
  story 1.2x. With samples of 3 to 12 this is noise, but it doesn't back "sad is dying" either. Confidence: low.
- **What we can say:** sad / emotional holds **0 of the top 21** and its Shorts are short (median 43 s). The sad
  *format* isn't where the big story Shorts are. That's a different claim from "sadness hurts a story". Confidence:
  medium.
- **To know:** four weekly scans (through 2026-11-23). `trends.py` keeps the history.

## What this changes (also in `playbook.md`)
1. **Length:** make story Shorts 100 to 150 s again, and keep the 20 to 45 s gags. Drop 46 to 75 s.
2. **Titles:** about 5 words, one emoji at the end, no hashtags, no question, no number. "The [thing] ..." noun
   titles are worth testing.
3. **One quotable line per story,** odd or funny, said flatly, meant to be typed back in the comments.
4. **Publish hour and descriptions:** leave them.
5. **Formats:** don't drop any on one-day momentum. Re-judge after 4 scans.
