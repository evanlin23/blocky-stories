# Rule proposals from reel research round 1 (not adopted)

**Status: proposals for the owner to decide.** Nothing here is in RULES.md. The owner, 2026-11-01: "wait for more research to come in. We want to keep a similar length target. We don't want to copy 1 person specifically." Each proposal below rests on a pattern seen on **at least 3 different accounts** in `2026-11-01-reel-watch-notes.md` (15 hits and 4 flops across 7 Instagram accounts; plays from 1.5M to 11M). Round 2 (YouTube Shorts plus audio) should confirm or kill each one before it's adopted.

Each proposal says what it is, the evidence, whether it conflicts with a current rule, and a recommendation.

## A. No conflict with current rules (adopt after round 2 confirms them on YouTube)

1. **The first line states one rule that touches everyone.** It's "In this world/town, …" plus a concrete rule, in under 16 words. All 15 hits, all 7 accounts. Commenters treat it as a brand ("You know the lore is gonna be peak when you hear 'IN THIS TOWN'").
   - *Conflict:* RULES bans batch 1's "At the PLACE, rule" opener. That ban came from our own 3 stories sharing one shape, not from viewers. This opener is the genre's signature, and the ban could narrow to "never the same rule *type* twice in a batch".
   - *Recommendation:* adopt, and replace the opener ban with a variety rule on the rule itself.
2. **The rule can be seen within 2 seconds,** ideally as a marker on people: a counter, tag, string, bell, balloon or glow. 12 of 15 hits, and markers on people appear on 5 accounts. Both format flops failed exactly here: a dark, unreadable opening, and a rule that couldn't be seen.
   - *Conflict:* partial. RULES says to keep the set free of text, because real-looking signage reads as a real claim. A number or glyph floating over a character isn't signage.
   - *Recommendation:* adopt. Allow "markers of our own design (numbers, made-up glyphs, objects)", and keep the ban on real words and places.
3. **A 3 to 5 example montage in the first 15 s** shows how the rule shapes daily life, often funny ("Grandmas come back just to judge your cooking"). 10 of 15 hits, all 7 accounts. *No conflict. Recommendation:* adopt.
4. **The narrator is the exception to the rule** (300 steps left, the one without powers, the bell that never rang). 7 hits, 5 accounts. *No conflict. Recommendation:* adopt as the default shape.
5. **The climax turns the rule's own logic against itself:** a loophole or a second reading, never new magic. 9 hits, 6 accounts. *No conflict.* It fits our "twist planted early" and lore rules. *Recommendation:* adopt, with the critic checking it.
6. **The last line echoes the first line or the rule.** 9 hits, 6 accounts. Already in RULES ("a last line that loops back into the first"). *Recommendation:* keep, and have the critic check it.
7. **Fast cutting: a new shot every 1.2 to 2.3 s.** 14 of 15 hits, all 7 accounts. ep13 measured about 2.6 s a shot. *No conflict. Recommendation:* adopt as "at least one cut every 2.3 s on average", which `preflight` can measure from the timeline.
8. **Bright, readable openings.** Detailed, well-lit sets with the blocky cast, and the creepy entity as a detailed model that contrasts with them. All 7 accounts, and both format flops opened dark.
   - *Conflict:* partial. "The look" asks for fog, flicker and long shadows. Those can stay for the scares, but the first 2 s must read clearly on a phone.
   - *Recommendation:* adopt "the first 2 s are lit to read; darkness comes later".

## B. Conflicts with current rules (owner decides)

9. **Family or a loved one at stake** (a sister, dad, mom, grandpa). 9 hits, 5 accounts. Our stories have used strangers, coworkers and an uncle.
   - *Conflict:* none directly, but RULES keeps everyone "adults or teens, never in peril". A loved one at stake can still be an adult (a parent or grandparent).
   - *Recommendation:* adopt with adult loved ones only.
10. **A first-person kid narrator** ("when I was little…"). 6 accounts.
    - *Conflict:* **yes.** RULES bans child characters because of the made-for-kids risk, and YouTube removes kid-styled videos with mature themes.
    - *Recommendation:* don't adopt. Use "when I was a teenager…" or an adult looking back. The research doesn't show the kid narrator itself drives views; it's a genre default.
11. **One word per caption.** Bold, high contrast, about 70% down the frame. 6 accounts.
    - *Conflict:* yes, with "two or three words".
    - *Recommendation:* test it on one pilot story first, because it changes the house look the owner asked to keep consistent (note 21).
12. **Length: median about 106 s** (66 to 159 s across the hits).
    - *Conflict:* the owner set 2:15 to 2:30 and asked to keep a similar target. The creepy hits ran 2:22 and 2:39, inside our range.
    - *Recommendation:* keep the target, as the owner said, and let `metrics` decide later.

## C. Tone

The 15 hits split 6 creepy, 5 wonder or fantasy, 4 emotional, and **11 of 15 have a warm or emotional beat**, even the creepy ones. That backs our existing "creepy, then a warm turn" rule. A "magical rule that turns eerie" premise fits our creepy lane and the genre's biggest shape. *Recommendation:* no change. Prefer rule-premises that start as wonder and turn eerie.

## What the flops teach (all 4)

- The rule wasn't visible early.
- Nothing personal was at stake.
- The captions were thin and low-contrast.
- They ran long without the early montage.
- Two had no world rule at all.

The scripts themselves were fine. The gap is in the first seconds and the stakes.
