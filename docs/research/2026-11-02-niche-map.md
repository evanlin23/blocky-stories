# Niche map: 66 blocky Shorts channels across 10 niches (owner's extension, data as of 2026-11-02)

**Method.** The owner's browser extension checked 5 to 12 channels per niche and each channel's 3 newest Shorts. It counted a channel as "new" if its first visible Short is under 90 days old. Voice type is unconfirmed (the extension can't hear audio). Kid risk is judged from themes and comments. Samples are small, so the medians are rough. Near the end, YouTube began answering lookups with "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot", and the extension stopped there (data collection was already done). Don't push browser studies harder than this. The full channel list is a CSV on the owner's machine (not in the repo).

## What it means for us
1. **Our lane is right, and the creepy angle is what sets us apart.** "Quiet dread" rule channels that are weeks old get 0.35M to 1.9M on new Shorts. Cheerful near-identical town-rule copies get 0.4K to 13K. Standalone blocky horror is nearly empty (5 channels, all small).
2. **What-if power/price has the best median (377K)** and ports to a narrator with no brand names. That makes it our strongest experiment lane.
3. **Sequels are wanted and rarely made.** Viewers write their own "Part 2" endings, and almost no rule channel keeps a recurring host. Try a recurring host (someone who keeps the town's rulebook, one rule per Short, adult or teen per hard rule 2) and Part 2s of anything that beats the median.
4. **Horror puzzles are a gap.** "Which one is not human?" got 1M with few channels doing it. It's a short experiment that fits our engine: line up blocky people, the viewer picks, then the reveal.
5. **Avoid:** POV skits (falling), game-fandom (not ours to use), "Mom vs Dad" family skits (oversaturated at the bottom, highest made-for-kids risk).
6. **Small channels still break in:** several under 10K subscribers get 100K+ on their newest Shorts. The feed tests anyone, so the first second decides (`metrics/studio/2026-11-02.md`).
