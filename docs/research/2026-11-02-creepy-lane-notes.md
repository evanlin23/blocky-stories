Research for Panda Nano Adventures' creepy blocky-story lane. Read only: no likes, follows, subscribes or comments. YouTube's caption setting was switched back to Off afterwards.

## How this was done
- **Search:** YouTube with the **Shorts** filter for "in this world roblox horror", "in this town roblox scary", "roblox horror story rule" and "creepy roblox story twist". That gave 332 unique Shorts. I checked publish dates on the top 110 by views; **32 were posted since Aug 2**.
- **The 10 hits:** the most-viewed **creepy/horror** story Shorts from that set, with at least 5 channels.
  - Left out:
    - Gelo's "Your Son Is Haunted" (9.6M): it's an emotional father/son story, already covered in notes 2.
    - A 7 s obby clip: not a story.
  - The 10th slot went to the best Short from a 5th channel (Baseplate, 5.7M) rather than a second Revan Short (7.8M), to meet the 5-channel minimum.
  - Channel mix: Gelo Stories ×4, BloxyRiddles ×3, Revan, Ryex, Baseplate.
- **2 low-view contrasts (<50K):** creepy rule Shorts from the same searches, over 2 days old: Blox Park (22.8K) and Blox Admin (8.7K).
- **"With sound":** each Short was played in full at 1× speed:
  - **Words** come from YouTube's auto-transcript (not hand-checked; a few mis-hearings are flagged).
  - **Audio** was measured live: loudness, a bass/drone level per second, **silences** (near-silent stretches ≥0.5 s) and **SFX hits** (sharp broadband spikes).
  - **Shots and the creature** were checked from frames captured at every cut.
  - Music genre and voice "feel" can't be judged this way, so they aren't claimed.
- **Comments:** the 5 most-liked among the ~100 YouTube loads by default.

### 1. In This Town Nobody Steps on the Ground… 😧
- **URL:** https://www.youtube.com/shorts/VTJLSVSIfkQ · **Channel:** Gelo Stories · **Posted:** Oct 14 · **Views:** 16.4M · **Likes:** 323K · **Comments:** 603 · **Length:** 118 s
- **First line:** "In this town, nobody has stepped on the ground in 100 years."
- **Rule in the first 2 s:** shown, not explained. Townspeople and even a dog are on tall stilts in a grassy town, with the caption "In this town / nobody has / stepped on the **ground**" (key word in colour). By 3 s: "Everyone's on stilts".
- **First scare:** a mild unease beat at **~29–31 s** ("A girl pointed at my feet and fainted"). The **first real scare is at ~44–55 s**: Beto's stilt snaps, "The ground shook. Thunder far below… the earth rose like a wave and rolled him away." The sub-bass swells at 48 s.
- **The creature:**
  - It's shown only indirectly for 70 s: warm grass (36 s), the ground "rose and fell slowly like breathing" (38 s), thunder from below, and the wave of earth taking Beto (51 s).
  - It's first seen properly at **73–76 s**, when the hill **opens one eye** ("It wasn't a hill. It was a head"). It's a giant mossy green head lying face-down under the town.
  - It's shown fully (head close-ups) from 78 to 99 s. It barely moves: breathing, blinking, a sigh. It speaks once: "My back."
- **Music:** no full silences. The **bass jumps about 14 dB from 63 to 67 s**, a sustained drone under the mayor's threat ("Tonight you come down barefoot… If the ground wants someone it's you"). It drops at 68–70 s as he's lowered on a rope, then a single big low hit lands at **85 s** ("My back.").
- **Twist:** the creature isn't eating people. "He didn't take people to eat them. He took them to scratch his back." Every step "tickled the giant", so the thunder was laughter. **Planted:** the warm grass, the breathing ground, thunder "with a clear sky" and the hill shape in early wide shots.
- **Warm turn:** a light one at ~92–106 s. Beto is "Safe and sound and exhausted", the narrator helps scratch with a rake, and "The kids learned they could run." It's played as comic relief more than emotion.
- **Ending/last line:** a comeuppance gag. The giant picks the lazy, long-nailed mayor as his scratcher: "99 years to go. **Like and subscribe or you scratch like the mayor.**"

## Answers

### 1. How do creepy hits keep the rule format but stay scary?

They keep every part of the "In this town…" rule template from notes 1 and 2: a one-line rule, an odd-looking rule image in 2 s, a quick gag montage, a newcomer as the exception, a twist planted early, and a callback ending. On top of that, they add a **horror layer**:

1. **The opening image is odd, not frightening, so the scare is held back.**
   - 8 of 10 open on something absurd or uncanny that reads as funny: people on stilts, walking on their heads, paper bags, giant hat brims, eating face-first, kids in lunch cages, blank name tags.
   - Only **Ryex** and **Baseplate** put the monster in frame 0.
2. **The newcomer breaks the rule and nothing happens to them; a bystander pays instead.** This happens in 7 of 10 (Gelo ×4, Revan, BloxyRiddles ×2):
   - "Nothing happened. But a man fell off his stilts…"
   - "Nothing came… Two days later, Mr. Dale's neck gave out."
   - The narrator is safe, which makes the rule feel random and the town's fear feel earned.
3. **The first victim is taken off-screen and leaves an object behind.** 5 of 10, across 3 channels:
   - "Only the cone was left" (Gelo, Hands)
   - "Only Martha's phone was left, lying in the grass" (Gelo, Name)
   - "His hat landed in the street" (BloxyRiddles, Standing)
   - "Just his empty paper bag rolling down the street" (BloxyRiddles, Paper Bag)
   - "the pizza, still steaming… Not one slice missing" (Revan, Eating)
4. **The creature is heard and inferred before it's seen:** a shadow sliding over rooftops, a whistle, ticking that speeds up, the ground breathing, streetlights dying "one by one".
5. **The town turns on the narrator.** 7 of 10 (Gelo ×4, Revan, BloxyRiddles ×2) have the same set-piece:
   - Townspeople block the road out ("Hundreds of them, silent. Every single one holding a padlock").
   - The **mayor** sentences the narrator: "Since you came…", "Tonight, you sleep in the square, face up", "If the ground wants someone it's you".
   - The narrator is left alone in the square at midnight and the creature comes. Being sacrificed by your own town is the core dread.
6. **The creature arrives with a close-up and stops before hurting him.** 6 of 10 (Revan, Gelo, BloxyRiddles ×2, Baseplate):
   - It leans down face-to-face ("bent down until that blank face was an inch from mine", "That huge grin was right in my face").
   - In the friendly-twist stories it then **kneels**.
7. **The twist reframes the monster.** All 10 twists were planted early:
   - 6 of 10 turn it **misunderstood**: itchy, hungry, nameless, a guardian, a referee, the viewers.
   - 3 of 10 turn it **worse**: the nice kid is the lure, you were the fourth, you become Mr. Tick.
   - 1 is a cliffhanger.
8. **The sound flips from thin to heavy.** See question 3; it's the main sound move in the hits and missing from both low-view Shorts.

**What the low-view contrasts lacked:**
- L1 is almost the same story as hit #3 (a name-taking faceless creature with a blank tag, named by the new kid). It has no rule image at 2 s, 2 gags instead of 5, a creature too dark to read, a flat soundtrack with no drone, ~5 s shots, and a sweet ending with no sting.
- L2 never shows the threat, shows the rule as a sign, and lands its only scare in the last 7 s with a twist set up just 20 s earlier.

**Warning sign:** the top comments on Gelo's Shorts are now about the formula, not the story:
- "Every story is 'We moved in ___day. No one told us the rule'… then he meets the Mayor and in the end he's the hero who frees everyone"
- "bro he always ends up in the 'square'"
- "can you make a diffrent story like no major or any creature"

The template still gets views (8–16M), but viewers have noticed it, so a channel entering now should vary the pieces.

### 2. When does the first real scare land?

- **Median: about 41 s into the story (range 23–54 s), i.e. 25–40% of the way through.** By hit: 23, 31, 35, 38, 40, 42, 43, 44, 49, 54 s.
- It's almost always **the first person taken**, with the object-left-behind beat.
- Before that, the first ~20–30 s are rule set-up and jokes. The early "scares" are **social**: the town panicking at the newcomer, a woman screaming, a man diving into a bush. Those are played for laughs.
- **Exception:** Ryex and Baseplate open on the monster itself (0–0.5 s) as a visual hook, then still hold the first story scare to 23 s and 54 s.
- In the low-view L2, the first visible scare comes at ~63 of 70 s, too late.

### 3. How much of the creature do they show?

- **Very little, and late.** In 8 of 10 the creature isn't clearly seen until **55–71% of the way through** (62–115 s).
  - Before that there are only traces: a shadow over rooftops, a hand reaching down, the ground rising, an empty object left behind.
  - Paper Bag shows it briefly at 32% (52 s), then hides it again until 95 s.
  - Eating shows it small and dog-like at 31 s, then grows it night by night: "Monday… over the fence. Wednesday… my bedroom window".
- **The two exceptions** (Ryex's MOVING, Baseplate's Mr. Tick) show the full creature in the first second as the thumbnail-style hook.
- **When it's finally shown:**
  - It's in full, well lit, often in close-up.
  - It's almost always **"too tall, too thin, faceless or grinning"**: "Taller than the streetlights, black as ink, no face", "Tall, thin, too tall. Smooth white skin where its face should be", yellow skeletal grinners, "taller than the clock tower".
  - **It always moves slowly:** bending, studying, tilting its head, kneeling. None of the 10 uses a fast chase or a lunge at the camera.
  - It speaks once at most, usually one short line ("My back.", "I'm Bob.", "You were the fourth.", "Found you.").
- **The sound does the creature's work before it's seen:** ticking that speeds up, a whistle, thunder from below, a low drone.

### 4. Do the creepy hits still have a warm turn?

**Most do, but they don't end on it.**

| Warm turn | Count | Shorts |
|---|---|---|
| Clear warm turn | 3 | #2 Eating (Pebble eats from his hand; the town eats outside), #3 Name (naming Bob; the taken return), #6 Paper Bag ("You found me"; the bags come off) |

| None | 2 | #4 MOVING (only "Don't take him, please"), #9 Walking Alone (bleak) |

- So **6 of 10 resolve warmly**. The same misunderstood-monster structure as the emotional lane, which explains why Gelo's horror titles carry "#sad".
- **The creepy lane's last line is almost never the warm moment.** It's a sting:
  - **A villain comeuppance**, usually the mayor, in 5 of 10 (the long-nailed scratcher, "Pom Pom", the bucket head, the red card, the faceless thing turning to the mayor).
  - **A horror loop**: "I found you." (Mr. Tick); the next letter to Mrs. Fern (Standing).
  - **A threat-joke CTA** in 8 of 10: "Like and subscribe or you scratch like the mayor", "…or I'll tell Pebble you didn't share", "…or from today your name is Pom Pom", "…or I'll tell it to check under your bag", "…or you're next".
- **The darkest Shorts** (Mr. Tick, Walking Alone, Standing Up) have the most "plot twist"/"Netflix series"/"we need a whole story" comments. **The friendly-monster Shorts** get "Pebble is so cute"/"Hi Bob"-style affection plus more template-fatigue comments.

### Sound summary (all 12)
- **No true silences anywhere.** None of the 12 Shorts drops to silence for ≥0.5 s; the creepy lane doesn't use dead air.
- **Instead, the bed starts thin and then turns heavy:**
  - The first third runs on a light, bass-poor bed.
  - **A sustained low drone (+15 to +40 dB of bass) enters at one moment and holds to the end.**
  - That moment is usually **the first on-screen taking or the town/mayor condemning the narrator**: 16, 44, 54, 59, 63, ~65, 71, 78 s in eight hits; ~61 s median for the narrated hits.
  - BloxyRiddles' Standing Up uses **repeated heartbeat-like low booms** instead (94–126 s).
- **The low-view Shorts have no drone at all:** L1's bass is flat throughout, and L2's is choppy with no build.
- **SFX are sparse (0–13 per Short) and cluster on the taking and the reveal.** Some sounds are narrated rather than played ("Tick, tick, tick", "Click", "dong").
