# PurpleHat (@PurpleHatRoblox) Shorts study

Studied 11 Nov 2026 (CST). Channel at the time: about 700K subscribers, 120 videos, roughly 96 Shorts in the grid.

## How this was gathered (and what's missing)

- **Read-only.** I didn't like, comment, subscribe or download anything, and no sign-in or bot check came up.
- **Dates, lengths and views** come from each Short's page data. View counts are what showed on 11 Nov.
- **Spoken lines** come from YouTube's auto-generated English captions. The "first line" quotes are word for word from those captions, so a word could be off here and there. The last word or two is sometimes cut off.
- **Visuals** come from paused frames at chosen timestamps (0:00, about 0:02, about 0:06, the middle, the twist, the end). Captions shown are the ones burned into the video.
- **Cut rate** is an estimate. I compared sampled frames every 0.5s over the first 15–20s.
- **I could not hear audio.** For music and sound effects I only have the caption tags ([music], [laughter]), sounds written into the narration ("Tick", "Ding", "Creak"), and what commenters said.
- **Pinned comments:** none of the 13 Shorts showed a pinned comment, so that part is "none" for every Short.
- **Comments** are summarized in my own words. No usernames are included.

### The big finding before the details

The channel has **two clearly different formats**, and it switched between them in the last month:

| | **Old format: "roast" Shorts** (Aug to Oct, plus a few in Nov) | **New format: "In this world…" Shorts** (from about 31 Oct) |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 48–74s | 129–158s (2:09–2:38) |
| Opener | "I was [doing X] when…" | "In this world, [magic rule]…" |
| Story | A rude stranger (often a "Karen") makes an absurd demand. Purple fires back one-liners, and a final twist humiliates the antagonist. | A world with one magic rule, then a family or friend problem, a race against a clock, and a warm ending |
| Recurring line | "I smiled and said…", "Bro…" | "I'm Purple, I'm 12, and…" |
| Views | All of the channel's top 10 (14–26M) | 117K–14.8M. Most get 0.3–2M, with outliers at 4–14M |

Commenters keep asking for the roasts to come back. When a roast Short comes out (e.g., *Red Or Blue*), comments cheer that "the old stuff is back." At the same time, "I'm Purple, I'm 12" has become a well-liked catchphrase.

## 1. She Wanted Me To Hit And Run... 💀
**26.3M · 1:01 · 25 Sep 2026 · Popular #1**

**First 3 seconds.**
- **First image:** a low shot from the hood of a bright purple sports car, with Purple (yellow head, purple bucket hat) driving, on a sunny city street with palm trees.
- **First line:** "I was driving slowly when a woman jumped in front of my car and threw herself on the ground…"
- **On-screen text:** "I was driving" (white, with "was" in yellow).
- **Why you'd stop:** a woman throws herself in front of a car within 2 seconds, so the injustice is clear right away. The car is the channel's signature purple, so you know whose video it is.

**Beats.**
- **0:00–0:04, premise:** a pink-haired woman fakes being hit.
- **0:04–0:08, escalation:** she demands that he call the cops and that he pay $500 for her "broken leg."
- **Middle:** Purple roasts her ("you're more likely to die from that guy bumping into you," pointing at an old man with a cane), and the crowd laughs. She calls the police on herself to report a "hit and run," and he says she's "missing both parts."
- **Twist (about 0:46–0:56):** a mustached cop is unimpressed ("running away by standing still?"). Purple turns her dent accusation around, and she admits the dent was there "before I threw myself on the ground."
- **Last line:** a cop arrests her with a joke about using her blinker.
- **Loop:** no. It ends on a payoff.

**Characters.**
- **Purple:** the classic yellow blocky "noob" head, a big purple bucket hat, a black-and-purple striped sweater and black pants with purple trim. He's readable from any distance.
- **The antagonist:** bubblegum-pink hair, round glasses, a lavender crop-top outfit.
- **The cop:** a huge curled mustache and a blue uniform.
- **Faces:** drawn-on 2D expressions on the Roblox head (wide eyes, tears, a gritted mouth). They are very exaggerated: she cries cartoon tear streams and shows a huge open-mouth scream.
- **Proportions:** standard blocky Roblox R6, used for physical comedy (she lies sprawled on the asphalt).
- **Telling characters apart:** each one has a single loud color or feature: purple hat, pink hair, mustache.

**Animation and camera.**
- Smooth, high-quality, Blender-style rendering of Roblox avatars with real-looking lighting and shadows.
- A new shot about every 1–1.5s (roughly 10–15 big changes in the first 15s).
- Lots of punch-ins to the face at reaction moments. There are low angles (from the hood) and over-the-shoulder shots.
- The auto captions tag [music] and [laughter], so there's background music and a laugh track on the crowd moment.

**Captions.**
- A heavy, rounded, slightly italic sans-serif in white with a dark drop shadow or outline, roughly 60% of the way down the frame.
- 2–3 words at a time ("I was driving", "of my car", "right now").
- One key word per caption is yellow.
- Sentence case.

**Comments.**
- **Pinned:** none.
- **Themes:** people quote the woman's self-own about the dent (the most-liked type of comment), joke that the protagonist can afford a supercar but not laundry, praise the animation getting better, and ask "how do you make videos like this?" There's some off-topic spam.
- **Overall:** quoting the funniest line. Very little arguing.

## 4. He Tried To Steal My Wife... 💀💪
**18.0M · 0:51 · 30 Aug 2026 · Popular #4, and the GUESS pick**

**First 3 seconds.**
- **First image:** Purple walking on a sunny beach boardwalk with his wife (glasses, pink top).
- **First line:** "I was walking with my wife when a guy said…"
- **On-screen text:** "I was walking".
- **Why you'd stop:** the hostile stranger shows up at about 0:06: a hugely muscular, shirtless guy with a smug face. That contrast in proportions grabs attention by itself.

**Beats.**
- **0:00–0:05:** a stranger tells the wife to leave Purple for him.
- **0:06–0:10:** he insults Purple ("ugly loser").
- **The guess moment (0:16–0:25):** Purple asks him the classic race riddle (you pass the person in 2nd place, so what place are you in?). The camera cuts to a running-track cutaway that acts the riddle out. The muscle guy confidently answers "first," which is wrong. **The viewer gets about 4 seconds to solve it before the reveal.**
- **Escalation:** the wife laughs at him, and he flexes and threatens.
- **Twist (about 0:49–0:51):** a hard cut to Purple calmly drawing a handgun, with "Get up!" The video ends on that image, which is a shock-joke ending.
- **Loop:** no. It's an abrupt cut-off ending.

**Characters.**
- **The muscle guy:** a giant torso, tiny head, messy black hair and a smug half-lidded face. The proportions are exaggerated far past Roblox norms.
- **The wife:** glasses, a pink headband, a pink top.
- **Purple:** keeps a deadpan face.

**Animation and camera.** Smooth. The riddle cutaway (a different location) works like a visual footnote. There are punch-ins on reactions, and the ending is a sudden tonal hard cut.

**Captions.** The standard style.

**Comments.**
- **Pinned:** none.
- **Themes:** huge engagement on the riddle. The top comments mock the "first place" answer, and many explain the correct answer (second place).
- People say they tried the riddle at school and others got it wrong too, so it starts real-world arguments.
- Others react to the shock ending and joke about plot twists.
- This is the clearest example of **comment-bait through a puzzle.**

## 6. My Best Friend Will Forget Me In 3 Days ⏳

- **The badger mascot:** a big brown fur suit with buck teeth.

**Animation and camera.**
- Cinematic lighting (blue night bedroom, warm porch lamps, an evening street).

**Captions.** The same font. 2–3 words each, **all lowercase**, with one yellow keyword ("by **one** on", "a giant **badger**").

**Comments.**

## Summary: 10 things they do differently from a generic Roblox story Short

1. **A permanent, readable mascot.** Purple has the same look in every Short (yellow head, purple bucket hat, black-and-purple stripes), and even props like his car are purple. You know the channel from one frame, even with the sound off.
2. **A recurring self-introduction.** "I'm Purple, I'm 12, and…" turns each video into the next episode of one character's life. Commenters quote it as a catchphrase.
3. **The rule comes before the plot.** New-format Shorts spend the first 15–25s on a one-sentence magic rule, then 3–5 quick examples that move from funny to scary (lottery winners need oxygen masks, then a house that sank and is gone forever). The examples entertain and set the stakes at the same time.
4. **A literal clock at the climax.** Almost every story ends in a race against a stated time (11:52, then 11:58, then 11:59; or 4:58, then 4:59) with numbers spoken aloud.
5. **Sound effects written into the script.** "Tick," "Ding," "Creak" and "Boom" are said or captioned, not only heard. That turns sounds into running gags people quote in comments.
6. **Cinematic rendering of Roblox avatars.** Real lighting, golden hours, night blues, particle snow and motion-blurred explosions, with a cut about every 1.5–2s, much faster than most Roblox story channels.
7. **The rule shown as UI.** Glowing number cards over palms, glowing faces on objects, heart badges with instrument icons. A game-HUD look makes abstract rules readable without extra narration.
8. **The setup comes back as the solution.** A detail planted early (a bad joke, the mascot costume, the neighbors' insult, Morse code) turns out to save the day. Viewers feel smart for noticing.
9. **One villain to beat in roast Shorts, and family love in story Shorts.** Each format has one clear emotional engine: righteous payback or a warm reunion. They don't mix the two in one video.
10. **Comment-bait built into the story, not asked for.** Riddles the viewer can solve, choices never answered, small logic gaps and catchphrases make people comment without any "comment below" line.

## 5 techniques most worth borrowing

1. **The rule in sentence one, plus a picture by second 2.** Open with "In this world, [one strange rule]…" and show a picture of the rule within 2 seconds (a number, a glow, a meter). Put the stakes in that first sentence too ("…and ours was sinking").
2. **Funny-to-scary example ladder.** After the rule, give 3–5 quick examples of how it works, about 3 seconds each. Go from silly to unsettling to "this is why everyone is careful." That builds the world and the stakes in about 20 seconds.
3. **Planted callback + stated countdown.** Plant one specific, slightly silly detail early, and make it the key to the climax. Pair it with clock times said aloud that tighten the pace (11:52 → 11:58 → 11:59).
4. **A recognizable, consistent lead and a signature intro line.** Use one avatar that never changes outfit, in one signature color, plus a short repeated self-intro. Keep side characters to one strong feature each (hair color, glasses, mustache, giant muscles) so they read on a phone at a glance.
5. **Captions and sound as branding.** Use 2–3 word captions in a heavy rounded italic sans with a dark shadow, about 60% of the way down the frame. Put one yellow emphasis word per caption, and use lowercase for warm stories. Say and caption your sound effects ("Tick.") so they become running jokes.

*A note on what not to borrow:* the old format's biggest hits lean on appearance insults and one ends on a gun gag. Those comment sections get argumentative, and on the newer videos commenters split between nostalgia and complaints. The newer warm-story structure carries over more cleanly to original characters.
