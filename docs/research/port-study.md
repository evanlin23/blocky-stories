# Port study: which formats outside the blocky niche to bring into our style (2026-11-03)

The owner, 2026-11-03: "We don't necessarily need to stay in roblox either, we can find some other high performing
niches and try to port to the blocky style? i dont know lowkey".

**Data.** The first wide scan (`docs/trends/2026-11-03.md` → "Outside the niche"; `rows_wide` in the `.json`): 12
query formats, 292 Shorts from 211 channels, published Oct 20 to Nov 3, each with its channel's age and the
uploader's made-for-kids setting. Read alongside the owner's browser study of 66 blocky channels in 10 niches
(`2026-11-02-niche-map.md`) and the blocky scan (median 157K views/day across 194 Shorts).

**How far to trust it.** One scan, about 25 Shorts per format, from search sorted by views, so it shows winners.
22 of the 292 titles aren't in English, and 50 lean on someone else's characters or game (anime, Minecraft,
Roblox), which we can't use. Made-for-kids is the uploader's own setting and almost always "no", so kid risk below is
judged from the titles and themes. Confidence is **low-medium** throughout. Re-run every Monday, and judge after four
scans.

What the table says:
- **What-if is the one open door.** Five channels under 90 days old have a what-if Short at 100K+/day. One is 13
  days old and holds 3 of the 25 fastest Shorts in the whole wide scan (535K to 938K/day). The niche map found the
  same inside blocky: what-if power/price has its best median (377K), with 2 of 3 new channels doing well.
  Confidence: medium.
- **Three of those new channels use the same shape: one thing doubles or worsens every second** (an ice cream that
  gets colder every 2 s, a snail twice as fast every second, a burp twice as loud as the last). That's a rule plus a
  meter that never leaves the screen, which is the reset's "rule meter" exactly. Confidence: medium (3 channels).
- **Our lane is confirmed outside blocky too.** The fastest new storytime channel (9 days old) runs creepy small-town
  rules with conditional warning titles ("If you ... you ..."). Horror animation's fastest is a warning ("Don't
  Open!"). Both back the opening recipe. Confidence: medium.
- **Real-world science what-ifs are educational** (tungsten rods, oxygen vanishing, mosquitoes), and the owner ruled
  out educational content. **We port the shape, not the science:** an everyday thing that escalates, a creepy or
  funny turn, and fiction only.

## The top 3 to port, with premise sketches
Each sketch follows the opening recipe (`playbook.md`) and hard rules 1 to 5: our own premises, no channel's
characters or titles, no real places, no children in peril, and nothing aimed at kids. They vary in title shape on
purpose (the panel lesson).

### 1. What-if escalation (60 to 120 s)
The shape: a "what if" about one everyday thing, an on-screen meter that climbs every few seconds, three or four
jumps that each change daily life, then a creepy or funny turn the escalation's own logic makes inevitable. One
narrator, one main set, which is cheap to build.
- **"What If Every Step Got Louder? 👣"**. Line 1: "Every step you take is louder than the last one." Meter: a decibel
  counter. By the 1,000th step the whole street hears you coming, and then you hear a second set of steps keeping
  time with yours.
- **"What If Saying Yes Made You Quieter? 🤐"**. Line 1: "Every time you agree to something, your voice gets a little
  quieter." Meter: a volume bar. A people-pleaser at work fades to a whisper, and the one word that brings it back is
  no. A warm sting, and a relatable want.
- **"What if your fridge got one degree colder every hour"**. Line 1: "Don't open the fridge after midnight. It's
  been getting colder all week." Meter: a fridge thermometer. Frost creeps out over the kitchen, and something has
  been staying fresh in there.

### 2. The town rulebook: a recurring host (100 to 150 s, one Short per rule)
The niche map: viewers ask for sequels and a recurring host, and "almost no rule channel" gives them one. The wide
scan's fastest new storytime channel posts a different small-town rule each time. Our host: **the Keeper of the
Rulebook**, an adult night clerk at the town hall who stamps the next rule into a huge book each night. They're
always the narrator and always the same blocky character. Each Short is one rule, stands alone, and ends on a torn
page that sets up the next. Recurring cast, our own (the playbook's "build things that are ours").
- **Pilot: "Rule One: Never Read Ahead 📕"**. Line 1: "I keep this town's rulebook, and rule one is you never read
  ahead." Tonight a page writes itself, and it has my name on it.
- **"Rule Two: Don't Feed the Streetlights 💡"**. Line 1: "In this town, you don't feed the streetlights. They lean
  toward anything you're eating." The Keeper finds out what they're really hungry for.
- **"Rule three was torn out"**. Line 1: "Rule three is missing, and everyone in town still obeys it." The Keeper
  watches the town act out a rule nobody can read.

### 3. Horror puzzle: which one is wrong? (20 to 45 s)
The niche map: "Which one is not human?" got 1M with few channels doing it, and it's a gap. The wide scan: riddle
animation has the most new channels of any format (16), and its top end reaches 178K/day. It ports cheaply: one
lit set, a line-up of blocky people, a three-second hold, then the reveal. The viewer comments a letter, which is the
playbook's proven comment engine (typing something specific).
- **"One Of These Neighbors Isn't Home 🏠"**. Line 1: "Don't wave back at the one who isn't real." Five neighbors wave
  at a barbecue, and one shadow points the wrong way. "Comment the letter."
- **"Which barista came back wrong ☕"**. Line 1: "After the power cut, one of the baristas came back different."
  Three identical baristas: one pours left-handed, one blinks, one doesn't. The reveal loops into line 1.
- **"One guest wasn't invited 🎂"**. Line 1: "Never count the guests at this party." Six adults at a birthday table,
  and the window reflects five.
