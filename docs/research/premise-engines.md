# Premise engines: proven movie-level ideas for rule-town stories (2026-11-03)

**Why this exists.** The owner, 2026-11-03: "like i want it to be cool enough that people are like 'christopher nolan could
never' ... all of htem are like the premise of what a movie could be", "maybe we can do that? use similar premises to
movies that have already been tried and true?", "Or any other media". RULES.md → "The lore is the point" already asks
for "movie level performance, like Christopher Nolan could never" (owner, 2026-11-02).

**The guardrail (it never bends).** We borrow the **engine**, never the work. An engine is the mechanism that makes a
premise bend your mind: a loop, a reversed life, a cover story, a price someone else pays. A work is a specific film's,
show's, book's or game's plot, title, character, famous scene, line, monster or setting. Works are named **only in
section 1**, to show where an engine is proven. Nothing outside section 1 may name or echo one, and no pitch takes a
plot. Every pitch has our own rule, place, cast and reveal, and is checked against hard rules 1 to 8, the live hits in
`docs/trends/` and `docs/trends/deep/`, `stories/ideas.md` (Made and Claimed big ideas), every `stories/*/story.json`
(ep80 to ep84 included), `stories/premise-bank.md` and the 2026-11-09 brief's N01 to N25.

**Two of the owner's own example twists are off limits for good** (RULES → The lore is the point): a door that's a
one-way gateway to the past, and a town that turns out to be a coma dream. Engines 26 and 27 below are there so the
writer recognizes them and steers away.

## 1. The engines (30)

"Reach" is a rough feel for how widely loved the examples are: **huge** (household name, hundreds of millions of
viewers or readers), **big** (a well-known hit or classic), **cult** (a devoted following). "Short?" says whether it
lands in 60 to 150 s.

**What the table says, in one line:** the engines that land in a Short have **one rule, one flip, and one picture that
proves it** (2, 10, 14, 18). The ones that don't are either famous enough that the viewer finishes the sentence first
(17, 24), or warm with no reframe (21, 22, 25).

planted all along, one reveal that explains every one of them at once, and a feeling at the end. That's RULES' "the rule
is the clue", so this brief doesn't change the shape. It feeds the shape better engines.

## 3. The pitches (25)

Every pitch: fiction, no children in the cast (siblings and friends are teens or adults, and "when I was born" means a
grown narrator), nothing of Roblox's, nothing aimed at one group, no gore or harm words, US English. No two share a
setting, and none uses a made setting or creature (`stories/ideas.md` → Made) or a live hit's premise. Titles are 3 to
7 words, exactly one CAPS word, one emoji at the end, no question. **Openings:** ban 3, if 4, must 4, count 4, confess
4, cost 4, whatif 2 (ban 12%, nothing over 16%). Only one needs a creature (E12).

**Fixes after the panels** (the panels saw the earlier wording, shown in brackets):
- **E15's line 1** read as body horror to panel A ("I traded my ear for music"). It's now "I traded my love of music at
  the swap shop." Hard rule 2.
- **E23's second plant** contradicted its rule. It's now: nobody in town laughs at his stories, they only nod.
- **E06 (ring toss)** was "muddled" to panel B: if nobody wins, how is he losing? Line 2 must show him *playing* his own
  booth every morning and missing.

## 5. How to use it (for the writer agent)

1. **Pick from the top 10 first**, one engine per batch slot. Never put two pitches from one "never pair" line together,
   and spread the openings as `opening-variety.md` says.
2. **To make a new pitch from an engine**, start from section 1's one-line mechanism, never from a work. Then choose,
   in this order:
   - the **rule** a town would live by: one sentence, with a visible meter
   - the **one flip** that turns that rule over
   - the **one picture** that proves the flip (a closet of bears, a watch on the wrong wrist)
   - **two plants** you can show in the first half
   - a **loved one** the flip changes something for.
   Only then pick a fresh setting from outside the Made table.
3. **The guardrail.** If a viewer could name the film from the pitch, it's too close: change the rule, the place, the
   cast and the reveal until they can't. Nothing outside section 1 may hold a franchise name, title, character, line,
   scene, creature or setting. Never use engines 26 to 28 (the coma dream, the door to the past, time dilation).
4. **The clarity test (before scripting).** Tell the reveal to someone cold in two sentences. If they need a third,
   or ask "wait, so who...?", simplify. ep20 was dropped for confusing people. "Nolan-level" means a cool idea said
   plainly, never a puzzle. The reveal is spoken in one or two lines over one picture in the last 20 to 30 s (RULES).
5. **The panel test.** Score 6 or more on both questions before writing, and redo the blind panel with each new batch
   of pitches.
6. **Log it.** When a pitch is made, mark it here with its story slug, add its reveal to `stories/ideas.md` → Claimed
   big ideas, and log its day-7 numbers next to its panel scores.
