# What ep11's numbers say (2026-11-02)

Source: `2026-11-02-our-channel-numbers.md` (YouTube Studio, read when "The driver on my night bus counted 6. I counted 5." was about 1 day 21 hours old). Early numbers from a single video, so these are leads.

## The hook and the length work
- **82.6% stayed to watch and 17.4% swiped away.** The first frames and line hold people.
- **Average view 1:55 of 2:18, about 84%**, and 67% are still watching at the end. Retention after the first 10 s is flat. That backs the owner's 2:15 to 2:30 length: length isn't what limits this video.

## Reach is what's limited
- **24.9K views and 15.2K engaged, 95% from the Shorts feed, 100% non-subscribers**, so the feed is testing it on new people. Likes run 2.3% of views, in line with the YouTube hits we studied (1.5 to 3.5%).
- **Shares: 3. Comments: 7.** These are the thin signals. The research says shares and comments push a Short beyond its first test audiences. What the hits do that we didn't:
  - The creator posts a question as the first comment (8 of 15 YouTube hits).
  - A rule that makes viewers argue loopholes ("just leave the town") or answer about themselves ("what would your number be?").
  - Both are now in RULES.md (What the hits do; Packaging → Pin).

## The one dip: 0:18 to 0:26 (about -6 points)
That stretch is L12 to L15. The woman in the green coat warns "don't look at it too long" and explains she's ridden for years, then a dent in a seat. It's a knowing elder explaining, plus description with no escalation, which is exactly the batch-1 pattern RULES.md later banned. In the hits, that window holds the 3 to 5 example montage that shows the rule shaping daily life. **Lesson:** seconds 15 to 30 must escalate or show the rule at work, never explain it.

## Who watches
- **58.5% of watch time is on TVs**, so captions and faces must read on a big screen as well as a phone. Ours do.
- **Ages:** 35 to 44 at 34%, then 13 to 17 and 25 to 34 at 22% each.
- **US:** 62% of views. Keep the not-made-for-kids setting and the mature-theme limits as they are.
