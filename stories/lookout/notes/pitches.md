# ep14-lookout: three takes on row 3 (scriptwriter, 2026-10-31)

Story 3 of 4 in blocky batch 2, for PandaNanoAdventures (note 21). Fiction, about 2:15 to 2:30 each. All three keep row 3's tower, its giant, a question for the first line and "I help it". Only the help changes. In A I walk it out. In B I light the road and stay behind. In C I switch my lamp off and let it go.

Cleared against:
- **Batch 1's template, from the README's Lessons.** The opener isn't "At the PLACE, rule". There's no knowing elder: the ranger is never on screen and only says "copy", "say again", "all clear" and the burn line. I'm the lookout "for the autumn", never "one autumn" (ep12 had "one SUMMER"). The creature isn't pale grey. There's no "pressed FLAT", no wet click and no "not X, it was Y". Nothing is done back, nothing returns on a later night, and nothing was lonely or kind all along.
- **The batch skeleton.** The closing section doesn't open on a time stamp, so the pitch's "Next SPRING," goes into the picture. No age on screen, no "it didn't touch me", no imperative title, and no gesture answered in kind: it leaves without looking back.
- **Batch 1 echoes inside row 3.**
  - The forest going quiet is ep12's crickets cutting out before each knock. Only B keeps that first line, and the quiet happens just once.
  - "One ridge closer each time" is ep11's row-closer and story 1's door-closer. No take counts it in. It gets nearer by sense: seen, heard, then felt.
  - The footprints have toes and are read for direction, unlike ep12's smooth trail. Nothing taps, knocks or fogs glass.
- **Titles.** The board's title starts "Something taller...", which is ep12's shape. None of mine starts with "Something" or with ep13's "Why".
- **Batch 2 lanes.** No lift, button or numbered door, and the ranger and I never meet (story 1). No mirrors, window reflections, copied voices or inside jokes, and nobody goes back for anyone (story 2). No boxes and no Mum, and nobody says "home". It walks to "the valley" (story 4).
- **`checks.py`.** I avoid every GRAPHIC word, traps included: "dead still", "the wind died", "stable", "shooting star". "Lost" and "fall" are HARM words, so the script has no child word anywhere.
- **The creature** is never named on screen, because "Walker" reads as a zombie word. It's stacked logs with stone shoulders, never a tree with a face.

## A. "It walked out of the fog toward my fire tower"

**First line:** "How do you / LOSE / something TALLER / than the TREES?"

**Set-up:** I'm alone in a glass cab 112 stairs above the pines, with a lamp and a radio. On day one the ranger says, "Controlled burn, east side, all week." That's plant 1, at about 8 s, shown as a grey smudge far off.

**Strange beats**
1. Through binoculars, a line of snapped treetops on the far ridge runs straight for the valley, then bends toward my tower.
2. At 3 a.m. there's slow breathing under the radio static, each breath ending in a creak like a trunk in wind. The ranger says, "Say again?"
3. At the tower's foot there are prints big enough to lie down in, full of rainwater, moss in the toes. Every one points downhill, toward the valley (plant 2).
4. At dusk the pines below lean apart in a moving line as something wide walks through them.
5. Two small warm lights hang in the fog, level with my window.

**The scare:** on the catwalk in thick fog, the tower groans and leans under something's weight. My mug slides off the rail, and I never hear it land. The two lights rise out of the fog an arm's length away, deep in a hollow of bark. The sound drops out. Then the radio says, "Lookout, report." I say, "All clear."

**The twist, shown:** I carry the lamp to the far window, and the lights follow it there. Both plants land. Smoke fills the valley, and in all that grey my lamp is the only light that keeps still. It's lost.

**The warm turn, in pictures:** I carry the lamp down and along the fire road, held high. A low wide shows my small lamp, with the giant stepping after it, fog to its waist. Close on my boots, its foot sets down slowly a stride behind. When I stop, it stops. At the valley's edge the fog thins under the moon. It steps past me and walks down until I can't tell it from the trees.

**Last line:** "There's a TREE / in every FOOTPRINT / on the fire ROAD. / All the WAY / to my DOOR." Spring is in the picture only. A hard cut takes it back to the question, which now has its answer: among the trees.

**Why it's clear:** no batch 1 story ended on a goodbye that leaves a mark. It's seen moving, which ep11's creature, the Attendant and the Boxes never are. The warmth is my walk.

## B. "The broken trees on the ridge bent toward my tower"

**First line:** the pitch's own, "Have you EVER / heard a FOREST / go QUIET / all at ONCE?"

**Set-up:** the lookout's gear carries it: the round map table, the haul bucket, and a stool with glass feet for lightning. Then the burn line (plant 1).

**Strange beats**
1. A grey column moves along the far ridge against the wind. I log its bearing. The ranger says, "Copy."
2. Each dawn I pencil the snapped treetops onto the map. The line runs straight for the valley, then bends toward me (plant 2).
3. The water bucket comes up the rope packed with wet moss.
4. At night the stool's glass feet ring with no storm, a chime on every step.

**The scare:** the cab tilts, and three windows go brown at once, bark and moss right up to the glass. In the fourth, two small warm lights look past me at the lamp.

**The twist:** smoke sits in the valley like milk, and my pencil line says the rest. My lamp bent it off course.

**The warm turn, in pictures:** I hang every light I own at the bends of the fire road, one flashlight tied to a gatepost with my bootlace. At dusk I switch the tower lamp off and watch from the dark cab. A dotted line of lights runs down the mountain. Its two lights pause at each one and go on, and at the last one the fog parts over the valley.

**Last line:** "There's a TREE / at every BEND / of the fire ROAD. / SEVEN of them." It stood by each light, and the bucket showed it sheds moss.

**Why it's clear:** I plan the help and I'm not there for it. The map, bucket and stool are the lookout's own, and seven doesn't echo ep11's five and six. **Weak spot:** the warm turn is seen far off through binoculars, so on a phone its size and its warmth both shrink.

## C. "I turned off the only light on the mountain"

**First line:** "Would you / switch OFF / the only LIGHT / for MILES?"

**Set-up:** on clear nights the river in the valley shines like a strip of foil (plant 3). Then comes the burn line, and smoke fills the valley until the river's gone (plant 1).

**Strange beats**
1. The tower sways at night, but the wind cups on the roof don't turn.
2. In the morning there's a long smear of moss on the catwalk rail.
3. The prints round the tower's feet all face the valley (plant 2).
4. From my cot, two small warm lights in the window until dawn. They stay on the lamp.

**The scare:** halfway down the stairs at dusk, the fog comes up fast. A wall of bark and stone rises past the landing, and its face comes level with mine, an arm's length off the rail. Its lights go right past me, up to the lamp.

**The twist:** it finds its way by the river. With the river hidden, my lamp was the brightest thing left.

**The warm turn, in pictures:** the ranger says, "Burn's out." The river shows again, faint, but it's still facing my lamp. My hand goes to the switch, and the cab goes dark except for its two lights. They turn toward the silver river, and it walks down the ridge.

**Last line:** "The ranger said, / 'Lookout, your LAMP / was OUT.' / I said, / 'COPY.'" A dry human beat, so the creature doesn't get ep13's last word.

**Why it's clear:** the light going out is the warm act, never ep11's or story 1's blackout scare. **Weak spot:** the warm turn is a dark frame of something leaving, so it may read as sad.

---

## My pick: A

A is the pitch with three fixes. The riddle hides the twist in the first line, since "lose" reads two ways until "It's lost". The ending opens on a picture, not a time stamp. And it gets nearer by sense, not by counting.

It's the only take whose warm turn is a two-shot: a small lamp on a road, with something huge stepping carefully behind it. That reads at phone size and needs no words. It's also the only ending that pays off a plant on screen: the footprints that pointed away from me grow a line of trees back to my door. B and C play their warm turns far away or in the dark. The pitch's first line drops into A unchanged if you want it, but ep12's crickets come with it.

**For the build:** the lamp hangs on a bail from a block hand, because R6 hands can't grip. The test still should be the catwalk reveal plus the road wide, the scale shot.

# Redo 2 (writer, 2026-11-02): ten big ideas for the same tower, one kept

Each had to be rule first, use the tower, fog, radio and lamp, avoid `stand-in`, and pass the one-sentence test.

1. **Kept. Time runs slower the higher up you are.** Four nights in the lookout are twenty years below, and the stranger on my stairs is my brother, now older than me, who moved higher every year to slow himself down.
2. The fog takes whatever nobody is watching. The lookout watches the valley, and the reveal is that Dad has stayed up every night watching my lamp. Warm, but no floor drops.
3. The forest moves at night, so the lookout radios the new paths. Reveal: the forest is still, and the tower walks. Big, but it needs the whole town on legs and the loved one has no stake.
4. Stand still in the fog too long and you turn into a pine. The giant is Dad. Too close to harm, and it ends sad with no turn.
5. Every lamp you light puts out a star. Pretty, but the reveal (my lamp is my sister's star) is a fact, not a feeling.
6. Every radio in the valley plays one day ahead. Too close to a well-known film, and to the owner's banned door-to-the-past shape.
7. A lit lamp brings home whoever you're waiting for. Every version of the reveal came back to a Keeper keeping a place.
8. The fog shows the shape of whoever is thinking of you. Too close to ep38's "who you miss" silhouettes.
9. Nobody remembers the nights. The nightly visitor is Dad. Confusing: why would I fear what I wrote down?
10. The fog rises a little every year, and the valley is a giant standing up. Awe, but nobody to love.
