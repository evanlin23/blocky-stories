# lookout r5 standards (redo round 1: new premise, script and package)

Verdict: approve

Checked script.md, story.json, package.json and the preview (quarter-res, every 4th drawing). I looked at the contact sheet, the stills sheet and one frame from every one of the 71 shots. I also read the premise and twist of every other story.

1. **Fiction:** pass. No real place, route or name. The set has no text or signs. The ranger's badge is a plain disc and bar, and the hourglasses have no numerals. The description says "It's made up."
2. **Kids and mature themes:** pass. Teen Ollie appears only at the gate in daylight (S12 to S18), and he is never in peril. The man in the door is lit to read and never touches anyone. No death words, no ages said or shown, and `made_for_kids` is false.
3. **IP:** pass. The faces, hourglass marker, beanies and plaid are all ours. No game name in the title, description or pin.
4. **Originality:** pass. Preflight shows 0 blocks. The time rule, the `time-gap` shape and the lookout set appear in no other story. There is no Keeper, so no lore overlap.
5. **Flashing:** pass on the preview (1 flash a second at most on the 1-frame reading, 2 on the 3-frame reading, 0 red). Watch these on the Actions measure: the hard cuts from dark cab shots to the binocular town-light POVs (S23, S26, S57, S59), where large white squares fill about a third of the frame, and the loop cut from black S71 to bright noon S01.
6. **Package:** pass. The title is 50 characters with one word in caps, and it doesn't spoil the twist. The description and pin are clean, with no dashes or semicolons. When this approval and the critic's are both in, remove `"hold"` from package.json in the same PR, because RULES.md says the hold comes off when a redo passes review.

Nice-to-haves:
- The premise says "a one-week shift alone". Saying "a week as the lookout" would keep it clear of the batch-1 night-shift narrator.
- The warmth is close to ep37's (a loved one keeping a nightly or yearly ritual for years). That's fine across batches, but don't put the two in the same week.
