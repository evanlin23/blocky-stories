# ep39-glass-house

**Slug:** `ep39-glass-house` (pitch #23 in `stories/ideas.md` → Batch 4 pitches)

## Premise
In this town everyone has a small white cloud over their head, and it rains when they're sad. My cloud (I'm an adult who lives with Mum) is the biggest and whitest in town, and it has never rained once, so everyone calls me the happy one. Since Mum's sister Lou moved across the sea in spring, Mum's cloud has gone dark, low and heavy, and it never rains. She sleeps in the glasshouse at the end of the garden in a wicker chair among Lou's drying plants, her cloud lower every morning. Each night a head on a long neck of stacked blocks is nearer the glasshouse.

## Rule
The world's rule, line one (L01): "In this town, everyone has their own little CLOUD, and it rains when they're SAD." (15 words.)
- **Marker by 2 s:** a small soft white cloud floating a hand above every head, on every character in S01, with Mr Pell's cloud drizzling. Lit by midday sun, faces in frame.
- **Montage, first 15 s (4 examples):** Mr Pell's twenty-year drizzle and his greenest lawn (L02 to L03), a break-up at the café in raincoats (L04), the front row at a sad film under umbrellas (L05), a wedding with the whole street freckled in sun (L06).
- **The narrator is the exception (L07 to L10):** my cloud is the biggest, whitest one in town, and it has never rained.
- **Loved one at stake:** Mum (adult, fifties). Her cloud sinks from above her head to over her face, and the Long Neck comes nearer her each night.
- **Seconds 15 to 30, the rule at work, nobody explains it (L09 to L16):** Mum's dark cloud that won't rain, the wicker chair among Lou's plants, the plants going brown. Then L17 to L21 show it sinking day by day. Mum says "Never BETTER" and the cloud answers by sinking. Nobody says why.
- **The loophole the climax turns (L45 to L53), a second reading of the rule's own logic, no new magic:** a cloud rains when you're sad, so rain is the only way the sad gets out. A cloud held in just gets heavier and lower. Mum held hers in for me, because I'm "the happy one", and I held mine in for her. When I say I miss Lou and that I've never cried, my cloud rains, and Mum's lets go a second later.

## Twist
The rain was never the bad thing. "It rains when they're SAD" opens as a sad town's curse and my dry cloud as luck. In the last 15 seconds it turns over: rain is how sadness gets out, the biggest cloud in town was the fullest one, and a cloud that never rains is the broken one. The last line, "Mine WORKS.", makes the first line a promise.
- **Plant 1 (L10, S10):** my umbrella still in its shop wrapper, hanging on a hook among the neighbours' worn, patched umbrellas. Paid off at L57.
- **Plant 2 (L02 to L03, S02 and S03):** the lawn under Mr Pell's twenty years of drizzle is the greenest on the street. Paid off at L45 and L61.
- Bonus plants: my cloud is the "biggest" (L07, S07: visibly twice the size of anyone's, and a touch grey on its underside, never commented on). Mum's "You're my HAPPY one" (L30) is why she holds hers in, which L48 pays off.

## World
One small town street in a dry late summer, and our back garden. Front: brick terraced houses with low painted gates (sage, cream, plum), a cracked pavement, yellowing verges, and one bright green lawn next door. Back: a long dry garden with straw-coloured grass and a stone path down to a Victorian-style glasshouse at the end: white-painted wooden frame, small panes (a few cloudy with age), a pitched roof with a hinged roof vent on a notched iron arm, a door with old iron strap hinges. Inside: two slatted wooden benches facing each other down the length, Mum's on the left and Lou's on the right, terracotta pots crowded on both, Lou's plants (big-leafed, ferny, a tomato vine) drying brown and curling. A potting table with a zinc tray at the back, a cane wicker chair by Lou's bench, two hanging oil-style lamps (electric, warm) on hooks. A tall privet hedge at the far end of the garden. Mr Pell's porch lamp next door.
- Montage sets, one shot each: a café front with a striped awning (no lettering), a small cinema's front row from the screen side (screen shows only colour), a wedding on a street of bunting in plain colours.
- No words anywhere: no shop names, signs, labels, umbrella brands or text on the wrapper (plain clear plastic with a paper band in plain blue).

## The creature: the Long Neck (never named on screen)

The blocks are posed per shot as a straight column or a stepped bend (each block offset a little from the last, like a stack of flower pots leaning), never a smooth tube or a thin rod.

- It carries no cloud (a Keeper never carries the town's marker). Never seen moving, only nearer. It never touches anyone. Lit from below (glasshouse lamps or Mr Pell's porch lamp from the side), a cool moonlight rim on the neck, front lights dead.
- Test still first: S36 at phone size, the head hanging through the roof vent beside Lou's bench, Mum asleep under her cloud in frame. Iterate till it's eerie, never silly (the bend must read as wrong and heavy, not as a snake or a toy).

## The last 15 seconds turn the first line
L01 opens with rain as the mark of sadness, and my dry cloud reads as the luckiest thing in town. L46 to L53 turn it: rain is the way sadness gets out, and a cloud that never rains is a cloud that's full. L59 to L63 show what rain does (Lou's plants standing up, Mr Pell's lawn), and the last line, "Mine WORKS.", makes the first line a good thing. A second watch sees the wrapped umbrella (L10, S10) and the green lawn (L02 to L03, S03) as the answer, given away at the start.
