## The story

- **Premise:** In this town, everyone glows a little brighter for every secret they keep. I'm the one person with no glow at all, because I tell everything. Grandad Abe guides at the museum on Friday late openings and has always been the brightest man in town. This spring the curator starts asking about a mended bone in the big skeleton's tail, and every week he gets brighter, until people cross the road and the museum moves him to the dark galleries. A line of dust wiped clean at knee height runs down the cases, and the skeleton's head turns a little further toward our old bench each week.
- **Rule:** you glow a little brighter for every secret you keep. Telling someone passes the glow to them (shown, never explained: the whisper in the montage).
- **Twist:** the secret Grandad has been keeping for nine years is mine. I knocked down the skeleton's tail my first summer helping out, I told him, and he told everyone it was him. Every time somebody asks about that tail, he keeps it again. I never glowed because he was keeping mine. The first line turns over: the brightest man in town had only one secret, and it was somebody else's.
- **Where the twist is planted:**
  - Plant 1, the mended tail bone (L14): one bone in the tail is a paler colour, mended. The curator asks about it (L15), and Grandad brightens each week (L16). The last turn (L75) names it.
  - Plant 2, the whisper (L04): a man whispers to a friend and the glow jumps across to her. It sets up the loophole (L59 to L61) and, on a rewatch, tells you how my secret became his.
  - Plant 3, a line said straight (L09): "I tell Grandad EVERYTHING." First time it's why I don't glow. Second time it's how he got my secret.
  - Rewatch reward, not narrated: in L12 to L15 a visitor points at the tail and Grandad's glow swells a little (slow 1 s ramp) while he smiles and looks away.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** L75 to L80. L75 says every question about the tail made him keep it again. L76 repeats the first line, and L77 ("Grandad kept MINE.") makes it mean something new: the brightest man in town was the most loyal, keeping one secret for someone else. A second watch plays L01 to L20 as Grandad carrying me.
- **Ending shape:** a short bench for two in a museum hall, the two of us shoulder to shoulder with two faint ordinary glows, and then a cold low hold on my seat, from knee height where its head hung, as the last word lands.
- **Warmth:** clearing his name. Shown: I say it to the whole crowd, the curator shakes his hand in front of the tail, people stop crossing the road, and we sit on our bench again.
- **The cold sting (L78 to L80):** now I glow a little, because I keep one secret of my own: what stood over my seat. It hints the Keeper would stand there again if I stopped coming, never that it stayed.

## World

A town museum, its fossil hall on a Friday late opening, plus its front steps in the sun, a few montage interiors and the basement store.

- **The steps (L01 only):** pale sandstone steps and two plain columns, a bright blue sky, visitors going up and down. No words or banners anywhere. The museum's sign is a carved relief of a curling fern fossil.
- **The fossil hall:** a long high hall of cream stone, a coffered ceiling, a chequered floor of worn cream and grey marble tiles. Down the middle stands the big skeleton, a long-necked, long-tailed blocky giant of ochre bone blocks on an iron frame, its head high over the hall. One block near the tip of its tail is a paler grey-cream, a mended piece with a thin seam. In front of it, facing it, is one short wooden bench with two iron legs, built for two and only just wide enough for two seated people (about 1.4 m of seat), so whoever sits on it sits shoulder to shoulder. Glass display cases on dark wood stands line both walls, full of blocky shells, ferns and teeth on felt.
- **The dark galleries:** side rooms off the hall, the same cases, the ceiling lamps off, only a weak blue light from high windows. Dust lies on the glass.
- **The basement store:** a low concrete room of crates and shelving, plaster casts under dust sheets, one bare bulb that stays off.
- **Montage sets (one shot each):** a living room with a sofa, a front doorstep at dusk, a small restaurant table with candles, a park bench in the sun.
- **No text anywhere:** cases have no labels (small blank brass plates), crates no stencils, the curator's badge a fern glyph.
- **What's true underneath (never said):** the bench was built for two. When I stopped coming in spring, Grandad kept sitting in his seat and mine went empty, so the hall's Keeper, the Stooped, came to stand over my seat and make the count. The wiped dust line is its hanging head passing along the cases. It has no glow, because a Keeper carries none of its town's markers. It never touches or takes anyone. Grandad's missing for an ordinary reason. When I sit back down on my seat, it's gone.
