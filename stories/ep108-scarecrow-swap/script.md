## The big idea
**Since June, the Grandpa at our dinner table has been the scarecrow, and the scarecrow in the pumpkin patch has been Grandpa, because the one thing every crow in the county is scared of is him, and Grandma has known all along.**

- **Engine:** the changeling swap, played for laughs. No folk tale's changeling or film's scarecrow: our patch, our pie.
- **Why it clicks:** zero crows since June (L05), the same week Grandpa went quiet (L06), a man who waves at the mail truck stops waving (L07, L08), straw at his sleeve (L09), Grandma's plate "for the crows" every night (L10), and the scarecrow waving back at me (L02).

Dropped: the scarecrow is a Keeper standing in Grandpa's place (stand-in), the scarecrow is alive and wants Grandpa's chair (a monster, too dark), Grandpa is a scarecrow who came to life fifty years ago (muddled), the crows are Grandpa's old friends (no picture), the scarecrow is me sleepwalking (ep86's shape), the scarecrow is Uncle somebody in disguise (ep112's shape, a relative in disguise), the scarecrow walks to the house at night (a creature at the door, ep103's knock), the straw one at dinner is a robot (an object read), Grandma is the scarecrow (two swaps, muddled), the crows are scared of the scarecrow because it's the farm's dog standing up (warm-shoes' tall cat).

## The story
- **Premise:** My grandpa's pumpkin patch is the only field on our road with no crows, not one since June. Since June, Grandpa has also gone very quiet at dinner, and Grandma carries a plate out to the field every night. On Tuesday at dusk, the scarecrow waved back at me.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.0 s):** "Never wave / at my grandpa's SCARECROW. / It waves BACK." L02: "Mine did. / Tuesday, / at DUSK."
- **The relatable want or fight:** a quiet grandpa at dinner, and grandparents who've been married so long they run the farm like a team.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag top-left with a crow glyph and a count of crows in Grandpa's patch, reading 0 all story. Beside it, a tiny wave glyph (a raised hand) that lights each time someone waves (L02, L07, L21).
- **The one beat:** following Grandma's plate out to the scarecrow with a flashlight (L11 to L13).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** zero crows (L05), the same week Grandpa went quiet and stopped waving (L06 to L08), and straw at his sleeve (L09, about 15 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** the one the scarecrow waved back at, and the one who follows the plate.
- **The loved one at stake:** my grandpa Walt, named "my grandpa" in L01 and seen at dinner from behind at L08, and Grandma, named at L10 carrying the plate.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** the warning says it waves back, and Grandpa has waved back at everything his whole life (L07). That's the tell, and it's how I know it's him at L13 before the line says so.
- **Twist and reveal (L12 to L16, about 21 to 31 s):** said plainly (L14: "That's GRANDPA.", L16: "The one at dinner / is the SCARECROW.") over one picture (S14: my flashlight on the scarecrow's face from below, Grandpa's own face under the sack hat with his reading glasses on, his eyes very still, a pumpkin field with zero crows behind him).
- **How the last seconds turn over the first line:** the swap is Grandma's idea as much as his, she likes the straw one at dinner, and Grandpa still waves at everybody, which runs straight into L01 as a warning about him.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L07: Grandpa waves at everybody, even the mail truck (so the waving scarecrow in L02 is him).
  2. L10: Grandma takes a plate out "for the crows" every night (his dinner).
  3. L09: straw at the dinner Grandpa's sleeve.
- **Ending shape:** a swap found out and left in place on purpose, then a loop that turns the warning into Grandpa's habit.
- **Warmth:** an old couple's teamwork and a joke they share, with me now in on it.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. Comedy.
