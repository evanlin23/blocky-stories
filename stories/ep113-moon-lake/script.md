## The big idea
**The moon sleeps at the bottom of Grandma's lake every day, and for fifty years she's rowed out at dusk and pushed it back into the sky with her oar. The swimming rule is there because swimmers wake it, and a moon that wakes goes up, whenever it wakes. So when her arms finally can't, the rule's own reason is how I do it: I swim.**

- **Engine:** the sky run by hand (lore.md allows another wonder run by hand, after ep85's sun, as long as nobody says who started it and no Keeper is in charge). No myth's moon or film's lake.
- **Why it clicks:** her oar scuffed silver down one side (L11), the moon rising in the same spot over her dock every night (L08), her shaking arms at breakfast (L13), the afternoon going dark when I swim (L23), and "Not YET" (L29).
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the glow is a drowned town (a death beat), the glow is a Keeper (a count), the moon is Grandma's (she is the moon, muddled), the lake is the sky upside down (no picture), the glow is the sun (ep85 again), Grandma is a giant (a person made wrong, we have three), the moon is stuck and I must free it (no rule logic), the glow is a fish king (a mascot), the moon is lonely and wants a swimmer (kind all along), the glow is Grandma's late husband's lantern (sad, and the batch's sad slot is ep110).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: ep85-early-alarm (killed, never uploaded) claimed "the sun sleeps in our backyard and Dad winches it up". This one is a different body, place and mechanic: no winch, no one-person loophole, and the twist is that the rule's warning (swimmers wake it) is the way to lift it. Not a stand-in, not the forgotten theme. Shape `inherited-job`.
- **The rule is the clue:** "never after noon" first reads as a safety rule. After L41 it's the one thing that can lift the moon, and the job it hands me.

## The story
- **Premise:** At Grandma's lake, swimming is free all morning and banned after noon, because the moon sleeps on the lake bed all day and swimmers wake it, and a moon that wakes goes up. I help Grandma at the lake every weekend. One hot afternoon I swim anyway, and the sky starts going dark. That night I follow her rowboat out, and watch her push the moon up with her oar.
- **Rule / hook (L01 to L03, about 8 s):** "Nobody swims / Grandma's lake / after NOON. / It costs the town / its AFTERNOON." "After noon, / the water gets WARM. / And something down there GLOWS." "Grandma says / that's the MOON. / It sleeps down there / all DAY."
- **The relatable want or fight:** a grandma getting older who won't say so, and the weekends I spend helping her.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag top-left with a day dial: a sun glyph swinging across a half-circle to a moon glyph, and a noon tick in the middle. Past the tick, the dial's half-circle glows pale blue. At L23 the dial's moon glyph lights early, in the afternoon. At L47 the moon glyph is dark at dusk. At L64 it lights.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L04 to L08, 7 to 19 s):** swimmers climbing out in a line at 11:59, fish gathering in the middle at noon, afternoon coffee made from lake water, the moon rising over Grandma's dock every night.
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** the glow (L02), and the moon always rising over one dock (L08).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** the only one who swims after noon.
- **The loved one at stake:** my grandma Nell, named "Grandma Nell" at L09 in the same shot as her on the dock.
- **First real scare (L17 to L28, about 32 to 51 s):** I swim at three, dive, the glow brightens under my hands, something huge rolls over beneath me, and the sky goes dark in the middle of the afternoon.
- **The big beat (L34 to L38, about 61 to 70 s, about halfway in):** the moon lifting out from under her boat, shown only by what it does: the lake swelling and pouring, a cratered edge sliding past the frame's bottom edge, a disc of light climbing up Grandma's face, and its reflection settling over the dock. Never the whole moon.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** nobody swims after noon because swimmers wake it (L41, L51). So when her oar can't lift it, I swim down and wake it on purpose (L52 to L57). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L30 to L42):** said plainly (L39: "Grandma's been pushing / the moon up / every night / for fifty YEARS.") over one picture (S37: Grandma's tiny red rowboat on a lake lit pale blue from below, the water around it heaving up and pouring off something under it, her oar's silver edge catching a light that is climbing past her into the sky, and only a sliver of pale cratered edge at the bottom of frame).
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** now we go out together, she rows and I swim. The rule's still the rule, never after noon, unless it's your turn. So come on in, any morning, which runs straight into L01.

## The creature (the moon: never shown whole)
**Creature-light (RULES, the 2026-11-04 owner note, item 4; batch 11 orchestrator note):** every full reveal in batches 9 and 10 failed the phone-size creature test, so the moon is never seen whole. The big idea still lands on screen because the light does the telling: the glow under the boat, then a disc of moonlight climbing out of the lake, up Grandma's face and into the sky over the dock.

`docs/research/creature-playbook.md` section 2, applied while writing (to the parts we do show):
