## The story

- **Premise:** In a town where moss grows on you while you wait for someone, I've never had a speck, because I'm always early and never wait for anyone. Jory and I have kept our static caravan on the headland for thirty years. Every evening I walk the headland path and he waits in his chair at the gate. This autumn he's coming up green, the moss won't come off when I get back, and every morning there are two deep knee hollows in the wet grass by the step.
- **Rule / hook:** "In this town, moss grows on you while you wait for someone." Soft moss and tiny white flowers on everyone's shoulders and hats, thicker the longer they've waited. It comes off in a green shower the moment the one you're waiting for arrives.
- **Twist:** I came back every evening, but I never arrived. I put the kettle on and was out again, I never once sat in my chair, and I was always on my way somewhere. Jory wasn't waiting for me to come back. He was waiting for me to stop. My empty chair left the caravan's count of two short, and the Kneeler has been kneeling beside it every night, standing in my place.
- **Where the twist is planted:**
  - Plant 1, Jory's moss is thickest on his **left** shoulder (L17), and L14 has already told us I always walk on his left. It's growing where I should be.
  - Plant 2, the old couple on the bench in the montage (L06, L07): he's green to the elbows while she sits right beside him checking her watch. You can be next to someone and still be waited for.
  - Plant 3, small: "I never SIT in mine. There's always somewhere to BE." (L12), and L21's "then I'm OUT again for MILK".
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** the first time, "moss grows on you while you wait for someone" is about people being late. By L54 we know Jory was waiting for me to stop, not to come back. In the last 15 s I'm the one in the chair, waiting for him and not minding, my own green shoulder in frame. L70 repeats the rule as something I've earned, then L72 bites: an empty chair is a place the Kneeler will kneel again. A second watch sees every evening homecoming as me leaving, and catches the left shoulder and the couple on the bench.
- **Ending shape:** two chairs at the gate at dusk, one green shoulder (mine), Jory coming slowly up the path. Then a cold last shot: my chair empty at dusk, and the wet grass beside it slowly dimpling into two hollows, no figure.
- **Warmth:** patience. I learn to wait for him, and I don't mind how long he takes. Shown, not told: I sit, he dawdles for every gull, I smile.

## World

A static caravan park on a grassy headland in autumn. Short wind-flattened turf, gold-brown bracken and dark gorse. A row of cream static caravans, each with a coloured stripe down its side (muted blue, rust, mustard, and ours lichen green), a three-step metal step unit at each door with a coir step mat, and a little porch lamp. Ours stands at the end of the row. In front of it, a low white picket gate between two chunky granite gateposts, 2.1 m tall, and two folding canvas chairs side by side on the grass inside the gate, facing out to the path: Jory's on the right, mine on the left (his left as he sits). The headland path runs from the gate up through gorse to an old wooden bench at the top, set well back from the edge, with the grey-green sea far below and beyond. The town is down the hill: a bus shelter, a small shop with a bench outside, and a one-track station platform.

- **No text anywhere.** The bus stop sign is a plain round disc with our own glyph (a curl over a bar). The shop sign is a painted kettle. The station has a lamp and a glyph board. The caravans have no numbers.
- **The marker (by frame 1):** soft cushion moss (`#6f8f3e` to `#9fb860`) in lumpy pads on shoulders, hat brims and the tops of heads, dotted with tiny five-petal white flowers (`#f4f2e6`). The longer someone has waited, the thicker the pads and the more flowers. People not waiting have none. When the awaited person arrives, the moss comes off as a short burst of green flakes and white petals that tumbles to the ground over about 0.6 s (particles only, no change in light).
- **What's true underneath (never said):** the caravan was built for two. For years I came and went but never stayed, so the van's count was short by one every evening. The Kneeler is this caravan's Keeper. Each night it knelt in the grass by the step, and then by my empty chair, standing in my gap so the count held. When Jory went up to the bench, it bent toward his chair too. Once I sit down and wait with him, the count is made true by people and it's gone. It carries none of the town's markers, so it's the only thing in the park with no moss. It never touches anyone, never takes anyone, and Jory was only on the bench.
