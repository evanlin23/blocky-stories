## The big idea
**Dryer nine is drying tonight itself, and the night wants out: it starts the dryer by itself every dusk and leaves a roll of quarters on top for whoever can't let tonight end, because every quarter buys ten more minutes of night and too much night won't fit in the drum.**

- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the laundromat is inside a giant's dryer (too close to `closing-time`'s "the hall is the exhibit"), lost socks as the dryers' fee (no turn), clothes that repeat what you said in them (no reveal), a load that holds you in the building until it's done (ghost, sad), dryers that show tomorrow's laundry (mild), a dryer that tumbles its load's owner (gag only), a dryer that dries the sleep out of the town (abstract), a voodoo-shrink dryer (gag only), a dryer drying a soaked man's clothes for forty years (no "wait, what").
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not being forgotten or unseen, not a stand-in, not a count of people, not altitude time. It's the night as a load of laundry. Closest neighbours are ep33 (a day at the top costs a year below) and ep31 (one dawn relived): both are about time for a loved one, but neither treats the night as a thing you can buy more of, see, or overfill. Flagged for the blind premise check.
- **The rule is the clue:** "you never use dryer nine" first reads as a dangerous machine. After the reveal it reads as the one thing everyone in town is tempted by: one more hour of tonight. **L02 turns over too:** "Even if it's already RUNNING" first reads as "even if someone else started it". On a rewatch, nobody did: the Night starts itself (L52) and leaves the quarters out (L16, L53) as bait, so a running nine is the trap already set. The loop line says so: "So no matter how much you want one more HOUR, / at the all-night laundromat, you never use dryer nine."

## The story
- **Premise:** At a bright all-night laundromat, dryer nine runs every night with no load in it, its window is never empty, and its timer counts down to sunrise. It's my big brother Jonah's last night before he moves out at sunrise, and nine says forty-one minutes. I'm not ready, so I feed it a quarter.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 1.7 s):** "At the LAUNDROMAT, / you never use / dryer NINE."
- **The relatable want:** one more hour with someone before they leave. Everyone has had a night they didn't want to end.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and every shot after:** dryer nine itself: a big painted **9** on its door, its round window full of tumbling starry navy, and its timer, a glowing amber `H:MM` readout over the door. Every shot has the timer, the window or the 9 in frame (the reaction shots keep nine's amber glow in the background, soft but readable).
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L07, first 11 s):** a woman swerves her basket wide around nine (L03). A man folds towels on top of dryer eight and leaves nine's top bare (L04). Nine's window is never empty, starry navy always tumbling behind the glass (L05, paid off at L50 when the drum is empty for the first time at dawn). Nine's timer doesn't count down to dry (L06). It counts down to sunrise (L07: the timer's 0:41 beside the sunrise glyph on my phone, also 41 minutes away).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** everyone else lets the night end. I'm the one who can't, tonight, and feeds it.
- **The loved one at stake:** my big brother, Jonah (adult), moving out at sunrise. Named plainly at L08 and shown with his packed truck at L09.
- **The rule broken:** L12, about 16 s in. "So I put a QUARTER / in nine." Then the roll of quarters sitting on top of it (L16), which nobody asks about.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic (the loophole):** nine is still a dryer, and a dryer stops when its door opens. Jonah sees the timer froze at 7:30 the moment the door clicked (L39, L40): out of the drum, the night never runs down. So we shut it (the Night folds back in across a cut, no shove) and spend every minute I bought with our backs against the door (L41 to L43). The danger is what grants the wish: the only way out is to use all seven extra hours with Jonah, together. No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L31 to L37, from about 44 s):** the thing climbing out is made of night, with the moon for a face, and there's no moon in the sky outside. "That's what / dryer nine DRIES. / The NIGHT." Said plainly in two lines and one picture, then left to land.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** dawn comes, nine stops, the drum is empty, Jonah drives off. The next night nine starts on its own (who starts it running, L02), a new roll of quarters sits on top of it (who left the first one, L16), something inside holds a quarter up to the glass, and the narration says it plainly: now it wants YOU to buy more night (so it can get out). The last line, "So no matter how much / you want one more HOUR,", runs straight into L01, so the warning now means "however much you want tonight to last".
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L05, narrated: nine's window is never empty (the night is always in it until dawn), paid off at L50: "For ONCE, the drum was EMPTY."
  2. L14, narrated: the moon slides back up the sky when the timer jumps.
  2b. L16 against L03: nobody sets anything on nine, yet a roll of quarters sits on top of it. It's in S04 too (a small paper roll at the back corner of nine's bare top). Paid off at L53: a new roll is there the next night. The Night left it.
  3. Unnarrated, spot-the-detail: the moon outside the front window turns in step with nine's drum in every shot from L01 to L09 (watch its three craters go round), and the Night's face at L32 has the same three craters. Frame 1's timer already reads 0:41, the number L10 says.
- **Ending shape:** a held door until dawn (two backs on a dryer, sun coming up), an empty drum, a goodbye at a truck, then a cold sting at the glass and a loop.
- **Warmth:** brothers. The last night before he moves out, spent the way I wanted it, talking until sunrise. Shown: the two of us on the floor with our backs to nine, laughing, Jonah's cap on my head by dawn.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. Nobody is gone, hurt or revealed to be anything. Jonah just moves out, hugs me and drives.

## World
One place: a small all-night laundromat on a dark parking lot, bright inside like a toy. White tile floor with teal grout, mint-green walls, cold-white fluorescent panels. Along the back wall, one row of ten front-load dryers in mint plastic, each door with a big painted number (1 to 10) and a round window. Nine is second from the right. In the middle, a row of top-load washers in pale yellow. A long folding table, four sunflower-yellow plastic chairs bolted in a row, a snack machine with rows of coloured snack shapes (no lettering), a change machine (a coin glyph only), a round wall clock with tick marks and no numbers, high over the front door. Big front windows: the dark navy lot, one streetlight, Jonah's plain white box truck (no lettering) packed and parked, and a big cream moon above it.

- **Dryer nine is older than the room** (the lore plant): under the same mint paint, its body is riveted iron, its door ring is worn brass with a row of rivets, and its timer sits in a brass bezel. The other nine dryers are smooth plastic. Nobody says so.
- **No text anywhere.** Numbers on the dryers and timers are fine. Signs are glyphs: a washer glyph over the door, a coin glyph on the change machine, a sunrise glyph (half sun over a line) on my phone.
- **What's true underneath (never said beyond the reveal):** the town's night is dried in dryer nine. It runs from dusk to dawn, and morning comes when it's done. A quarter adds ten minutes of night, and the moon slides back up. Too much night won't fit in the drum, and with the door open it climbs out, filling the room with dark. With the door shut it stays in and runs down. It never touches anyone. Nobody is ever taken or hurt.
