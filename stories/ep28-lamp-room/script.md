## Premise
On our first trip away, a month in, Ines and I rent an old lighthouse on a sea cliff for a stormy February week. It was built for a crew of two and still sleeps two: her bedroom halfway up, my cot in the lamp room at the top, because I'm shy about it being so soon. Every night a text from her wakes me ("Who's that on the stairs?"), I'm at the rail with my flashlight, and far below at the foot of the spiral stair a figure in a yellow oilskin looks straight up at me with a face on the top of its head. Each night it's nearer.

## The rule
The first line states it as a warning: "In this lighthouse, never leave your floor after dark." (L01, 2.2 s or less), and L02 tightens it: "Not even for a text." (the package title says the same rule as a statement). The stairs are steep, and the storm door at the bottom opens onto the sea. I break it every night without knowing.

## The twist (and where it's planted)
The one on the stairs was me. I sleepwalk, and every night I've walked a turn further down toward the storm door, while the Upward came up a turn every time I came down (so it looked two turns nearer each night). It isn't guarding me and it isn't kind. It only counts: it keeps level with whoever is on the stairs, one turn for every turn, and the story never says why. Ines saw me pass her door and texted to wake me. "Who's that on the stairs?" turns from her fear into her watching over me, and L03 ("mine at the TOP") turns out to be only where I fell asleep. L01 turns over too: I left my floor every night, and in the end Ines leaves hers to sit between me and the storm door. L02 ("Not even for a text.") turns over with it, because every text was her way of bringing me back.

## World
An old whitewashed stone lighthouse rented out as a vacation place, on a grassy cliff top, in a February storm. Inside, a tight stone spiral stair (seven turns, top to bottom) with an iron handrail on the open side and a deep central well, so from the top you look straight down the well to the bottom floor. Slate floor at the bottom, a heavy plank storm door with a drift of pale sand under it. Halfway up (turn 4), Ines's bedroom door, painted sea blue, with a warm wall lamp beside it. At the top, the lamp room: round, glass all round, the big old lens dark and dead in its brass frame in the middle, my cot beside it, rain streaming down the glass. The lamp-room door is oak with a small brass bolt plate. A kitchen on the ground floor beside the stair foot (breakfast table, window onto gray sea). No text anywhere in the set, and the phone screen always faces away or shows only a glow (captions carry the words). Nobody goes outside or near the cliff edge. No lightning.
