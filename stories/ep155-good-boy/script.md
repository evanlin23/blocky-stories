## The big idea
**In our town a lost dog walks home as a person until someone calls its name, and the man who spends the whole day on the Dales' porch helping them wait for their beagle Pepper is Pepper.**

- **Why it clicks:** the rule's fine print is said in the first fifteen seconds as plain world-building (dog-people can't read, can't sit still for a doorbell, and never know what they are, only who they love), and each line of it is me: the poster's letters wiggle for me (L11), I'm at the window first on every "Ding-dong." (L09), I've "never trusted" their vacuum (L13), I eat the sausage left out for Pepper (L15), and I sit on the exact step where Pepper always waited (L18).
- **The misdirect is love:** the day is spent getting ready for Pepper (his bowl, his blanket, the poster on the door), so every kind thing I do for the Dales is recast as a dog coming home.
- **Ten pitched, this one kept.** Dropped: a lost cat (the rule reads less warmly), the dog comes home as Grandpa (confusing, and a death read), the family knows and hides it (no reveal), the dog can talk (silly), the dog is a Keeper (canon), a dog that left on purpose (sad), a stray picks the house (no "his owners"), the man is a burglar the dog sent (eerie, off lane), the dog is a puppy (a young one in peril), the dog stays a man forever (loss).
- **Checked against "Claimed big ideas":** not ep139 (future me speaks to my beagle), not ep145 (a dog judges a stranger), not ep108 (a grandpa swaps in for a scarecrow), not ep52's lost things hopping home. The narrator is the lost pet, never told. Shape `about-me`.
- **The rule is the clue:** "until someone calls its name" sounds like the danger (say it on the street and it's lost again). It's the welcome home.
