## The big idea
**The silent reading room's thing is made of every laugh anyone ever swallowed under its dome, it drifts toward whoever is holding in the most, and if it reaches you, you never laugh again. My brother has held in forty days.**
- Engine: held-back feelings that take a body (an old myth shape, no specific work).
- The rule is the clue: "days since you last laughed" reads at first as a fun stat. After the reveal it's a danger meter, and the room's silence is what builds the thing. Every swallowed snort we watch is the thing being fed.
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: no other story has a creature built from what people hold back. Closest is ep47 (lie tags), which is about lies on clothes, not a body made of feelings.

## Premise
In this town a glowing number floats over every head: the days since you last laughed. Almost everyone shows a 0 or a 1. My little brother Tam (an adult, never shown with an age) has finals, and he's crammed for six weeks in the old reading room, where nobody makes a sound. His number says 40. I sit beside him and keep quiet too. One afternoon I notice a reader with no number at all, a person printed in reverse with a grin drawn upside down, and every time someone in the room swallows a laugh, it's a table nearer to Tam.

## Rule
- **The world's rule (L01 to L02):** the number over your head is the days since you last laughed. One laugh rolls it back to zero (L03). Nobody explains it on screen, nobody made it, and it's just how the town is.
- **The place's rule, shown and said once:** under the reading room's glass dome, nobody makes a sound (L09). I break it at L37 to L40.
- **The rule meter:** a number is on screen in every shot. Tam's 40 is the meter of the story and sits in frame (or at a frame edge) in every reading-room shot until L42.

## Twist (and where it's planted)
**The thing is made of swallowed laughs, so the silence is what feeds it, and the laugh the room forbids is the only thing that undoes it.** The first line plays one way first time ("forty is a funny, sad stat about my stressed brother") and another on the loop ("forty days of laughs held in is the thing's whole meal, and the town's number is a warning").
- **Plant 1 (L16 to L18, S27 to S30):** a man two rows over snorts and swallows it, and when I look back the thing is a table nearer. On the first watch it reads as "it moves when you look away". On a rewatch it's the thing growing by one swallowed laugh. My own swallowed laugh at L20 moves it again (S33 to S35), so the viewer sees it twice before L30 says why.
- **Plant 2 (L14, S24):** its mouth is a big open grin drawn upside down (a new mouth decal). It reads as a frown-ish wrongness first time. L31 pays it off plainly: it's a laugh, held the wrong way.
- **Unnarrated (S13):** in the first wide of the room, a dark seated figure is already at the far table in the shadow of the stacks, with nothing over its head. Nobody points at it.

## How the climax turns the rule (the rule's own logic)
1. The rule as stated: one laugh rolls your number back to zero (L03, shown on the woman's 1 rolling to 0).
2. The reveal tells us what the thing is (swallowed laughs, L29 to L30), who it goes to (whoever holds in the most, L32) and what it costs (you never laugh again, L33). Tam is the most, at 40 (L34).
3. So the fix is the rule itself, no new magic: get Tam to laugh once and he's at zero, no longer the one holding in the most (L36).
4. The only way to get a laugh out of him in that room is to break the room's own rule: I stand up and say our dumbest old joke out loud (L37 to L40).
5. Tam cracks (L41), his 40 rolls to 0 (L42), and laughing is catching: the whole room lets go (L43). Every number rolls to 0 (L44). A thing made of held-in laughs can't hold together in a room where nobody's holding in, so it comes apart, and every laugh it swallowed rises up the dome as a soft echo (L45 to L46).
6. The sting by the same logic: next day the dome is silent again, people are swallowing laughs again, and something new is sitting at the far table (L48 to L49).

## The joke (ours, clean, short)
"Why is the dome so QUIET? / Because if it laughed, it'd CRACK up." (L39 to L40). A glass-dome groaner that brothers would have made up in this room years ago. It sets up L41, "Then he CRACKED."
