## The big idea
**This motel makes every review come true: every one of its worst rooms was perfectly fine until a guest wrote a one-star card about it, and the eight-foot clerk who never blinks was a normal guy named Hal until someone wrote that he was, in 1996.**

- **Engine:** the literal wish (whatever is written comes true, with a monkey's-paw sting), our own version. No film's plot, place or people.
- **Why it clicks ("oh, THAT's why"):** the countdown at the top sounds like a list of complaints because it is one. Each "rule" is a review, word for word (the bed is always warm, like somebody just got out of it). Room one is "the only room with no rule, yet" because nobody had reviewed it. Dev, who gives everything one star, writes "the walls are too close", and they come. The clerk is eight feet tall and unblinking because a 1996 card said so. Then the sting: my own five-star card said we never wanted to leave.
- Twelve pitched, this one kept. Dropped: room one is the room you watch from (ep84), the staff are one-star guests kept forever (a place that keeps you, and too close to ep90's tab), the rooms check out with you and turn up at home (no feeling), the rooms are old bedrooms people outgrew (the crowded "forgotten" theme), each room is a year of the same night (time), the rooms rank the guests (no picture), the motel is one room (ep84), the TV narrator is room one's thing (a loop you're in, ep84), five long-stay ghosts (ghosts, sad), a family on the other side of the wall (ep62's copy), the rooms are cheap because a cousin pays (ep83's who-pays), the motel moves at night (no stakes).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not forgotten, not a stand-in, not a reflection, not time, not a loop room. It's words making the world, and the clue is the countdown's own wording.
- **The rule is the clue:** "five rooms you never book" first reads as five haunted rooms. After the reveal, they're five rooms somebody complained about, and "You're in ONE" is the room whose review is still being written.

## The story

Our car breaks down outside at eleven at night. The eight-foot clerk who never blinks gives us room one, the only room with no rule yet, and there's a comment card on each pillow.
