- **Panel must-fixes built in:**
  1. **The logic.** Message 1 is painted at dusk and says "TOMORROW, Theo doesn't walk the creek path". Message 2 is painted at sunrise, which is that same tomorrow, and says "Today, Theo walks the creek path with June". They're real opposites for the same day.
  2. **June hasn't gone yet.** At sunrise she's on the bench by the board, lacing her boots, waving me over (L17, S24), and she's going with or without me (L24). Her stick and pack are beside her on the bench, never at the path. Message 2 says to walk it *with* her, so the order is never in question.
  3. **The stakes are concrete:** "so she comes HOME" (L19), then said plainly in L20: "As if, without me, she DOESN'T." June is an adult and is never shown in danger. The stake is only spoken.
  4. **The dawn-only clue is loud** on three channels. It's said aloud and planted (L02: "It only paints at SUNRISE. Never any other time."). It's in the picture: message 1's board shot is under a huge low orange sun in a pink-orange sky, and every other board shot is under pale morning gold with the sun behind the trees. And it's in the words: message 1 starts "TOMORROW", and L13 says out loud that it never has before.
  5. **Dot's two reactions each point one way.** At dusk (L14) she says, "Somebody's been at my BOARD." That points to side 2: someone else painted message 1. At sunrise (L23) she says, "That isn't OURS," about the path. That points to side 1. She's never shown unsure.
  6. **Distinct from ep136's canyon.** This is a green trailhead at the edge of town: a gravel lot where a town street ends, a white picket fence, a small green warden's hut, a bench, tall pines and a quiet creek. There's no rock, no drop and no red anywhere.
  7. **Balance.** Message 1 has one tell (painted at dusk, "TOMORROW"). Message 2 has one tell (it gives a reason and fills the whole board, which the board never does). Each side also gets one Dot reaction.
- **The lane's staging and build constraints, applied to every shot:**
  1. Flash-safe. Nothing turns on or off, blinks or pulses. The hut has no lamp. There's one slow light change, a 1.6 s fade from dusk to sunrise on a held wide of the tree line with the board out of frame (L15). The board's words change only across cuts and are never painted on screen.
  2. Two faces at most in a shot. Every emotional line and the last question go on a single close-up facing camera. June and I share a frame only once, with a depth stagger (L24).
  3. Spacing. June on the bench and me at the board are 3 m apart, staggered in depth. Dot is always alone in frame. Sits and turns are eased over 0.4 s or more, walks happen on cuts, and nobody crosses a shot in under 1 s.
  4. No small prop has to read. The board is 2.4 m wide, its lettering is our own glyph script (hard rule 1: no real words in the set), and the captions carry the words. The clues are the board's size and the number of lines on it, the sky, the sun, faces, spoken lines, a big canoe, a giant mushroom and a whole new path.
  5. Nothing is glued to the camera. The marker is the board, in the scene.
  6. One set: the trailhead, seen at dusk and at sunrise.
  7. No creature.

## The big idea
**The trail board that's never wrong painted two opposite rules for the same morning, one at dusk telling me to stay off a creek path that has never existed, and one at sunrise telling me to walk it with my sister so she comes home. By sunrise the path is there, and June has already seen it.**

- It holds up as a film because the board's own habits are the evidence: it paints only at sunrise, always "Today", always one line, and it never says why (L02, L03, L05, L07). Each message breaks exactly one habit.
- Ten pitched, this one kept: the board paints itself (kept); the board warns about the weather (no person at stake); the board writes your name when it's your turn (a meter, no contradiction); the board and the warden disagree (a person, not the same source); two boards at two trailheads (two sources, against the lane); the path is a person (a creature); the board is the warden's handwriting (a cover story); a lake raft (ep113's lake); a creek that flows uphill (no stake); a mountain whose trails swap at night (confusing).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas": not ep102 (a road that forgets you), not ep95 (a maze as narrator), not ep109 (a voice names who to avoid).

## The story (the lane's engine)
1. **A town rule with a payoff, working for two people (L01 to L07):** every sunrise the board paints one rule, and obeying it pays off. Dot gets a mushroom the size of her head. Marcus gets his lost canoe back.
2. **My turn (L08 to L14):** I'm Theo, and on Saturday my big sister June says she's doing "the creek path" at dawn. There's no creek path (the creepy detail: the warning names something that doesn't exist, and June named it first). At **dusk**, which it never does, the board paints **message 1:** "TOMORROW, Theo doesn't walk the creek path." Dot, locking up, says, "Somebody's been at my BOARD."
3. **Message 2, from the same board (L15 to L22):** at sunrise there's a path along the creek. June is lacing her boots. The board has painted over itself: "Today, Theo walks the creek path with June, so she comes HOME." It says the opposite and raises the stakes: without me, she doesn't come home. It gives a reason, which it never does, and it fills the whole board.
4. **No answer (L23 to L27):** Dot says the path "isn't OURS." June's going, with or without me. "Which board do I follow?"
- **The marker (the rule meter):** the big forest-green board, in frame in every shot except the fade wide and two face close-ups (where its edge stays in frame).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** the board has never painted twice for one person, or at dusk.
- **The loved one at stake:** my big sister June.
- **The loop:** L27's question runs back into L01 ("Every sunrise, our trail board paints a rule you HAVE to obey.") Two boards, one rule, and you have to obey it.
