**Line 1, "My shadow has never left my feet."** Yes, a concrete, personal oddity (confess shape, tagged in package.json). It is not yet a threat or a rule, but L01b ("Everyone else's goes where they're happiest.") supplies the rule at about 2 s. The pair works, and it reads fine in captions at phone size. A viewer wants to look at the feet, which is the point of the shot.

**Frame 1 (0.3 s):** a low close-up of two orange-trouser knees and shoes on green grass. It is a true close-up, bright and readable at 300 px, with no wide first. But **the shadow is not visible**. There is only a faint dark smear at the far left edge and a soft darker band under the left shoe, and neither reads as "a shadow stuck to my feet". The line promises a picture the frame does not show, so the hook has nothing to prove it.

**1.5 s (f00045):** a medium-wide of me and a second walker, with a tree and the horizon. This is a wide in the 0-3 s window, so it breaks "no wide in 0-3 s". Faces are small at phone size, and there is still no shadow under me. The loose shadows on the grass at the right edge are too dark and too small to read as "everyone else's".

**3.0 s (f00090):** the same wide, with a purple-shirted stranger clipping in at the left edge, cropped. A cropped head and a muddled frame. The grass is empty, with no lone shadow doing anything odd.

**4.5 s (f00135):** a legs-only close-up of a third person on grey pavement, with no shadow under them. This is the moment that should show "nobody has a shadow under them", and the legs do it (nothing under the shoes), but it is not tied to my feet, so the contrast with mine is lost. The shot is flat and grey, and the tone does not match the bright grass.

**Phone-size read:** frames 1 and 4.5 are clear. Frames 2 and 3 are weak: small faces, one cropped head. The rule marker (the shadow) never reads as what it is.
