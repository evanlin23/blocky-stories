## The story

- **Premise:** I live on a narrowboat on a quiet canal with my old dog Moss. For twelve years I opened the hatch at first light and said "good morning, Moss", and she climbed the three steps to wag it back. This summer her legs got slow and she stopped coming up, so I stopped saying it. Now, each dawn in the autumn fog, something on the towpath says "good morning" in my voice, and Moss climbs up to meet it. It's a person built as long as my boat, lying face up, one mooring ring nearer each dawn.
- **The rule:** my own, and the boat's: the first words of every day are "good morning, Moss", said at the hatch. I break it this summer, before the story starts, by stepping around her bed and going out without a word. The rule is never called a rule on screen. It's shown as a habit (L04 to L09).
- **The twist:** it was never greeting me. A boat's morning greeting is a roll call of the crew, and on a boat everyone aboard is crew, a dog included. When I stopped calling it, something came and called it for me, to her, in my voice, because it was doing my job. The first line, "something on the towpath says good morning in my voice", turns over at the end into "somebody says good morning to my dog in my voice. It's me."
- **Where the twist is planted** (two plants a rewatch catches):
  - **Plant 1 (L13, about 0:22):** on the first strange morning Moss is already sitting at the foot of the steps, looking up at the shut hatch, in the shot under L10 before the voice speaks. She's waiting to be called, the way she waited for twelve years. Held a full 1.5 s at L13.
  - **Plant 2 (L12 and L40):** the voice only ever says the two words. "Just those TWO WORDS" (L12), then "It never once said MY NAME" (L40). It never addressed me.
  - **Also for the rewatch (picture only):** L31, Moss doesn't turn when I say her name, because my voice wasn't saying the morning words. And the Long Man's head at L41 turns toward the boat's back deck, where the hatch is, not toward me.
- **The last 15 seconds:** L71 to L76. The fog eases off the water, the towpath is empty, the tally on the ring, then my own morning walk with Moss at her pace, and the last line says the first line again with "something" turned into "somebody", "to my dog" added, and "It's ME." The voice the first line was scared of is now mine, doing the job.
- **World:** a narrowboat on a still, nameless canal in early autumn. Navy hull with cream coach lines, a short back deck with a sliding wooden hatch over three steps down into the back cabin, my bunk with one small round window on the towpath side, Moss's bed at the foot of the steps. A gravel and grass towpath right alongside, set with old iron mooring rings every 4 m along the stone edge. Dawn fog on flat water, a line of willows on the far bank, a hedge behind the towpath. No bridges, locks, signs, boat names or any text anywhere.
- **Cast:**

Our own face: tired eyes, short brows, a small mouth. Expressions used: `neutral`, `worried`, `scared`, `wide`, `smirk` at the very end (a small smile).
  - `Moss`: my old dog, never in peril, never touched by the Long Man. Brown and tan coat with a white chest. Two drawn dot eyes with highlights. She's posed, never seen walking fast. She never leaves the boat until the walk at L74.
  - Nobody else. No other boats are moored in sight.

## The lore connection

- **What:** a plain Keeper tally, two short upright strokes, pressed into the top of the iron mooring ring by my bunk window, where the Long Man's head last lay, in its canal-weed green (`#4d6638`). One stroke for me and one for Moss. On a boat everyone aboard is crew, a dog included. There's no extra stroke, because I'm calling the roll again and it's no longer standing in the gap.
- **Where:** P03, after L72 ("The towpath / was EMPTY."), held 1.5 s, never narrated.
- It touches no open thread, names nothing, and shows only this one Keeper.
