- **Word count:** 118 spoken words in 19 lines, all `narrator`, plus one 3.0 s hold beat (P01) and one 0.6 s drop (P02).

## The big idea
The extra face in the elevator mirror isn't a reflection. It's something standing in the glass, practicing being my brother, and when the doors open it trades places with him.

- **Wait, what:** the puzzle the viewer just solved was the thing getting ready. By the end the C in the car is the copy, and "Five people got in the elevator" is no longer true on the loop.
- **Different from the claimed ones:** mirror-maze is "your reflection stays behind when you walk away" (the narrator is a left-behind reflection). ep50 is "your reflection does what you really want". Ours is a swap: an outside thing in the glass, which never was anyone's reflection, takes a real person's place and leaves him in the mirror.

## The story
- **Premise / hook:** five people in an office elevator, all facing the mirror-steel doors. The doors show six faces. You've got three floors to find the extra one.
- **The rule (line 1):** "Five people got in the elevator. The mirror shows SIX." A count that doesn't add up, said over a close on the doors' six faces.
- **The challenge (line 2):** "Find the EXTRA one." Then letters A to E pop over the five backs, and the floor display counts the last three floors while the picture holds.
- **The answer:** C. Two figures in the glass wear C's blue jacket. A real reflection keeps C's orange bag on the same side of the screen as C's back. The extra one has it on the other side: it didn't flip, so it isn't a reflection.
- **Twist:** C is my big brother. The doors open (the mirror splits into the walls and the reflections go), and close again. Now the C in front of me has his bag on the other shoulder and his floating letter is backwards. The thing in the glass swapped with him. My real brother is in the glass.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over line 1:** first time, "five people, six in the mirror" is a puzzle. On the loop, one of the five "people" in the car is the copy, and the sixth in the glass is my brother. "Now the EXTRA one's out HERE." plays straight into "Five people got in the elevator. The mirror shows six." as one thought.
- **Plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. **The gap.** From frame 1 the front row has one empty spot, exactly one person wide, just left of C. In the glass, the copy stands in it. The car has room for the sixth and nobody in it.
  2. **The eyes.** Every reflection looks at its own rider's eyes. The copy alone looks straight into the lens (at me, D) from the first frame. Its eyes never change. (Its expression is `neutral` like C's, so it only reads on a second look.)
  3. Small: L13's "practicing being him" says the swap before it happens.
- **Ending shape:** a puzzle answered, then a quiet swap across one door cycle, then a cold close on my real brother inside the glass, and a loop.
- **Warmth:** none needed beyond one beat. I'm standing right behind my brother, close as brothers stand, and it's my brother I'm looking at in the glass at the end. No sad family reveal: it ends on a sting, not grief.

## Building the mirror (for the builder)
- Fake the doors as a glass plane over a mirrored duplicate set: the car, the lights and the five riders copied across the door plane (scale -1 on the axis through the doors, normals fixed), so every reflection keeps its rider's screen side, as a real mirror does.
- **The copy is not mirrored.** It's C's model turned 180 degrees to face out, placed in the gap's spot in the duplicate set. That's the whole tell: its bag lands on the opposite screen side from C's.
- **After the swap:** the car's C is the mirrored model (scale -1), with the tag flipped. The duplicate set now holds the copy's reflection at C's spot and my brother (C's model turned 180 degrees, not mirrored, `worried`) in the gap.
- The door halves carry the glass and the duplicate set slides with them out of view on L14 (or the duplicate set is hidden as the halves part). Behind them: a bright empty corridor, white walls, a teal floor stripe, nobody.
- A faint steel tint and slight blur on the glass so the doors read as polished steel, but the bag colour and faces must stay crisp at phone size.

## Risks
- **Can a phone viewer solve it?** Two blue jackets in the glass are easy to spot. The bag side is the "why". Test the hold frame at phone size before anything else: both blue jackets, both bags and the gap must read in the S09 frame.
- **The letters' voice:** "A" must not come out as "uh". Respell as above.
- **Third reflection story** (after mirror-maze and ep50). The swap and the puzzle format make it a different idea, but the critic should judge that.
- **Mirror nitpickers** will argue about what flips. That's comments, and fine.
