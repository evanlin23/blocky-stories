## The big idea
**Everyone warns you never to eat a peach in my aunt's valley or every road out leads back to her gate, and the warning is backwards: the valley's road only remembers the people who've eaten there. For twelve summers I never ate one, so the road has been forgetting me, a few miles longer every year, and this summer it rubbed itself out behind me.**

- **Engine:** food of the other world (engine 29), turned over. No myth's pomegranate, no film's orchard: our peach, our road, our odometer.
- **Why it clicks:** the drive getting longer every summer (L09 to L12) is the road forgetting me. The mail carrier and the two brothers (L03 to L06) always "end up back" here, which sounds like a trap, and it turns out they leave all the time and are always home by lunch (L27, L28). Mom "keeps missing the turn" (L14, L15) because she stopped eating them when she moved away. Aunt Clem's peach by my bag every summer (L07) was her trying.
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the peach makes you forget the city (crowded memory theme), the valley's people are all peaches (a gag), the peach makes you a tree (body horror), Aunt Clem is the valley (a creature twist), the road eats people (a monster), you age there (ep33's time), the valley is a model (ep80), the peach is a debt (ep90's tab), Aunt Clem is lonely and lying (sad), the peaches are free and someone pays (ep83).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not a stand-in, not ep52 (nothing I own travels, a road forgets a person), not ep93's loop, not a fed thing. Shape `backwards-warning`.
- **The rule is the clue:** "every road out leads back to her gate" first reads as a trap. After L25 it reads as a promise: the road will always bring you back.

## The story
- **Premise:** Everyone warns visitors never to eat a peach in Aunt Clem's valley, or every road out leads back to her gate. I've visited twelve summers and never eaten one. Every summer the drive there gets longer, though the map says nothing's moved. This summer it takes two days, and in my mirror the road behind me is gone.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 3.1 s):** "Never eat Aunt Clem's PEACHES, / or every road out / leads back to her GATE." The ban and its price in one breath, with our picture in it (a road that loops back to one gate).
- **The relatable want or fight:** a family place you love and keep at arm's length, and a mom who's drifted from her sister.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream odometer tag in the top-left, a road glyph and a mile count to Aunt Clem's gate: 412 in the hook (this summer), rolling back to 12, 30, 90, 240 through the montage, climbing during the drive, and 12 again at L36. A tiny peach glyph beside it stays grey until I eat one at L34, then turns gold.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L08, 3 to 17 s):** the mail carrier on his route (L03, L04), the two hiking brothers at their gas station (L05, L06), Aunt Clem's peach by my bag and on the sill (L07, L08).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** the odometer: 412 miles to a valley that was 12 miles away my first summer (frame 1), and the map that says it hasn't moved (L13).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** everyone in the valley has eaten one. I never have.
- **The loved one at stake:** Aunt Clem, my mom's sister, named at L01 and L07 in the same shots as her, and Mom, named at L14.
- **The rule broken:** none at first. The road breaks first: it vanishes behind me (L18 to L20).
- **First real scare:** L18 to L20 (about 31 to 37 s): the road behind me in the mirror is gone, just grass, as if it had never been there.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** the warning says eating means every road leads back. That's the cure, not the trap. So I eat (L34). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L23 to L26, about 39 to 46 s):** said plainly (L24, L25) over one picture (S25: Aunt Clem at her gate with the peach, the road a strip of grass behind my car).
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** the drive home is twelve minutes, Mom eats the second peach and finds the turn for the first time in five years, and now when people ask why I keep going back, I tell them the rule, which loops into L01 and now sounds like a promise.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L04: every route the mail carrier drives "still ends at her gate", and every road "brings" the brothers back (true both ways: they come and go, and always get home).
  2. L07, L08: Aunt Clem leaves a peach by my bag every summer, and I leave it on the sill.
  3. L14 to L16: Mom keeps missing the turn, and she grew up here.
- **Ending shape:** a warning read backwards, then a loop where the same rule is a welcome.
- **Warmth:** belonging. The valley keeps the way home open for anyone who takes something from it.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. Mom is fine and comes back.

## Clarity test (cold, two sentences)
Everyone warns that eating a peach in his Aunt Clem's valley means every road out leads back to her gate, but it's backwards: the valley's road only remembers people who've eaten there, so after twelve summers of never eating one, the road was forgetting him and vanished behind him. He eats one, the drive home takes twelve minutes, and he brings one to his mom, who'd stopped being able to find her own hometown.

- **Aunt Clem's place:** a white farmhouse with a wide porch, a porch swing on chains, a wooden gate with an iron latch, rows of peach trees down the slope, a kitchen with a checked tablecloth.
- **The road:** two lanes over the ridge, one wooden fence post where the turn is, fields of tall grass on both sides.
- **Our apartment in the city:** a kitchen with a window over rooftops.
- **No text anywhere:** the map uses glyphs and lines, the odometer numerals.
- **What's true underneath (never said):** the valley's road knows the people who've eaten from it and always finds them. People who leave without eating are forgotten by it a little more each year, until one year the turn isn't there. Nobody knows why. The old warning came from visitors who ate and kept coming back.
