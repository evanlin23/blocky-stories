## The story
- **Premise:** In a seaside town, forty iron lamps on the boardwalk are lit by hand every night by the Lamp Man, and nobody may walk the boardwalk until he passes. Nobody has seen him up close. My grandpa never watches, and he's yelled at his neighbor over the hedge for forty years. Tonight Grandpa's bed is empty at eight, and I go out on the boardwalk to find him.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.9 s):** "After sunset, / you have to stay OFF the boardwalk / until the Lamp Man PASSES." L02: "And right now, / I'm standing ON it."
- **The relatable want or fight:** a grandpa and his neighbor who can't stand each other, and a grandson who worries about him.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag in the top-left with a lamp glyph and a count, 0 to 40, each lamp lighting as he passes. It reads 28 at L02 (where the story opens), runs back to 0 for the montage of every night (L03 to L08, the tag shows the town's nightly count rolling up), and is at 28 again by L19. It sticks at 31 from L26 to L41, then rolls to 40 by L42. At L46 a small boot glyph beside it shows 3.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L08, 3 to 17 s):** diners with forks up, a man and his dog on a step, the ice cream shutter, a jogger running in place.
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** nobody's seen him up close, nine feet tall (L09, L10), then Grandpa's empty bed (L17).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** the only person on the boardwalk after sunset.
- **The loved one at stake:** my grandpa Russ, named "My grandpa, Russ," at L11 in the same shot as him waving goodnight at his window.
- **The rule broken / first scare (L19 to L29, about 30 to 48 s):** a low cough behind me, a hum high up, the lamps lighting toward me, then the Lamp Man stopped in front of me, knees shaking, the coat blowing open.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** the rule says stay off until the Lamp Man passes. It never says how many the Lamp Man is (L38, L39). I get under the coat. No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L30 to L35, about 49 to 62 s):** said plainly (L32, L33) over one picture (S30: the coat blown open at chest height, Mr. Kowal's face between two buttons, lit from below by the pole's flame).
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** the next morning they yell over the hedge as always, and Grandpa lets the coat out one more foot. The Lamp Man has three pairs of boots now and is twelve feet tall, so trust me, which runs straight into L01.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L19, L20: a cough down low and a hum way up high, two sounds at two heights.
  2. L15: both old men icing a sore shoulder on facing porches every morning.
- **Ending shape:** a secret partnership found out and joined, then a loop that makes the rule bigger.
- **Warmth:** old rivals who were partners all along, and their grandson-and-neighbor under the coat with them.
- **Sad family reveal?** No.

## The creature (the Lamp Man: a person made wrong, never a monster)
Creature-light by default: he is two ordinary men, and he's never fully shown lit until the coat opens. `docs/research/creature-playbook.md` section 2, applied while writing:
- **Wrong silhouette (rule 1):** a man far too tall for the lamp posts, his long coat bending in a second place at chest height, his arms far too short for his height (only Grandpa's forearms come out of the sleeves, up top). At a glance he's a lamplighter with a pole, not a robot, mascot, snowman, appliance, box, dummy or costume, because the lit pole and flame tell you what he is first.
- **What's stretched (rule 2):** the torso, two torsos long, in one dark navy coat that falls to the boots. The arms stay short, the legs stay normal. Built as one set piece in `set.py` from `kit.box` (coat as stacked tapered boxes with a slight kink at chest height, a wide-brim hat, two boots), with Grandpa and Mr. Kowal as `rig.Character`s hidden inside for the open-coat shots.
- **Scale proof in the reveal shot (rules 3 and 13):** S23 and S24: low camera at my chest, framed hat to knees, his hat brim level with the lamp glass beside him, my head at his belt in the same frame.
- **The one lit identity cue (rule 12):** the brass lamp pole with its little flame at the tip. The flame lights the hat brim and the coat front from below.
- **Light (rule 5):** from below, the pole's flame held low and the lamp he's lighting, and from behind, the last lit lamp giving a hard rim to his hat and shoulders. No warm front fill. The top half of the hat's shadow hides where a face would be (it's Grandpa's chin, in shadow).
- **Eyes and mouth (rules 7, 8, 15):** none shown before L30. Under the brim, only darkness until the coat opens.
- **Where he is in each beat:** L01 to L02 his shadow on the planks behind me. L09, L10 a far shape between lamps. L19 out of frame behind me (sound only). L21 a tall shape two lamps back, rim only. L22 to L25 full height beside me, half in shadow. L26 to L29 stopped in front of me, knees shaking. L30, L31 the coat open: Mr. Kowal's face at chest height, Grandpa on his shoulders above. L40 to L43 the coat closed again with me inside (we only see boots, six of them by L46). Never seen moving fast, never chasing.
