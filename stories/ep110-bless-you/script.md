## The big idea
**Every sneeze here lands you beside whoever's thinking about you most, and every one of my sneezes lands me beside the same old stranger in a green armchair, because he's thought about me every day of my life: he's my mom's dad, and Mom takes a pill every morning so she never sneezes, because she'd land next to him.**

- **Engine:** summoned by thought. No folk tale's devil or film's line: our sneezes, our armchair.
- **Why it clicks:** two cups always poured (L11), he already knows my name (L16), he hums the song Mom hums at the stove (L19), Mom has never sneezed and never misses her pill (L20, L21), and the wall of twenty-four years of me (L32 to L34).
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the stranger is a Keeper counting me (a count), he's my future self (time), he's a stalker (no warmth, too dark), he's my real dad (narrows and saddens), the sneezes land me by my ex (romance), he's the town's lonely man who picked me (no family), he's thinking of me because I'm the one he'll pass his house to (a death beat), the old man is my grandpa but he's gone and this is a memory (death), Mom is the stranger in disguise (a silhouette twist), the rule moves the thinker instead (a muddle).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not a stand-in, not ep106's split, not ep107's bought-back calls (nobody buys or keeps anything, a grandpa was kept out by his daughter's pills), not the forgotten theme (I'm thought about, hard). Shape `who-thinks`.

## The story
- **Premise:** In a spring town, every sneeze takes you to whoever's thinking about you most at that moment. Sixty-three sneezes this spring have landed me beside the same old man in a green armchair across town, with two cups of tea already poured. My mom has never sneezed in her life.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.6 s):** "If you sneeze HERE, / you land next to whoever's / thinking about you MOST." L02: "Sixty-three sneezes / this spring. / Same STRANGER. / Every time."
- **The relatable want or fight:** allergy season, and a parent who won't talk about her own parent.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag in the top-left: a pollen glyph with a five-dot count (5 of 5 lit in spring), a sneeze count (63), and a little map pin glyph that's green when I've landed at the armchair. It ticks to 64 at L27, the pin turns green and pulses. At L57 a second pin appears, then both sit side by side at L62.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L08, 3 to 18 s):** the meeting sneezer landing in his mom's kitchen with a plate already out, the dog walker in his dog's bed, the party sneezer in his boss's office.
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** the same stranger sixty-three times (L02), then the armchair (L09 to L11, about 18 to 23 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** everyone lands beside family or friends. I land beside a stranger.
- **The loved one at stake:** my mom, Carol, named "Mom" at L19 with her humming at the stove in frame, and by name at L38.
- **First real scare (L27 to L35, about 50 to 65 s):** a sneeze at 2 a.m. into his dark front room, him awake in the TV light, and the wall behind him covered in me.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** the rule moves whoever sneezes to whoever's thinking about them. So I walk Mom through the pollen, she sneezes and lands beside him (he's thinking about her), and I sneeze right after, because now they're both thinking about me (L59 to L61). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L36 to L45, about 65 to 80 s):** said plainly (L39 to L41) over one picture (S39: the old man in the green armchair in the TV light, the wall of clippings behind him, a framed photo on the side table of a young woman at a stove: Mom at twenty, the same apron she wears now).
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** three cups, Grandpa hums and Mom hums the next part. Mom sneezes a lot now. She says it's the pollen, which runs straight into L01.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L19: he hums the same song Mom hums at the stove.
  2. L20, L21: Mom never sneezes, not even in May, and takes a little white pill every morning without fail.
  3. L11: two cups, always already poured (one was always for her).
- **Ending shape:** a family put back in one room by the rule that kept it apart.
- **Warmth:** a mom and her dad together again, and a grandson who finally knows who's been thinking of him.
- **Sad family reveal?** Yes, soft: twenty-four years apart over a fight nobody remembers. Nobody is gone, and it ends warm.
