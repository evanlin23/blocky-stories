## The big idea
**The thing that walks our court at midnight trying every handle is me, sleepwalking home since the night I moved out, and my aunt has been racing me every night, bolting every other door so the only one I can open is hers.**

- **Engine:** the haunting was you all along (a reversal engine), our own version: nobody is gone, nobody is a ghost, the "intruder" is the narrator asleep. No film's plot, place or people.
- **Why it clicks ("oh, THAT's why"):** one plain mechanism: at midnight she locks ten doors, each with its own key, and before sunrise she unlocks them all again, so nobody is ever shut in. The midnight clicks are locks turning, not doors opening. Ten click, and mine never does because I'm already outside it, asleep in my sneakers. She has a key to mine so she can walk me back before sunrise and lock it behind me. She skips her own door because it's the one she wants me to find. And she told me to lock mine before bed because a locked door would have kept me in.
- Twelve pitched, this one kept. Dropped: she locks everyone in against a tall walker (ep80's street figure and ep81's protective parent in other clothes), the houses swap places at midnight (confusing), her keys lock dreams in (abstract), every key is a person who moved away (sad), the street's houses walk at night (ep82's house that is somebody), her ring has one key too many for a house that comes at night (a count, ep62's lane), she lets herself in to leave gifts (kind all along), the keys open the viewer's house (a gimmick), the locks keep the town's noise in (whimsy), she is the one sleepwalking (a twist on her, no stakes for me), the court is a test she's grading (no picture), the keys open the same house in other years (time, the owner's door example).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not forgotten, not a stand-in, not a reflection, not time. ep41 (killed) also had sleepwalking, but there the prints were the clue and the warmth was a grandmother following a glow. Here it's a camera, a court of doors and an aunt racing me.
- **The rule is the clue:** "a key to every house here, including yours" first reads as a threat. After the reveal it's a promise: she can bolt your door against me, so I never walk into your kitchen asleep.

## The story
- **Premise:** My aunt Lou, the court's locksmith for forty years, has a key to every house on our court. For a month, every door has clicked at midnight and clicked again before sunrise, and the neighbors think she's letting herself in. Last month I moved into number seven, my first place, and gave her no key. She has one anyway. Mine never clicks, and every morning my sneakers are wet. I put a camera on my porch to catch her.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.4 s):** "My aunt has a key / to every house HERE. / Including YOURS." L02 tightens it: "And every night / at MIDNIGHT, / she uses them."
- **The relatable want or fight:** being embarrassed by a relative the neighbors talk about, and wanting your own place to be yours.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** Aunt Lou's brass key ring as a round inset in the top-left corner, our own design: eleven keys with numbered round brass tags (2 to 12, no letters). Every midnight the inset's tags glow teal one by one as each door is locked, and fade as she unlocks them before sunrise. Tag 7, mine, never glows at midnight. At L58 it glows at dawn. Frame 1 is the ring itself, close, in her hand at the end of her sleeve.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L09, 4 to 17 s):** twelve houses round a fountain (L03), the midnight clicks (L04, L05), every lock clicking again before sunrise (L06), Mr. Park sleeping in his armchair facing his door (L07), Mrs. Haddad changing her locks twice, and Aunt Lou having the new key by Friday (L08, L09).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** every door locked at midnight and unlocked before sunrise (L04 to L06, by 10 s), then "ten locks click, mine never does" (L15, L16, at 25 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** every door clicks but mine.
- **The loved one at stake:** my aunt, Lou, named at L11 in the same shot as her on her porch at number one, ring in hand.
- **The rule broken:** L24, about 39 s in: I leave my door unlocked on purpose.
- **First real scare:** L33 to L43 (51 to 62 s): my door opens on the footage, someone walks out slow with arms out, tries every handle, turns, and walks at the camera until a face fills the screen, eyes shut. It's mine.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** sleeping-me is looking for my old room. So we move my old room to me: her spare room's lamp and my squeaky old bed go across the court into number seven (L61 to L67). That night on camera I sit up at 12:04, look around and lie back down. No new magic.
