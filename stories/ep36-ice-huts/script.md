# ep36-ice-huts

**Slug:** `ep36-ice-huts`

## Premise
In this town a small amber flame floats a hand above every head and always leans toward home. Mine never leans toward my flat. It wanders all over town, and I took that to mean I don't have a home. I've just moved back to town. Grandad fishes alone now from his red hut out on the frozen lake, where we used to share two stools over one hole. Each Saturday I drop by and won't sit down, the fog comes and goes, and each time it lifts, a person in a rust-red coat with no flame stands a little nearer his hut, tipped at a slant no one could hold.

## Rule
The world's rule: your flame leans toward home (L01). Shown at work in the montage (L02 to L06), on the ice when the fog comes and every flame swings to the shore (L14), and turned in the climax (L46 to L49). The narrator's own small rule-break, never said as one: I won't sit on the second stool (L18 to L19, L20, L24, L32).

## Twist
My flame was never drifting. It leans toward home, and home is Grandad: it has followed him around town, in the car and across the ice. The first line, "always leans toward HOME", first reads as a fact about houses. After L56 to L58 and the last line ("Mine leans toward GRANDAD.") it reads as the answer to what home is.
- **Plant 1 (S01 and S02, under L01):** in the opening wide on the lake shore, every flame leans back toward the town behind us, and mine leans the other way, out across the ice at the red hut. Never said. In S11 and S12 (L08, "wanders all over TOWN") my flame points somewhere different each shot, and Grandad is small in the background of each, right where it points.
- **Plant 2 (L25 to L27, S32 to S34):** on the drive back he dozes in the back seat and my flame bends back over my shoulder toward him. I blame the car heater.
- Bonus (L18, S23): the blanket folded on the second stool. He's been saving it. It pays off at L61.

## World
A small made-up town in deep winter, on the shore of a big frozen lake. Afternoon sun, low and gold, at the start, then grey-white fog that rolls in and lifts on every visit. On the ice: a dozen small wooden fishing huts painted red, teal, mustard, sage and white, in loose rows, each with a stovepipe and a low door. Snow over the ice in drifts, boot paths between the huts, small round holes with little flagged sticks (plain coloured cloth, no marks). Grandad's hut is the red one, furthest out in the second row: plank walls, a dark tar-paper roof, a low door with an old **iron thumb latch**, one small four-pane window with a checked curtain. Inside: a tiny iron stove, an oil-style lamp on a hook (warm, steady), a shelf, and **two wooden stools over one round hole** in the floor. On the shore: dark pines, a slipway of packed snow, and the town behind (brick and timber houses, a hedged lane, a café window, a kerb). Grandad's old estate car with a cloth interior. No lettering anywhere: no signs, plates, van livery, shop names or labels.

## Lore connection (one)
Two short rust-red strokes scratched into the plate of the red hut's iron door latch (S17, under L12, "I took him a FLASK"), held about 1.8 s, never narrated. Two strokes for a hut built for two, in the Leaner's own rust red. In the canon (`stories/lore.md`), it's a Keeper's tally. The Leaner stands in the gap left by the stool I won't sit on, tips nearer each week the gap stays open, and is gone the moment I fill it. It carries no flame, because a Keeper never carries its town's marker. No extra stroke, and no other thread touched.
