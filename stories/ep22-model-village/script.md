## Premise
I've just moved to a small seaside town, out of season, and taken the job repainting the model village on the front: every house in town, knee high, with tiny painted townsfolk glued in the streets. Nobody in town talks to me. A thumb-sized man in a mustard scarf, made better than any of the painted figures, stands in a different tiny window every afternoon, one window further up the hill each time, toward the street where my own model cottage stands.

## Rule
The houses in the model don't open, and the tiny people are glued down. He isn't, and he gets inside anyway.

## Twist (and where it's planted)
He was never coming toward me. From inside, every afternoon, his window filled with a face bigger than his whole house: mine. Every window I leaned into, he left, house by house up the hill, to the tea shop where all the tiny townsfolk sit. To him, the thing in the window was me. "There's a little man in the window" turns over twice: first he was the one with a giant at his window, and at the end the little man in the tea shop window is the figure of me.
- **Plant 1 (L08, about 0:15):** the first wide of the whole village. Every tiny figure stands with its back to the path side, where I stand. Not narrated. L16 ("He stood with his BACK to me") and L20 say it of him alone, so it reads as his creepiness, not a pattern.
- **Plant 2 (L15 to L32):** he is always in the window after the one I last leaned into. I lean at the bakery (L15), he's gone next day (L18). I lean at the blue door (L21), he's crossed the street by Thursday (L24). I lean at the chapel (L30), he's further up on Friday (L32). L29 ("One window NEARER every DAY") is my misreading, said out loud.
- **The turn (L49 to L55, about 1:52 to 2:05)** is shown as the reverse of L15: the camera inside the tiny bakery, behind him, looking out at my giant face.
- **The last 15 seconds (L66 to L69)** are shot from inside the tiny tea shop, beside my own figure, looking up at kind giant faces. That's the first line seen from his side, and then it's me.

## World
A seaside model village on the front of a small made-up town, on grey out-of-season afternoons. Knee-high houses of painted wood and plaster on a sloping ground of painted green, a main street up a little hill to a chapel and, at the top, a tea shop. Tiny glazed windows. Behind it a low sea wall, wet slate sea, a gravel path round the model's front edge where I kneel. The full-size town is brick and slate on the same hill: my cottage with a yellow door, and the full-size tea shop with steamed windows. No text anywhere: no shop signs on the models or the town, no plaques.

## Cast (all adults, nobody in peril)
- `me`: the narrator, an adult newcomer and painter. Brown block hair, a green work coat with paint flecks, grey trousers. Face drawn plain and a bit tired at first.
- `maggie`: the tea shop owner, an older adult woman. Grey bun hair, a navy and white striped apron, round cheeks. She talks plainly once, at L60 to L61, mouth flapping under the quote. She explains nothing else.
- `townsfolk`: four or five adult extras for L58 and L64 to L67 (a fisherman in a cable jumper, a man with a scarf-less dark coat, a younger woman in a yellow raincoat, an older man in a cap). None wears mustard.
- **The tiny townsfolk** in the model: simple painted blocky figures, glued, matte, a little crude, so the Resident stands out as made better.

## The creature: the Resident
- A perfectly made blocky person, every proportion right, shrunk far too small: the height of my thumb (about 4.5 cm against my rig). That one wrong dimension is the whole creature.
- Navy coat, mustard scarf (the only saturated mustard in the film), warm skin tone. Its face is our own drawing: small round eyes with a sharp highlight, a thin flat mouth. Too neat. Never pale grey and never blank.
- Never seen moving. It's only somewhere new after a cut to a new afternoon.
- One model, built once in `set.py` at scale and posed per shot. Light it inside each tiny room from a low side key (the room's own tiny window light), with a rim from the window. Its eye highlights are the brightest thing in its shots.

- **Caption key colour:** sea-glass teal `#5ccfc0`.
- **Music:** D Dorian, 76 bpm. A low synthesized reed drone and a slow soft plucked figure in the low-mid register, with synthesized sea wash and far gull-like calls. No music box, no bell tones. The drone stops for P01 (0.6 s, only the voice and the hit). From L56 the same tempo moves to D major with a warm pad.

Match the average brightness across the cuts at L42, L50 and L57 (the warm tea shop is warm in hue, not brighter).
