## The big idea
**Our town photo only shows people who'll still be here next year, and I'm missing because from now on I'm the one who takes it: the old photographer has held the camera for eighty years without getting any older, and today he handed it to me.**

- **Wait, what:** the gap was never a warning that I'd be gone. The photo was right. The photographer is never in it, and the job doesn't end.
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: I'm moving away (no twist), the gap is a twin (silly), the photo predicts a loss (sad, against the bar), the gap is a Keeper (canon as twist), the camera steals faces (creature-ish), the photo is from the future (time travel, crowded), I'm already gone (death read), Mom hides me (no rule), the town forgets me (crowded "forgotten" theme), the camera picks its owner by magic (new magic, not the rule's logic).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas": not floor-thirteen or ep43 (forgotten or unseen: everybody sees me all along), not ep20 (a photo of two sisters), not ep134 (extra rooms are me). Nobody forgets anyone.
- **The rule is the clue:** "shows people who'll still be here" is about who's in front of the lens. It never promised to count the one behind it.

## The story
- **Rule / hook (L01, 14 words, about 2.6 s):** "Our town photo / only shows people / who'll still be HERE / next year."
- **The count (L02, L03):** 212 posed, 211 faces, the missing one is me. Stakes in the same breath: missing means not here next year.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen from frame 1 and in every shot:** the town's wooden headcount board, a green chalkboard on an easel beside the bleachers with two chalk numbers, "212" over "211", our own plain numerals. In close shots it rides as a small corner copy (top-left, chalk-green tile, white numerals). It changes once: at L27 the bottom number is wiped and rewritten "212".
- **Examples (L04 to L07, 2 jokes, about 4 to 10 s):** Uncle Benny's blank spot last year (he sells kites on the coast now), and Mrs. Pell, who retires every year and is in every photo.
- **Stakes-raising beat (L08, L09, about 9 to 12 s):** my mom, Dana, counted the proof nine times, then stopped letting go of my hand.
- **Countdown (clock times aloud):** "Saturday. Nine A.M." (L10), "Eight fifty-NINE." (L21).
- **The silly plant that becomes the key:** Mr. Okafor yells "Say PICKLES!" every year, and the whole town yells it back (L12, L13). At L28 I hear myself say it. That's the moment the viewer knows the job is mine.
- **Escalation (L14 to L17):** test shots. Right in the middle: a gap where I sat. Holding Mom's hand: in the print, her hand holds nothing.
- **Plant 1 (L18 to L20):** eighty years of photos on the town hall wall, and Mr. Okafor isn't in one. "Obviously. He's always behind the CAMERA." It plays as a joke the first time.
- **Plant 2 (L22, L23):** his hands shake too much to hold it still. He looks straight at me and holds it out, as if he'd always known it would be me.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** I stand behind the camera, so I'm not in the photo, and the count comes out whole (212, with Mr. Okafor in the front row). No new magic: the photo was counting right all week.
- **Reveal (L31 to L35, said plainly over one picture):** for the first time in eighty years, Mr. Okafor is in the print. My grandma says he looked exactly this old when she was a girl. He pats my shoulder: "Every year now. Your TURN."
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** "only shows people who'll still be here next year" first reads as a threat to me. On the loop, it's my job description: I'll be here every year, just never in it.
- **Ending shape:** `behind-camera`. Ends eerie: relief, then the sting that the job doesn't end.
- **Warmth:** a town that turns out every year for its photo, and Mom's hand.

## Clarity test (cold, two sentences)
In this town the yearly photo only shows people who'll still be around next year, and the narrator is missing from the proof, so he dreads leaving all week. On photo day the old photographer hands him the camera, so he isn't missing at all, he's the new photographer, and the old man, who hasn't aged in eighty years and was never in a photo, finally sits in it while the narrator takes over for good.

## World
- One small bright town, one square, one Saturday photo every year. Everybody comes. The photo is printed on the spot from a big wooden box camera on a tripod (our own design, brass fittings, no lettering, no logo).
- Nobody explains why the photo knows. It's simply how this town is.

## The shot plan
Build rules for every shot: characters stand 1.4 m apart or more unless touching on purpose (Mom holding my hand, seated rows shoulder to shoulder). Sits, stands and turns are eased over at least 0.4 s, and no turn over 45 degrees within 2 frames. The square has sky all round; the hallway has a full ceiling. No camera move into an unlit volume. No wall or bench fills the frame clipped white: the white bleachers are shot at an angle with faces in frame. No push ends on the flat face of a prop (pushes on prints end with a face or a hand in frame). Every named prop (the print, the camera, the shutter bulb, the chalkboard) is raised at chest height on the camera side and big at phone size. A cut or punch-in every 1 to 2 s, nothing still over 1 s.
