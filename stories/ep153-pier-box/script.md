## The big idea
**The lock-box on our pier answers whatever you ask in your own handwriting and has never once sent two cards, and when I asked whether to tell my best friend Ty about the bike I built him, it sent two that disagree, and either could be Ty's, because Ty knows my handwriting.**

## Clarity test (cold, two sentences)
A lock-box on a pier answers whatever you ask in your own handwriting and has never sent anyone two cards, and when the narrator asks whether to tell his best friend Ty about the extra-tall bike he built him as a surprise, he gets two cards: "Tell him tonight" and "Don't, he already knows." Each card has clues that it's really his own (his blue pen and his nickname for Ty on one, his upset slant and Ty's odd behavior on the other), but Ty knows both his nickname and his handwriting, so the narrator asks the viewer which card to trust.

## World
- One bright bay town with a fishing pier and one old green lock-box at its end. You drop a folded question through the slot at night, and in the morning the lid is unlocked with one card inside. Always one.
- Nobody explains the box. People ask it small things, and it's always right.
