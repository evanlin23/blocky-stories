## The big idea
**The lock-box on our pier answers one question per person, ever, in your own handwriting, and when I asked whether to tell my best friend I'm moving, it answered twice and disagreed, and both cards could be mine, or both could be Ty's, because Ty knows my handwriting.**

## Clarity test (cold, two sentences)
A lock-box on a pier answers one question per person, ever, in the asker's own handwriting, and when the narrator asks whether to tell his best friend Ty he's moving across the country, he gets two cards: "Tell him tonight" and "Don't, he already knows." Each card has clues that it's really his own (his blue pen and his nickname for Ty on one, his upset slant and Ty's odd helpfulness on the other), but Ty knows both his nickname and his handwriting, so the narrator asks the viewer which card to trust.

## World
- One bright bay town with a fishing pier and one old green lock-box at its end. You drop a folded question through the slot at night, and in the morning the lid is unlocked with one card inside.
- Nobody explains the box. Everybody gets one question, and most save it for years.
