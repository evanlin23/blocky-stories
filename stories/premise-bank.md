# Premise bank (for the 2026-11-03 reset)

Thirty premises written from `docs/research/2026-11-03-market-analysis.md` and `playbook.md`, for the 2026-11-09 batch
and after. The writer takes from the top. Each one has a title, a first line, the want or fight anyone has had, and the
evidence it rests on. None takes another channel's premise, characters or titles, and none repeats a rule type from
`stories/ideas.md` → Made. Hard rules 1 to 5 hold: fiction, no deaths or children in peril (siblings are teens or
adults), not aimed at kids, no game brand, and each one a different world, cast and twist shape.

Shape: **story** means 100 to 150 s, **gag** means 20 to 45 s (playbook item 2).

## How it was ranked (blind panel, 2026-11-03)
Two fresh agents that had read nothing in this repo:
- **Panel A** ranked 40 titles alone: these 30 shuffled with 10 real fast-risers from the 2026-11-03 deep scan
  (hashtags removed). **The real titles took ranks 1 to 6, 10, 14, 37 and 40. Our best were 7, 8 and 9.** The panel
  also picked out ours as one writer ("Title Case, short premise, one trailing emoji"), and two titles as British.
- **Panel B** ranked our 30 on title plus first line, for "would watch to the end and comment".
- **Combined score** = the mean of our rank among ours in A (1 to 30) and our rank in B. Lower is better.

What the panels taught us (also in `playbook.md`):
1. **Our titles read as a template.** The real hits mix shapes: an ALL-CAPS rule, a first-person "I get STRONGER!",
   "I Confessed to My Crush on...", a trailing "..." with 😭. Vary the shape story to story (hard rule 5 applies to
   titles too). Panel A's taste for caps and "..." disagrees with the view data (caps neutral), so this is a style
   lesson, not a caps rule. A panel of one model may also know the real titles: weigh it lightly.
2. **Write in US English.** 62% of our views are US. Panel B marked "Mum", "neighbour", "trolley" and "petrol" as costing hook
   strength. The fixes are below.
3. **The concrete, creepy, personal first lines won** (warm shoes, a roommate who never leaves, a best friend who didn't
   come back). **The pure family gags came last in both panels** (thermostat, dad jokes, mom hears walls, gym gravity). Gags
   live on the picture, so test one, but don't lead with them.
4. Panel B's "already seen": the battery rule, the body swap, the pause remote. Each needs a twist that isn't the obvious one.
