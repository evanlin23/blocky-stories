## The big idea
**Wrapped, a present here is whatever you hope it is. The present I gave Dad when I stormed out at eighteen was an empty box, and for twelve years it held the one thing he hoped for: me coming home. So when he finally opens it, it's empty, because what he hoped for is standing in his doorway.**

- **Why it clicks:** the box got heavier over the years (L27, L28), something inside walked up the steps (L29 to L31), its bow always faces the front door (L25), and Dad says it holds "everything" (L23). After L56, every one of those was the hope of me walking in.
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the box holds an apology in my voice (a sound effect, no picture), the box holds my childhood (forgetting is crowded), Dad's box for me (wrong way round), a box that grants wishes (ep101's well), a box that's a trap (no warmth), a box that grows on screen (a set move), a box that holds the fight (too dark), a box Dad wrapped for Mom (no rule), a box that holds a person (horror, wrong tone), a box that's opened in the first minute (no wait).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas": not ep121 (a fortune you write for someone else), not ep124 (a call made to listen), not ep120 (a promise kept by staying away). Nothing travels in time and nobody is replaced. Shape `empty-box`.
- **The rule is the clue:** "Wrapped, it's whatever you hope" sounds like a fun gift rule. After L56, it's a man who kept my insult wrapped for twelve years so it could keep being his hope.

## Clarity test (cold, two sentences)
In this town a wrapped present is whatever you hope it is until it's opened. The narrator gave his dad an empty box out of spite when he moved out, and his dad kept it wrapped for twelve years, so when the narrator moves home and his dad finally opens it, it's empty because what he hoped for, his boy coming home, has already happened.
