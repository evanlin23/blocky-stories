## The big idea
**If your best friend stops talking to you here, you take a number and wait in the hall for them to walk in. For six years the board has been stuck on 210, waiting on the man in the chair in front of me, 211, and he's Marcus, my best friend. He's been waiting for me to walk in, and I've been waiting for him, one chair apart, and neither of us could see the other because every chair faces the door.**

- **Why it clicks:** turning around sends you to the end of the line (L04, L05), the chairs face the door so you only see faces when someone walks in (L06), 211 laughs my laugh (L26, L27), the clerk says 211 is waiting on someone stubborn (L30 to L32), and 211 came in the same week I did (L33). In this town, if your best friend stops talking to you, you take a number and wait in a hall facing the door until they walk in, and turning around sends you to the back of the line. ## World
- **The waiting hall:** a long bright room downtown. Mint walls, honey wood floor, three high windows on one side with steady sun shafts. Ten rows of tall-backed mint chairs, all facing one big green door at the front. Each chair back is taller than a seated head and has its big cream number painted on the back, so you see the number of the chair in front of you and nothing else. The flip board over the door is as wide as the door, three cream cards each the size of a chair seat, with black numerals. The clerk's desk sits by the door, side-on to the rows, a big brass stamp on it.
- **The rule's own law:** you sit facing the door. Turn around, and you go to the back of the line. When the person you're waiting for walks in, the board flips to your number and you get up and go to them.
- **Outside the door:** a sunny sidewalk and a bench.
- **No text anywhere,** only numerals.
- **What's true underneath (never said):** the hall is simply how that town is.
