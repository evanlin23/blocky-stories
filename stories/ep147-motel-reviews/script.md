## The big idea
**At this motel every review card comes true, and the polite man in our closet was never the motel's: he was the last guest, kept in Room Six by a card that said he never left, and when I write him out, the card he leaves behind is about my dad and me.**

- **Wait, what:** the closet man isn't a haunting. He's what happens to a guest someone reviewed. Every joke review (the singing ice machine, the bucket pool) was the same power, aimed at a room. Aimed at a person, it keeps him.
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the clerk is a creature (ep87's failed still), the motel eats guests (graphic), the man is a ghost (death read), the cards are written by the motel (unexplained power), we write our way out cleanly (no sting), Dad wrote the card (confusing), the man is my future self (crowded), the cards rewrite the past (time), the closet leads somewhere (a door to elsewhere, owner's example family), the man is a mannequin (rule 1: dummy).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas": ep87 was "words make the place" (the clerk and the bad rooms written into being). This one is "words keep a person", and the twist is the next card. Not ep96 (a card with a double reading) or ep121 (a fortune given). Nobody is hurt or taken.
- **The rule is the clue:** "you get whatever the last guest wrote" is said as a joke in L02. It's the whole mechanism.

## The story

Its filled stars change as cards change: our first card shows 5 stars; the card about us at the end shows 1.
- **Examples (L03, L04, 2 jokes):** five stars, the ice machine sings (it does, opera). One star, the pool's the size of a bucket (it's a bucket).
- **Stakes-raising beat (L06 to L09, about 9 to 14 s):** our card says the man in the closet is very polite. Dad laughs and opens the closet. A man in a pressed gray suit, facing the wall. He doesn't turn around.
- **Countdown (clock times aloud):** "Ten P.M." (L11), "At six A.M." (L22).
- **The silly plant that becomes the key:** overnight he folds our shirts into perfect little squares (L13) and irons Dad's socks (L14). At the end Dad is folding my shirt into a perfect little square (L33).
- **Plant 1:** the clerk says Room Six's other key never came back (L10). At L23, the closet man sets that key on the desk.
- **Plant 2:** the old card in the drawer (L15, L16): "Room Six: the man in this room is very quiet. He never LEFT." He was the last guest (L17, L18).
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** a card comes true, so I write one: "The man in the closet checked out before breakfast." He does, politely.
- **Reveal and sting (L26 to L33, plain):** under the key, one more card in handwriting we don't know: "Room Six: the father and son were very quiet. They never LEFT." Our car won't start. Dad is folding my shirt into a perfect little square.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** "you get whatever the last guest wrote" was the joke. On the loop, the last guest wrote about us.
- **Ending shape:** `words-make`. Ends eerie. Nobody is hurt, nobody is taken: Dad is folding a shirt.
- **Warmth:** a dad and his grown son on the last night of a road trip, laughing at a bucket.
