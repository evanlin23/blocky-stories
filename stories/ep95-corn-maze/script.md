## The big idea
**The voice telling you this story, the one that tells lost people where to turn, is the corn maze itself, and on the farmer's last night before she moves away, she walks in at lights out on purpose to stay the night with her best friend.**

- **Engine:** the unreliable narrator, "who is speaking" (premise-engines engine 16), our own speaker: a field. No film's or book's narrator, place or line.
- **Why it clicks ("oh, THAT's why"):** "our maze" was the maze talking about itself and June. "I know every turn" and "I always know where everyone is" (L11, L12) are true of a maze. The voice in the corn that gets you out (L05) was the narrator the whole time. "She talks the whole time" (L15) is June talking to it, and "so you can see me off" (L18) is why she cut a face this year. She turns left when the voice says right because she isn't lost. She came to stay.
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the maze keeps her because it won't let her go (possessive, the pitch's own), the narrator is the scarecrow (a "narrator is the X" with a body, too close to the old reveals), the maze is a Keeper standing in a gap (a stand-in), the voice is June's late dad (a sad family reveal), the maze moves its walls to trap visitors (a chase, a live genre beat), the paths spell her name (letters on screen), June is the one who's lost every night (soft), the maze is a giant lying in the field (ep82's disguise lane), the voice is the viewer's own (a gimmick), the maze is a model of the town (a live hit's premise).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not forgotten, not a reflection left behind, not a stand-in, not a disguise, not time. Shape `maze-narrator`: the place is telling the story.
- **The rule is the clue:** "you stay all night" first reads as a threat. After L40, staying is what June came for, and after L51 it's a promise.

## The story
- **Premise:** At our corn maze, if the lanterns go out while you're still inside, you stay the night, and in twenty years nobody has, because a voice in the corn tells lost people where to turn. June owns the farm and cuts new paths every summer. She's sold the farm, and tonight is the last night.
- **Rule / hook (L01, L02, about 3 s):** "If the lanterns go out / while you're in our MAZE, / you STAY." "All NIGHT."
- **The relatable want or fight:** a best friend moving away, and one more night before she goes.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a row of twelve small lantern icons of our own design in the top-left corner, all lit until L22, going dark one by one through L27, none lit from L27 to L48, then twelve lit again at the new maze (L50, L51).
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L04 to L10, 6 to 20 s):** a lost man hearing the voice (L04, L05), the scarecrow and the pumpkin (L06, L07), a man and his dad walking out laughing (L08), a phone waiting on a hay bale (L09), a guy getting lost on purpose (L10).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** a voice in the corn that tells you where to turn (L05, about 8 s). Whose?
- **How the narrator is the exception:** everybody gets out before ten. The voice never does.
- **The loved one at stake:** June, who owns the farm, named at L13 in the same shot as her, and called my best friend at L16.
- **The rule broken:** L20, L27: June walks in at 9:58 and is still inside when the last lantern goes out.
- **First real scare:** L22 to L29 (about 36 to 46 s): the lanterns going out, the voice telling her right and her turning left, the corn leaning in behind her and the path she came by gone.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** "you stay all night" was never a punishment. Nobody stayed because nobody wanted to. June does (L39, L40), so the maze gives her every path she ever cut (L41 to L45). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L31 to L38, 49 to 59 s):** said plainly over one picture (S34, the overhead face): "She's RIGHT." "I'm not a PERSON." "I'm the MAZE." "That voice / that gets you OUT? / That's ME."
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** at dawn the maze opens a path to her truck, she fills her pockets with seed corn for her new yard, and next fall, in some other town, when the lanterns go out, "I'll be the voice / you HEAR." That runs straight into L01: the "you" is any of us, in her new maze.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L11, L12: "I know every TURN in here." "I always know / where EVERYONE is."
  2. The narrator never says "I walked" or "I went". It only says, knows, learned, gave and opened.
  3. L15, L16: she talks the whole time she cuts, with nobody else there, and leaves a second cup in the straw (S16).
  4. L17, S17: this year she cut a face, "so you can see me off", and we only see part of it until L34.
  5. L21: she walks in with a blanket and a thermos, two minutes before lights out.
- **Ending shape:** a goodbye that's also a seed, and a loop.
- **Warmth:** a best friend's goodbye. Twenty summers of one-way talking, one night of talking back, and she takes the maze with her.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. June is moving, happily, and the maze goes with her as seed.
