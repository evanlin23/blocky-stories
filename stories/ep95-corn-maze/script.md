## The big idea
**The voice telling you this story, the one that tells lost people where to turn, is the corn maze itself, and the farmer who has cut its paths for twenty years walks in at lights out on purpose, because staying all night is the only way she'll ever hear it.**

- **Engine:** the unreliable narrator, "who is speaking" (premise-engines engine 16), our own speaker: a field. No film's or book's narrator, place or line.
- **Why it clicks ("oh, THAT's why"):** "our maze" was the maze talking about itself and June. "I know every turn" and "I always know where everyone is" (L11, L12) are true of a maze. The voice in the corn that gets you out (L05) was the narrator the whole time. "She talks the whole time" (L15) is June talking to it, and "so you'd HAVE one" (L18) is why she cut a face this year: she gave the maze a face. "She's never HEARD it" (L19) is why she walks in: she can't get lost in a maze she cut, so the voice has never had a reason to talk to her. She turns left when the voice says right because she isn't lost. She came to stay.
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the maze keeps her because it won't let her go (possessive, the pitch's own), the narrator is the scarecrow (a "narrator is the X" with a body, too close to the old reveals), the maze is a Keeper standing in a gap (a stand-in), the voice is June's late dad (a sad family reveal), the maze moves its walls to trap visitors (a chase, a live genre beat), the paths spell her name (letters on screen), June is the one who's lost every night (soft), the maze is a giant lying in the field (ep82's disguise lane), the voice is the viewer's own (a gimmick), the maze is a model of the town (a live hit's premise).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not forgotten, not a reflection left behind, not a stand-in, not a disguise, not time. Shape `maze-narrator`: the place is telling the story.
- **The rule is the clue:** "you stay all night" first reads as a threat. After L40, staying is what June came for. And a whole night is what the maze needs: one night is long enough to recut every path, so the field that only ever got shapes gives one back (L46 to L49). After L51 the rule is the maze's own invitation.

## The story
- **Premise:** At our corn maze, if the lanterns go out while you're still inside, you stay the night, and in twenty years nobody has, because a voice in the corn tells lost people where to turn. June owns the farm and cuts new paths every summer, so she's never been lost in it, and she's never heard the voice.
- **Rule / hook (L01, L02, about 3 s):** "If the lanterns go out / while you're in our MAZE, / you STAY." "All NIGHT."
- **The relatable want or fight:** wanting to hear, just once, the friend you've talked to for years.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a row of twelve small lantern icons of our own design in the top-left corner, all lit until L22, going dark one by one through L27, none lit from L27 through dawn (L50), then relighting one by one at the next dusk in L51 (soft 0.4 s fades), back to twelve for the loop into S01.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L04 to L10, 6 to 20 s):** a lost man hearing the voice (L04, L05), the scarecrow and the pumpkin (L06, L07), a man and his dad walking out laughing (L08), a phone waiting on a hay bale (L09), a guy getting lost on purpose (L10).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** a voice in the corn that tells you where to turn (L05, about 8 s). Whose?
- **How the narrator is the exception:** everybody gets out before ten. The voice never does.
- **The loved one at stake:** June, who owns the farm, named at L13 in the same shot as her, and called my best friend at L16.
- **The rule broken:** L20, L27: June walks in at 9:58 and is still inside when the last lantern goes out.
- **First real scare:** L22 to L29b (about 37 to 49 s): the lanterns going out, the voice telling her right, then "NOW", and her turning left anyway, the corn closing in behind her, the path she came by gone, then the next path, then the next, until her flashlight hits corn on every side.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** "you stay all night" was never a punishment. Nobody stayed because nobody wanted to. June does (L39, L40), so the maze gives her every path she ever cut (L41 to L45), and because she stays the whole night it has time to recut itself into her face (L46 to L49). It only uses what it already did on screen: closing and opening paths. No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L31 to L38, about 52 to 62 s):** said plainly over one picture (S34, the overhead face): "She's RIGHT." "I don't have FEET." "I'm the MAZE." "That voice / that gets you OUT? / That's ME."
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** every year she gave it a shape, so that night it gave her one. At dawn, from the porch roof, the face in the corn is hers, and when she waves the whole field waves back. Then "And now, I'm the one who tells you the RULE." runs straight into L01: the rule at the top was the maze speaking, to you, and "you STAY" is an offer.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L11, L12: "I know every TURN in here." "I always know / where EVERYONE is."
  2. The narrator never says "I walked" or "I went". It only says, knows, learned, gave and opened.
  3. L15, L16: she talks the whole time she cuts, with nobody else there, and leaves a second cup in the straw (S16).
  4. L17, L18, S17: this year she cut a face, "so you'd HAVE one", and we only see part of it until L34.
  5. L21: she walks in with a blanket and a thermos, two minutes before lights out.
  6. L19: "She's never HEARD it." She's the one person the voice never talks to.
- **Ending shape:** a gift returned (a face for a face), and a loop.
- **Warmth:** a friendship that finally goes both ways. Twenty summers of one-way talking, one night of talking back, and a face cut for her in the dark.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. Nobody leaves. June stays, and laughs.
