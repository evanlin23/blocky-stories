## The big idea
**My neighbor Vern lives his days in reverse, so the day I moved in was his last day on our street, and the thirty years of friendship he thanked me for, crying, are thirty years I'm only about to have. On his first day, thirty years later, I'm the one who knows everything, and I'm the one who says goodbye.**

- **Engine:** the reversed life (engine 3). His days run in reverse order: each one runs forward, but his tomorrow is my yesterday. No film's character, plot or line.
- **Why it clicks:** he waves before I open the door (L09), his mailbox flag goes up before the mail (L08), he thanks me for fixing a fence that isn't broken yet (L16 to L18), he asks about a Nora I haven't met (L19, L20), he cries at my wedding (L21), he gets younger every year (L22 to L24), never remembers yesterday, only tomorrow (L25, L26), gets happier around me as I run out (L27 to L30), every photo of us comes down the day after it happens (L35 to L37), the last one is of his first day (L39, L40), and at the end he doesn't know my name (L41, L42). All of it is a man who remembers my future.

## The story
- **Premise:** The day I moved into my first house, my neighbor Vern, about 85, hugged me on my lawn, cried, and thanked me for thirty years. Vern lives backward. Every year I know him he's a year younger, he never remembers yesterday, only tomorrow, and he's a little happier around me while our days together run out. His porch calendar counts down from 10,950.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 3.4 s):** "Vern's calendar says / almost eleven thousand days LEFT. / He said goodbye / the day we MET."
- **The relatable want or fight:** a friend you know you'll lose, and spending the time anyway.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream flip-calendar tag in the top-left counting Vern's days down: 10,950 in the hook, dropping by years through the montage (9,125, 5,475, 1,825), 7 at L41, 1 at L43 and 0 at L60.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L05 to L15, 10 to 28 s):** his mailbox flag up before the mail (L08), his wave before I open the door (L09), his calendar flipping backward (L10 to L12), his boxes going out to a truck at night and back inside unpacked in the morning (L13 to L15).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** a man who thanks a stranger for thirty years, and a calendar counting down from 10,950 (frame 1 and L10, about 19 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** everyone else meets Vern and lets him go. I'm the one he remembers.
- **The loved one at stake:** Vern, my neighbor, named at L01 in the same shot, and my wife Nora, named at L20.
- **The rule broken:** I ask him what happens at zero (L32), and he won't say.
- **First real scare:** L35 to L42: his walls emptying, every photo of us coming down the day after it happened, the last one left a young man hugging an old man who is me, and Vern waving at me like a stranger.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** if his last day was my first, then his first day is my last, and I know everything he doesn't yet (L49 to L53). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L44 to L53):** said plainly (L49: "For him, / today is the FIRST day.") over one picture (S48: a young Vern, about 26, holding out his hand on his new lawn beside the moving truck, and me at 56 on mine).
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** I hug him on his new lawn, cry, and thank him for thirty years, exactly as he did. He laughs Vern's laugh and says he'll look forward to them. So that's why he cried, which runs into L01.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L03: he thanks me for thirty YEARS on the first day.
  2. L16 to L18: he thanks me for fixing a fence that isn't broken yet.
  3. L19, L20: he asks how Nora is, before I've met her.

- **Vern's living room:** a long wall of framed photos (no text), a rug, a radio, moving boxes stacked by the door.
