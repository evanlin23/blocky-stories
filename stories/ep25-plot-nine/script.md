## The story

- **Premise:** In the driest spring on the hill, I take over old Mr. Carter's allotment plot when he says it's time for somebody new. Every other plot cracks in the sun, but mine is dark and wet at every dawn. Then a head and shoulders of root-wrapped soil rises out of plot one by the gate, and each dawn it's in a bed further down the path, nearer to my beans, leaving a round hole behind it that no cane can find the bottom of.
- **Rule / hook:** no rule is posted. The hook is a smell that can't be there: rain on a plot in a six-week drought. The only spoken rule is Mr. Carter's own "water at dusk, never at noon", which he keeps by coming in the dark.
- **Twist:** Mr. Carter never stopped coming. Every night since he gave me the key, he's let himself in with his old key and his old can to water the plot he couldn't let go of. The Digger rises along the path he walks, a bed nearer each night, following him in to plot nine, because he's still counted there. "It hasn't rained in six weeks" turns over: six weeks is exactly how long since he gave me the key. The smell of rain was his can.
- **Where the twist is planted:**
  - Plant 1, the wet padlock: beaded with water on day one (L09), wetter the earlier I come (L28), dripping on my shoes on Friday (L31).
  - Plant 2, the empty hook in his shed where his watering can should hang (L08). Paid off when he steps out holding it (L43), and echoed when the newcomer's shed has an empty hook too (L66).
  - Rewatch reward, not narrated: under L04, faint wet boot prints come in from the gate down the dusty path and turn into plot nine. Every bed the Digger later rises in sits right beside those prints.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** the first time, "My plot smells like rain" is the creepy thing. By L52 to L53 we know the smell is an old man's can, every night, and the "six weeks" is the time since he handed over the key. In the last 15 s (L64 to L70) the plot is full, I hand my own can on to the next newcomer, and "It always WILL" makes the first line mean that someone will always be looking after it. A second watch hears L01 as Mr. Carter's love for the place, and catches the padlock and the empty hook.
- **Ending shape:** a watering can passed from my hand toward the newcomer's sleeve at sunrise, cut away before any grip, then Mr. Carter's nod from his bucket, then a still wide of plot nine in gold light.
- **Warmth:** passing it on. He teaches me the plot, then I start teaching the next one.

## World

Allotment gardens on a gentle hillside at dawn, in a dry spring. A wooden five-bar gate at the top with a chain and an old iron padlock. One grass-and-dust path runs straight downhill from the gate. Ten plots sit along it, odd numbers on the left and even on the right, each a rectangle of soil with low board edges, a plain wooden stake at the path corner and, on most, a small tarred shed. Every bed but mine is pale cracked clay, crusted, with brown wilting leaves. Plot nine is near the bottom on the left: dark wet soil, a double row of bean canes tied in wigwams, and Mr. Carter's creosote-brown shed at the plot's front corner by the path. Beyond the bottom fence the hill drops away into a valley that fills with mist.

- **No text anywhere.** The plots aren't numbered on screen: the stakes are plain wood, the sheds have no names, the seed packets in the shed are plain paper with drawn pods. The captions carry the numbers.
- **What's true underneath (never said):** the allotments were built for a count of plot-holders. When Mr. Carter handed over his key, the count went short by one, but he kept coming back, so he's still counted here. The Digger is this place's Keeper. Each night it rises in his footsteps, standing in the gap he left, a bed nearer along the path he walks, until it stands in plot nine itself. When I tell him to keep his key, he's a plot-holder again, the count is whole, and it sinks back down. It never wanted me or him. It only counts.
