## The big idea

**The faceless thing of soil and roots climbing out of the allotments, one plot nearer Mum each dawn, is Mum: every root she ever put down on this hill, coming up in her shape, because she's leaving and couldn't tell me.**

The rule ("you grow roots where you belong") is the idea seen from inside. In the reveal (L29 to L35) every strange detail clicks at once:
- It rises in plots one, four and seven, then nine, because her roots run up the path under every plot she's ever had (L08 to L09, shown at about 15 s).
- It rises higher each dawn, and her roots get shorter each dawn (L22), because they're the same roots.
- It always faces the gate (L16), the way off the hill.
- Its head turns round to look back at her, because part of her doesn't want to go.
- The pebble never lands (L20), because her roots went that deep.
- She gives away her bucket and flask, and her shed is bare (L11, L12), because she's packing.
- She "just goes quiet" (L17) instead of being scared, because she knows what it is.

The "oh no" is the narrator's: he belongs nowhere except across the path from her (L10), so once she goes he'll be "across the path from NOTHING" (L36). The rule's own montage holds the way out: nobody here moves house, they get repotted (L03).

### Ten pitches, one kept
1. **Kept: roots where you belong, and the creature is Mum uprooting herself.** Above.
2. Whatever you're scared of grows in your plot, and the thing growing toward Mum is my fear of losing her. The fear-made-flesh idea is worn out, and its montage is objects sprouting again, the killed version's look.
3. The plants grow toward whoever needs them most. The leaning plants copy ep36's leaning flame.
4. The gardeners are the hill's crop, and the Digger is its gardener, come to pick the ripest. A real "wait, what", but cruel, with no warm way out that isn't new magic.
5. Things nobody loves anymore get up and walk off down the hill ("Nobody weeds here. The weeds just LEAVE."). A great montage that never found a person to love. Kept for the backlog as a fresh idea.
6. Everyone on the hill sleeps planted in their own bed and comes up at dawn. A lovely world, but the faceless one that never wakes reads as a coma, the owner's own example.
7. Whatever you give up on comes up in your plot. Objects sprouting from beds again, too close to the killed version.
8. You can only eat what someone else grew for you. Sweet, with no "oh no".
9. Under every gardener, someone stands upside down in the soil, feet to feet. Eerie, with nothing to show above ground.
10. The plots tell the time, and the Digger is the hand of a hill-sized clock counting down to something. Clever, and nobody to feel for.

## The story

- **Premise:** On an allotment hill where everyone grows roots into the ground where they belong, I'm the only gardener whose boots come up clean. Mum has plot nine across the path from mine, and her roots are the longest on the hill, running up the path under every plot she's ever had. This week she gives me her bucket and her flask. Then a head and shoulders of soil and roots with no face comes up in plot one by the gate, facing the gate, and every dawn it's further down the path toward her, higher out of the ground, while her roots get shorter.
- **The rule (line 1):** "On this hill, you grow roots where you belong." 9 words.
- **The marker by 2 s:** thick dark roots fanning out from every gardener's boots across the pale clay. In the first frame: old Mr Pell (the reused `carter` model) standing in his bed, cap on, smiling, face lit and at least 1/5 of the frame width, his roots spreading from his wellies like a tree's. ## World

The same allotment hillside at dawn in a dry spring. A wooden five-bar gate at the top with its chain and padlock (seen, never mentioned). One dusty path runs straight downhill. Ten plots, odd numbers on the left and even on the right, each with low board edges and a plain wooden stake at the path corner. Every bed but plot nine is pale cracked clay (the look, not narrated). Plot nine near the bottom on the left is dark worked soil with two rows of bean wigwams and Mum's creosote-brown shed at its path corner. Plot ten, across the path from it, is mine: neat, bare, raked. Beyond the bottom fence the hill drops into a misty valley.

- **No text anywhere.** Stakes are plain wood, no numbers. The captions carry the plot numbers.
- **What's true underneath (never said):** on this hill, people grow roots into the ground where they belong, and the longer they belong somewhere, the further the roots run. When someone means to leave, their roots come up out of the hill, oldest first, and gathered together they take that person's shape, without a face, facing the way out. If the roots are torn out whole, the person leaves with nothing of the hill. If they go with some of the hill's soil (repotting), the roots go with them and the rest settles back down. Objects left long enough root too. It's simply how this hill is. No Keeper is in this story.
