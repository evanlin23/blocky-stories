# ep25-plot-nine, round 5: standards

Verdict: kill

The premise and twist are new for this story but copy `floor-thirteen`, already on main ("In This Building Everything You FORGET Goes To Floor 13"). Line edits can't fix that. The set, the Digger and the cast can carry a different idea.

## Must-fix

1. **Originality (hard rule 5), whole story.** The two stories line up beat for beat. Forgotten things reappear in one place and get fetched back (L01 to L05, with keys in both montages). The narrator never forgets (L06, L07: "I don't FORGET things" against "I've never forgotten anything"). Grandad is forgetting a loved one, and that person is the creature. A scarf is the clue. Finding it brings the name back (L34 to L38: "he says her NAME" against "he says my name"). The forgetting comes back again (L41 to L43). The package's `forgotten` shape is floor-thirteen's `lost-property`. The title has the same shape too: "On This X Whatever You FORGET Comes Back Up". The batch also has ep31-dawn-flight-redo ("if your family forgets you") and ep52-ferry-crossing (lost things go home, Gran). Pick a big idea that isn't about forgetting. `check` misses this because the branch is behind origin/main. Merge main before the next check.
2. **Hard rule 2, L29 (S38, about 50.3 to 51.6 s, "round its HEAD," / "KNOTTED").** Close up, the knot is pale, jointed, segmented root ends that read as skeletal finger bones. With "It's GRAN", "still hangs" (L10), "like Gran's still THERE" (L22) and a figure climbing out of the soil, the reveal reads as a buried body rising. Any reuse of the Digger needs dark, tapering roots with no finger-like joints, tied like a cloth knot.

## Passes

Fiction (no text in the set). IP: the faces are our own. Flash preflight PASS. The `voice.py` homophones only change the ASR comparison, and no other story on main uses gran or grand. Package fields and rule 8 are fine.
