## The big idea
**The number over your head counts the days since you made somebody else laugh, though the whole town reads it as days since you laughed, and my funny aunt Bev is at 492 days and going gray because I stopped laughing at her jokes, while my 0 is every time I fall down in front of her.**

- **Engine:** the double reading (engine 10). The town reads its own rule wrong. No film's line or gag.
- **Why it clicks:** Earl never smiles and reads 0 (everyone laughs at Earl, L05, L06), the man in lane four laughs at all his own jokes and reads 9,000 (L07, L08), Bev howls at her own joke and nothing moves (L16, L17), and I "mostly fall down" (L12).
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the number is how long since someone laughed at you meanly (a bully beat), the number counts how many laughs you have left (a death clock), the gray people are Keepers (a count), Bev's number is mine swapped (a muddle), the number is set by the oldest person who loves you (ep110's shape this batch), it counts days since you cried (sad lane), Bev is faking her laugh (no turn), the bowling alley takes the laughs (a monster), the narrator is gray and doesn't know (the forgotten theme), the 0 is a broken counter (no idea).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not ep43's "how many people know your name" (it counts a laugh given, not a name), not the forgotten theme, not a stand-in. Shape `misread-count`.
- **The rule is the clue:** "how many days since you laughed" is what the town believes. After L24 the same number means "since you made somebody else laugh", and every number we've seen flips.

## The story
- **Premise:** In this town a number floats over everyone's head, and everyone says it counts the days since you last laughed. Past 500, your color starts to go. My aunt Bev, the funniest person in our family, laughs all night at league night, and hers says 492.
- **Hook / rule (L01, about 2.6 s, then L02):** "Aunt Bev laughs / ALL night. / Hers says FOUR-NINETY-TWO." L02: "In this town, / the number over your head / is how many DAYS / since you laughed." (r1: swapped so line 1 is the wrong count, its shape `count`, and the rule lands by about 6 s.)
- **The relatable want or fight:** an aunt whose jokes you outgrew, and wanting to make someone you love laugh.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** the number floating over every head, in chunky white numerals with a soft colored glow (Bev's coral, mine teal). A small cream tag top-left mirrors Bev's number and a little gray bar fills toward 500.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L10, 4 to 20 s):** Earl at shoe rental, never smiles, reads 0. The man in lane four, laughs at his own jokes, reads 9,000 and is totally gray. Past 500, your color goes.
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** the numbers don't fit the rule (L04 to L08).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** mine has said 0 my whole life, and I don't even laugh much (L11, L12).
- **The loved one at stake:** my aunt Bev, named "My aunt Bev" at L13 with her in frame telling a joke.
- **The scare (L18, L35, L36):** her sleeves going gray, then gray to her shoulders at 499, the night before 500.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** a fake laugh doesn't count (L31). It has to be real, so she tells me every joke she has, and what finally gets me is the thing that always gets her: someone falling flat on their back (L39, L40). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L23 to L29, about 38 to 52 s):** said plainly (L24) over one picture (S24: my number flickering from 1 back to 0 over my head as I lie on the lane, and Bev doubled over above me, her own number not moving).
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** her counter rolls to 0 as the pins fall. Then Bev buys me bowling shoes that fit, I haven't fallen in three days, and mine says three. I'm working on material, which runs straight into L01.

- **Ending shape:** a misread rule read right, then a loop that flips it onto me.
