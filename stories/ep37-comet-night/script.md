# ep37-comet-night

**Slug:** `ep37-comet-night` (pitch #21, `stories/ideas.md` → Batch 4 pitches)

## Premise
In my town, every promise you make ties a small glowing knot onto a cord that floats over your head, and it stays until you keep the promise. I'm the only one with no knots, because I don't make promises. My dad has the oldest knot in town, grey and hard, ten years old, and I always took it for a promise he broke to me the year he moved out. This August Mum says it has started pulling tight and he's sleeping in his observatory on the hill, so in shooting-star week I go up for the first time since he left. Every night his knot creaks a turn tighter, and a man with a square hole through his chest stands a little nearer the empty second chair by the eyepiece.

## Rule
The world's rule (L01): every promise ties a knot over your head, and a kept promise slips loose in a puff of light (L06). Nobody explains it after L07. The house rule I live by: I don't make promises (L09). I break it at the very end (L67).

## Twist
The grey knot was never a broken promise. It was the one promise Dad kept every single year: "Every August, I'll save you a seat" (L56). He kept that chair clean for ten years, and a saved seat can't count as kept until somebody sits in it, so the knot could only come loose with me in the chair. The first line, "every promise you make ties a knot over your head", opens as a weight people carry for what they owe. After L56 to L62 it reads as the opposite: the oldest knot in town was the longest-kept promise in town. The last line ("I've got ONE now") turns the exception around: the one person with no knots makes a promise of their own.
- **Plant 1 (S12 and S13 under L10 and L11, S19 under L17, paid off in S71 under L62):** Dad's cord has a second loose end that trails out of frame. In the hill wide (S19) a faint grey line runs from the dome down the slope toward my front door. When the knot slips free (S71), the loose end lies across the floor to the second chair, under me. Never narrated.
- **Plant 2 (S23 under L20, S24 under L21):** the second chair's leather seat is clean, while the stool beside it, the chart table and the pier all wear a grey film of dust. L21 says "dusty stool", so a rewatch sees the chair is the only clean thing in the dome. Paid off by L57.
- Bonus (L13): "Something he promised me." It was a promise to me, just the other kind.

## World
One small hill town in August. Day: a sunny cobbled square of cream stone, a pale stone civic building with broad steps and a round blank medallion over its door (no lettering), bunting in plain triangles (no print), a café's tables under a striped awning, low terracotta roofs, the hill rising behind with the dome on top. Night: a stone path up the hill through dry grass, my small house with one lit window at the bottom. On top, the observatory: a round stone drum with a verdigris-green copper dome, the shutter slit open to a made-up starfield (random dots, no real constellations, no named comet). Inside: whitewashed timber wall boards, a plank floor, a long brass telescope on a black iron pier, two wooden chairs with oxblood leather seats side by side by the eyepiece (Dad's worn, the second clean), a dusty three-legged stool by the wall, a slanted chart table with pinned star charts of our own dots and glyphs (no words), four warm bulbs in brass cages on the wall, and one red working lamp low on the pier. The dome door is an arched plank door at floor level. Nobody goes near the dome's edge or the slit: everyone stays on the floor, and the slit is only ever seen from below.

## The last 15 seconds turn the first line
"In this town, every promise you make ties a knot over your head" opens on a square full of debts: the call never made, the mayor's scarf, Dad's grey knot that I took for the promise he broke. L56 to L62 show the grey knot was the one promise kept every August for ten years, waiting for me to sit down. The loop drops back to the square, and on a second watch every bobbing knot reads as someone still keeping a promise, the first line sounds hopeful, and the narrator with no knots is the sad one.
