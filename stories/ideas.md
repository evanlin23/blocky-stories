## Batch 5 pitches (ep46 to ep55, 2026-11-02)

Ten separate sessions each run `/week 1` on one pitch, so every pitch below stands on its own. Take only your own row and notes. The scriptwriter may change details, but keeps the row's rule type, setting, creature, loved one, kind of warmth and marker form. This batch adds the creepy-lane lessons (`docs/research/2026-11-02-creepy-lane-notes.md` → Answers) on top of batch 4's shape. RULES.md and the owner's notes still win where they differ.

Same in every pitch:
- **The shape:** the rule opener as the first line, its marker on or with every character by 2 s, a 3 to 5 example montage in the first 15 s, the narrator as the exception, an adult or teen loved one at stake, the rule shown at work from 15 to 30 s with nobody explaining it, a loophole or second-reading climax (never new magic), the twist planted twice, a warm turn shown and not told, and a last line that echoes the first.
- **Open odd, not frightening.** The first 2 s are bright and readable, faces in frame, and a little funny. Darkness and fog come after the hook.
- **Hold the scare.** The first real scare lands at about 35 to 55 s, and it's the first person "taken": gone, with an object left behind, never shown hurt. It happens off-screen, the person is away for an ordinary reason the twist explains, nothing says a death word, and they're back by the end. A Keeper never takes or touches anyone.
- **The creature is a Keeper:** one blocky person wrong in one dimension, standing in a place someone should fill. Traces come first (an object moved, a sound, a light fading, a chair turned). It's shown clearly only at about 55 to 70% of the runtime (roughly 80 to 100 s), then in full, well lit, slow: bending, tilting, leaning in close. It never chases or lunges, it says nothing, it carries none of the town's markers, and it's gone once a person fills its place.
- **The template the viewers are tired of:** the narrator is never new in town, there's no mayor in any pitch, nobody is summoned to a square at midnight, and the town never turns on the narrator in this batch.
- **Endings:** every pitch keeps its warm turn (owner's call), but the last line lands cold: it loops back to the first line and ends on a small eerie sting. The darker stings got the most theory comments.
- **Sound:** no silent gaps. A thin, light bed for the first third, then a sustained low drone that enters at the first real scare and holds to the end. Keep `music_drops` short (0.5 to 0.8 s) with the voice and the hit kept.
- **Lights** go down and come back as slow fades (1 s or more), never flicker or strobe. Any marker that changes (a glow, a colour, a bead) changes as a slow fade too.
- **Markers** are our own design. Nothing in the set carries real words or places.
- **Packaging:** the title states the rule ("In this town, ..."), as a statement or a warning (RULES, 2026-11-03) and never spoiling the twist. The pin is a question the viewer answers about themselves.
- **The lore plant** is one plain tally (lore.md → Open threads → Fifth parallel batch), shown once, held, never narrated.
