## 1. `floor-thirteen`: "Our building doesn't have a thirteenth floor"

- **First line, a sound:** "DING. / Floor THIRTEEN. / Our building / doesn't HAVE one."
- **Cast:** me, an adult who moved into the tower this year, and Mr. Okafor from 12B.
  - Mr. Okafor is a grumpy neighbour who never says hello. He knows nothing more than I do. He's scared too, and he isn't a guide.
- **The creature, the Attendant:**
  - A block figure in a brass-and-black lift operator's uniform. Where the face should be, its head is a square panel of round lift buttons. They light up one at a time, the way eyes would.
  - Its arms are too long, with white-gloved hands always folded. It stands too close to the doors.
  - It is never seen moving. It's only in a new place after each DING.
  - Its colours are brass, black and a cold button glow. Not pale grey.
- **Beats:**
  1. The lift stops at 13, then stops there again on later days, each time on a different trip.
  2. The doors open on a hallway carpeted like ours, but every door is shut and numbered 13.
  3. The Attendant is one door closer each time.
  4. One morning Mr. Okafor and I end up in the lift together, and it takes us both to 13.
  5. The doors won't close.
- **The scare:** the lights go out, and when they come back the Attendant is inside the lift with us. All its buttons are lit, pointing at us.
- **The twist (plant it early):** the lift only goes to 13 when someone rides alone and doesn't talk.
  - Plant 1: Mr. Okafor always takes the stairs.
  - Plant 2: I always wear headphones.
- **The way out:** we get out by talking. He tells me about his late wife's garden. Keep it gentle, and never say "died" or "dead" (`checks` blocks them). I tell him I don't know anyone in the city.
  - Each time one of us speaks, one of the Attendant's buttons goes dark. When the last one goes out, the doors close.
- **The warmth, two strangers:**
  - We ride down together every morning now.
  - He brings tomatoes from a pot on his balcony.
  - The creature stays unexplained.
- **The last line, looping to the first:** "The button for THIRTEEN / is still there. / We never ride / ALONE." Then cut to the DING.
- **Risks:**
  - The lift lights flickering. Do the flash pre-check on the lights-out beat.
  - Mr. Okafor is an adult. Nothing about him reads as a stereotype.
  - "Late wife" is fine. Never say "dead" or "died".

## 2. `mirror-maze`: "Me and Tasha went into the mirror maze. Only one of us walked out"

- **First line, an inside joke:** "Tasha and I / have a RULE. / Whoever says / 'PICKLE' first / pays for FRIES."
  - The joke is the proof of who's real later.
- **Cast:** me and Tasha, best friends since forever, both young adults (`age` "adult", so nothing near a teen in peril).
- **Setting:** the last weekend of a travelling fair, rainy and half packed up. The mirror maze is the only ride still lit.
- **The creature, the copy:**
  - Tasha, exactly her rig, in her yellow raincoat. But her eyes are mirror-black with no highlight, and she always moves half a beat after I do, like a reflection that's late.
  - In the mirrors there are too many Tashas, and one of them is facing the wrong way.
  - It is the only "creature", and it's the same rig in every shot. It isn't pale grey: it's Tasha, wrong.
- **Beats:**
  1. We split up inside the maze for a laugh.
  2. Tasha comes out.
  3. On the walk home she's off: she doesn't laugh at the right moment, and she copies my words a second after me.
  4. I say "PICKLE". She doesn't react, and she doesn't make me pay.
  5. Her reflection in a puddle is a step behind her.
- **The scare:** at the bus stop I look at her reflection in the shelter glass. It's knocking on the inside of the glass, mouthing my name.
  - The knocking is a light knock with a hand, never "pressed flat" (README: a batch 1 echo).
- **The twist (plant it early):** the copy was the one beside me on the way out. The real Tasha is still in the maze.
  - Plant 1: the maze attendant's sign, written in made-up words, says something like "ALL GUESTS MUST EXIT TOGETHER".
  - Plant 2: when we split up, I heard her laugh twice.
- **The warmth, loyalty:**
  - I go back into the maze as the fair packs up, through the mirrors, shouting "PICKLE" until one reflection laughs.
  - I grab that hand.
  - Outside, the real Tasha, soaked, says "you owe me fries."
- **The last line:** "She PAID. / First time EVER." Then loop to "Tasha and I have a RULE."
- **Risks:**
  - **Flashing:** a mirror maze has bright reflections and fast cuts. Do the flash pre-check on every mirror shot at the final's own frame steps. Don't use strobe fair lights.
  - **Harm:** both are adults. The copy never hurts Tasha and nothing describes harm to her. She's "still in there", not taken.
  - **No real fair brands.** The sign uses only made-up words.

## 3. `lookout`: "Something was walking through the forest, and it was taller than the trees"

- **First line, a question:** "Have you EVER / heard a FOREST / go QUIET / all at ONCE?"
- **Cast:** me alone, an adult volunteer in a fire-lookout tower for one autumn. My only contact is a two-way radio to the ranger station.
  - The ranger only says short things like "copy" and "all clear". She isn't a knowing elder and explains nothing.
- **The creature, the Walker:**
  - A giant blocky figure, as tall as the trees and wide, never thin: built of stacked logs, bark and moss, with stones for shoulders.
  - Its face is a hollow in the wood with two small warm lights deep inside, like lanterns.
  - It moves only when the fog is thick. Through binoculars it's one ridge closer each time.
  - Its colours are moss green and dark bark brown.
  - **Never** thin, never in a suit, never between trees at arm's length. That reads as the Slender Man trope, which standards bans.
- **Beats:**
  1. Birds stop.
  2. A line of broken treetops on the far ridge.
  3. The radio picks up something like breathing through static.
  4. Huge footprints in the mud at the tower's base, filled with water.
  5. One night its lantern eyes are level with my window.
- **The scare:** I'm on the catwalk in the fog, and the tower creaks as something leans against it.
- **The twist (plant it early):** it's lost. The fog and smoke from a far-off controlled burn hide the valley it's trying to reach, and it's following the only steady light it can see: my tower's lamp.
  - Plant 1: the ranger says "controlled burn, east side, all week".
  - Plant 2: the footprints all point the same way, and it isn't toward me.
- **The warmth, I help:**
  - I can't speak to it. But I can move the light.
  - I carry the lamp down the stairs and walk it along the fire road toward the valley, one slow step at a time, with the Walker following.
  - At the valley's edge the fog lifts, and it walks on alone.
- **The last line:** "Next SPRING, / a line of tiny TREES / came UP the road. / All the WAY / to my DOOR." Then loop to the quiet forest.
- **Risks:**
  - The controlled burn is distant smoke only, with no fire shown.
  - No real national park or forest names, and no text on the radio.
  - The giant's scale has to read at phone size.

## 4. `moving-day`: "The box was taped shut from the inside"

- **First line, mid-scare:** "The box / was taped SHUT. / From the INSIDE."
- **Cast:** me, an adult, and my mum. We're moving into a smaller house after the old one was sold.
  - Mum is tired and hasn't laughed in months. She's no guide; she thinks I'm tired too.
- **The creature, the Boxes:**
  - A tall figure built entirely of taped brown moving boxes, with a black marker face drawn on its front box.
  - **The face is different every time we see it.** It's always the same box and the same marker line weight, but the drawing changes while nobody's looking: first a frown, then a stare, then far too many eyes.
  - It's never seen moving. It's in a different room each morning, standing very still among the other boxes.
  - Its colours are cardboard brown, black marker and packing-tape shine.
- **Beats:**
  1. Things are unpacked in the night, into the wrong rooms.
  2. Scraping sounds, like cardboard dragged across the floor.
  3. Marker drawings appear on other boxes.
  4. Mum's mug is on the windowsill every morning.
  5. The box figure is in the hallway one morning, much taller than any stack we made.
- **The scare:** at 3 a.m. I find it standing over Mum's bed. Its marker face is drawn wide open, and its hands are reaching.
- **The twist (plant it early):** it isn't unpacking into the wrong rooms. It's putting everything where it lived in the **old** house: the mug on the windowsill because that's where the old kitchen window was, and the photos at the old height. It's built from our old house's boxes.
  - Plant 1: "The boxes all smell like the OLD hall."
  - Plant 2: Mum: "That's where I always kept it."
  - At 3 a.m. its hands were tucking the old quilt over her, the way the old house's draughty room needed.
- **The warmth, home:**
  - Mum sees the arrangement, recognises the old house in it, and laughs for the first time since the sale.
  - We unpack the rest together, the old way.
  - The box figure is flattened, folded and kept in the hall cupboard. Its last drawn face is a smile.
- **The last line:** "We kept ONE box. / It's taped shut / from the INSIDE. / We don't OPEN it." Then loop to the first line.
- **Risks:**
  - **The bed scare:** standards checks that nothing reads as harm to Mum. The reveal of the tucking has to be clear.
  - **Box text:** brand names or real moving-company names on the boxes. Only plain boxes and marker drawings, no words.
  - **The scraping sound:** it must not be a sample. Everything is synthesized.

Notes for `model-village`: nobody in town talks to me, and I spend my days leaning over the little streets with a brush. Each day the Resident is in a window one street further on, and I read it as creeping toward something. The scare: I lean in to the model café and its thumb-sized face is right behind the glass, head turned too far round to look at me. The twist is perspective. One shot from inside the model shows what it sees every morning: my giant face filling its window. It was never coming at me. It was moving away from me, house by house, toward the café where all the tiny townsfolk sit. Plant 1: in an early wide shot every tiny figure in the village has its back to the side where I stand. Plant 2: it's always one window further from wherever I last leaned in. The warmth: I stop peering in and walk into the real café. The owner shows me that every resident gets a figure and hands me a blank one to paint as myself. Ending shape: the townsfolk lean over the model together while I set my figure in the café window, seen from inside, looking up at kind giant faces. Notes for `only-line`: I'm an adult with stage fright who joined the company to meet people. I have one line in the last scene, where the old king asks who stands with him and each player answers from their tape mark. Every run-through I freeze, and the line comes from the wings in my own voice. The Prompter is one curtain leg nearer each time. The scare: at the dress rehearsal I freeze, the lights cut, and when they come back the Prompter is standing on my mark, its huge mask turned to me, saying my line. The twist: the scene is a roll call and a mark can't be left empty, so each time I froze it answered for me. "I've never said it" turns from shame into the reason it was there. Plant 1: on day one the director laughs that the company can't afford a prompter. Plant 2: the first time the line comes from the wings, nobody on stage turns round. The warmth is courage: opening night, I say it, shaking, and the wings are empty. Ending shape: one line into a held silence, cut to black on the first clap. No bow and no audience reversal (that was ep13). Last line loops to "I'm HERE, my LORD." Lore plant (the "one stroke too many" thread): a chalk tally on the back wall of the wings, one stroke per player, eight strokes for a cast of seven. Shown in beat 2, not narrated, and never tied to ep20 on screen.

Notes for `wash-cycle`: the first freezing night I come in soaked, a dryer nobody started finishes, and inside is a warm coat my size. I wear it all week.
