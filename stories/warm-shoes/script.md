## The big idea (one sentence)

The stranger who walks one streetlight behind Dad every night is our cat, standing up tall to walk him home, and the warm shoes on the mat are what it steps out of at our gate when it gets small again.

Why it's the one kept (of ten pitched: the cat copies tomorrow's shoes, the cat lives eight other lives, a thing outside trying on our sizes to become one of us, the shoes walk you where their owner went, the cat swaps bodies with whoever wears them, the shoes are its old families', and three more): it turns the cat from thief to bodyguard, Dad from the cat's enemy to the one it loves, and the creepy stranger into the hero, all at once, and every strange detail (warm, a new pair every night, nobody ever comes for them, it always stops at our gate, the cat comes in thirty seconds after Dad) clicks in one picture. It isn't `stand-in`, it isn't forgotten or unseen, and nobody is sad at the end.

## The story

- **Premise:** Every night our ginger cat, Biscuit, carries a pair of old shoes in through the cat flap, every one a size eleven, Dad's size. They're always still warm, there's always a little of somewhere on them (sawdust, sand), and nobody in town ever comes for them. Dad lines them up on the porch rail for their owners, calls Biscuit a thief and says he goes on Saturday. And for a week, someone too tall has walked one streetlight behind Dad on his walk home from the bus at sundown, stopping at our gate.
- **The want (relatable):** keeping my cat when Dad wants him gone.
- **The rule (line 1):** "Never keep the shoes our cat brings home. They're still warm." A warning to you, from me.
- **Twist:** the someone behind Dad was Biscuit every night. When Dad drives him to a farm past the lake, the stranger still comes up the road at sundown, in muddy rubber farm boots with hay on them, and at the gate it gets smaller and Biscuit climbs out of the left boot. It was walking Dad home. The shoes were never stolen: they're old pairs from people's trash, which is why nobody comes for them, and he picks the size of whoever he walks home. He needs a pair outside to stand up in. The rail is outside, so it never counted. A pair kept inside means somebody walks home alone, which is why he came to my window for the sneakers I kept. Friday's pair was my size because Friday he walked me home (the squeak behind me, L33a).
- **Where the twist is planted:**
  - Plant 1, L19 and L27: it always stops at our gate, and the cat flap goes thirty seconds after Dad comes in. Every night.
  - Plant 2, L37 to L44: the thing at my window looks at the sneakers I kept, then at me, and gives me the slow blink (what cats do to people they love). In the morning the sneakers are gone and Biscuit is asleep right where they were, 
  - Plant 3 (the logic the climax uses), L03 and L04: every pair carries a little of where it's been (sawdust, sand). So muddy farm boots with hay can only mean the farm.
  - Plant 4 (the warmth), L11: Biscuit's bowl is full by five every morning. Paid off at L69: it's Dad.
  - Plant 5 (the size), L10a and L33a to L33c: every pair is Dad's size eleven, until the night something squeaks behind me on the way home from Ben's and the pair is mine. Paid off at L66b and L66c. Line 2 ("Even if they're YOUR SIZE.") is that pair.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** first time, "Never keep the shoes our cat brings home. They're still warm" is a creepy warning about stolen shoes whose owner comes looking. By the end, they're warm because our cat was just standing in them, and you don't keep them because a kept pair means somebody walks home alone: Dad now leaves his old work boots and my old sneakers on the gatepost every night. The last line ("This morning, my old SNEAKERS were back on the MAT.") runs straight into line 1, which now means give them back.
- **The sting (cold last beat):** I walk home late, and one streetlight behind me is someone way too tall in my old sneakers. I didn't look back.
- **Ending shape:** a loop through an object. My old sneakers on the same mat, steaming, cutting straight to frame 1's pair on the mat.
- **Warmth:** loyalty from the one you push away (the cat loves Dad, who says he hates it), and Dad and me on the same side. Shown: Dad picking Biscuit up mud and all, Dad caught filling the bowl, Dad's boots and my sneakers on the gatepost.
- **Shape tag:** `same-one` (the threat and the protector are the same one). New tag.
- **Sad family reveal:** none. Nobody is gone, hurt or grieving. Dad is fine all story.

## World

One place, like back-row: our house and the long road up to it, in a small made-up town at the end of summer. A single-storey house with mint-green siding, a white wraparound porch with a long white rail, a screen door with a cat flap in the wooden front door behind it, a coir doormat, a white picket gate between two square white gateposts, a mailbox on a post (no lettering). The front window of my room looks over the porch, the gate and the road. The road runs straight from the bus stop shelter at the far end, past a field of tall gold grass, under a line of eight streetlights, to our gate.

- **No text anywhere.** The bus shelter has our own glyph disc (a circle over two bars). The mailbox is plain. No house numbers.
- **The rule meter (on screen the whole time):** the pairs. A neat row of strangers' shoes along the porch rail (each pair side by side, toes out), and a chalk number on the porch post by the door that I update, digits only. Every shot carries either the rail row, the chalk number, or a pair of the warm shoes in frame. Road shots are framed from my window or the porch with the rail and its row in the foreground. Count: 8 at the start, 9, 10, 11 through the montage week, 12 after Thursday's shiny shoes, still 12 on Friday (I keep that pair, the chalk doesn't change, a rewatch catch), 0 at L49. From L71, the rail is empty and the meter is the two pairs on the gatepost by night (Dad's boots, my sneakers), and the pair on the mat by morning.
- **"Still warm":** every warm pair gives off a soft curl of steam for its first shot on the mat. Steam is particles, slow, never a light change.
- **What's true underneath (never said):** Biscuit can stand up as a tall person at night. The tall shape is Biscuit's own, never a Keeper. To stand up it needs a pair of shoes left out in the open, so it takes old pairs from people's trash, in the size of whoever it's walking home, carrying a little of that place on them. At our gate it gets small again and the pair is left behind, warm, and it carries them in because cats bring you things. Pairs on the outside rail are still within its reach. A pair shut indoors isn't. Once Dad leaves his boots and my sneakers on the gatepost, it wears those.
