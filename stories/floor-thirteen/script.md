# ep14-floor-thirteen: the script (redo r4, writer, 2026-11-02, and a new opening r5, 2026-11-03)

**The big idea:** in a building where everything you forget turns up on the thirteenth floor, the elevator starts taking me there on its own, and the thing that's been forgotten is me: Grandad doesn't know who I am anymore.

The test, said out loud: "Everything you forget ends up on floor thirteen, and the elevator keeps taking him there, because his grandad has forgotten him." That gets an "oh no". It isn't a stand-in: nothing keeps my place. The rule acts on me.

## The new opening (r5, 2026-11-03: the opening recipe v1, `docs/research/playbook.md`)

The old opening (P01's DING on bright thirteen, then "In this BUILDING, everything you FORGET turns up on floor THIRTEEN.") stated a wonder rule. The recipe wants a warning to "you" about the thing that's actually dangerous here. Riding up to thirteen isn't dangerous: everyone does it to fetch things. The danger is the elevator taking you there **on its own**, to a dark thirteen, which means you're the thing that got forgotten. So the warning is about the 13 button lighting by itself, and the order is to stay in the car. The climax is me breaking it on purpose (L42), and L02's "Even if your KEYS are up there" rhymes with it: what's up there is me.

- **L01 (warning, 11 words, about 2.1 s):** "If the / THIRTEEN button / lights up / by ITSELF, / don't step OUT."
- **L02 (tightens it, 7 words, about 1.3 s):** "Even if / your KEYS / are up there." The "even if" is back-row's "even if it's the only seat left": the one reason you'd want to step out.
- **L02b (the rule, 8 words, about 1.8 s, done by about 5.5 s):** "Everything you FORGET / turns up / on floor THIRTEEN." This is the old L01 without "In this building", and it replaces the old L02 ("Lost your KEYS? THIRTEEN."), whose keys now sit in L02. No name, place or backstory before 4 s: Mr. Abel is first named at L03.
- **P01 is cut.** The warning starts at 0 s. The DING moves onto L01's last word.
- **First shot (0 s to L01's "don't step OUT"):** inside the car, the floor panel close and lit by the car light (camera about 0.7 m from the panel, 32 mm pushing to 45 mm while moving 0.2 m closer, centred for TV). The **13 button is lit, cold, and nobody pressed it**: my finger hovers by the 12, not touching anything. That's the marker and the thing the warning names, wrong from frame one. No wide in the first 3 s.
- **Second shot (on "don't step OUT"):** my face in the car (`FACE_ME`, 24 mm, about 1.5 m), worried, looking at the panel. **The sound hit lands on "OUT":** the elevator bell plus a low hit. Then L02 on bright thirteen's key board (22 mm, about 2 m) as Mr. Abel lifts his set off, and L02b over the doors parting on bright thirteen (`FIRST`, 22 mm, about 3 m), then the tagged shelf.
- **The loop:** L50 "...and FETCH myself / BACK." cuts to the lit 13 button and "If the THIRTEEN button lights up by ITSELF..." It plays as one sentence: I go back up, the button lights, and I'm the one stepping out anyway.
- **The plant this adds:** at L13 ("I pressed TWELVE") the 13 lights up by itself beside my finger as it lifts off the 12 (a panel cue at L13+0.7).

## Premise, rule, twist (for story.json, the builder copies these over)

- **premise:** In an older apartment tower, everything anyone forgets turns up on the thirteenth floor, where residents ride up to fetch it: keys, a forgotten anniversary as a cake, even a first boyfriend doing the crossword. I've never forgotten anything and never been up there. Then the elevator starts going past my floor to a dark thirteen, where something in a black-and-brass elevator attendant's uniform with a panel of elevator buttons for a face is nearer each morning, a brass tag with my apartment number hanging from its folded gloves.
- **twist:** I was the thing that turned up on thirteen, because Grandad next door had forgotten me. Nothing on thirteen is gone, though, and what you fetch back you remember: I fetch the red scarf he knits me every winter from my shelf up there, put it in his hands, and he says my name. He forgets me most mornings now, so most mornings I go up and fetch myself back.
- **world:** an older apartment tower in an unnamed city: a walnut-and-brass elevator car, the thirteenth floor as a long umber hall of open shelves of forgotten things with brass tags (warm and busy when people fetch, dark with one cold light when the elevator brings me), a beige twelfth-floor landing with two doors, and two side-by-side concrete balconies with a low wall and Grandad's crowd of terracotta pots.
