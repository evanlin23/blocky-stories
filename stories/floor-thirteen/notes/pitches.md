# ep14-floor-thirteen: three pitches (scriptwriter, 2026-10-31)

Blocky story 1 of batch 2 for PandaNanoAdventures (note 21), row 1 of `reels/blocky/batch2-pitches.md`. It's fiction, told in first person, and never claimed as true. Every line is `who: narrator` in voice A, and Mr. Okafor speaks only in reported speech. Target about 2:15 to 2:30 at about 290 wpm, so about 560 to 640 words, 3:00 at most.

## What all three keep from the row

- **Setting:** one apartment tower in an unnamed city, and its lift. The tower has 20 floors, and the lift panel goes from 12 straight to 14.
- **Creature:** the Attendant (fixed points below).
- **First line:** a sound. Every pitch opens on "DING."
- **Warmth:** two strangers become friends. That's me, an adult who's lived here since this spring and knows nobody in the city, and Mr. Okafor from 12B, who never says hello. He knows nothing more than I do. He's scared too, and he isn't a guide.
- **The rule underneath:** the lift only goes to 13 when nobody in it is talking, and two people riding in silence count as riding alone. Each time one of us speaks, one of the Attendant's buttons goes dark, and when the last one goes dark the doors close.
- **The way out:** he tells me about his wife's tomatoes, and I tell him I don't know anyone in the city. She's gone, but it's said gently and never with the words `checks.py` blocks.

## The Attendant, the same in every pitch

- One model, built once in `set.py` and posed per shot.
- A block figure our height in a lift operator's uniform: black jacket and trousers, brass buttons, brass piping, a brass collar.
- Its head is a square brushed-brass panel of round buttons in brass rings. That's a 3x3 grid in A and C and a 2x2 grid in B. There are no numbers or letters on them.
- The buttons glow cold. At rest one is lit, and the lit one moves around the grid the way an eye would. At the scare they're all lit. Once a button goes dark it stays dark.
- Its arms are too long. The white-gloved hands are always folded, low, down by its knees. They never unfold, never reach and never touch anyone.
- It stands too close to the doors.
- It's never seen moving. It's just somewhere new after each DING, or when the doors have hidden it for a moment.
- It never speaks and makes no sound of its own. The DING is the lift's.
- Its colours are brass, black and the cold glow, never pale grey. It's lit from below by its own buttons and from the side, with the front lights off.

**The lift, in all three:** dark wood walls and brass rails, with **no mirror anywhere** (that's mirror-maze's lane). The doors are matte brushed brass, so they don't reflect faces. The panel has round buttons for 1 to 12 and 14 to 20, plus open and close arrows. There's no maker's plate and no words, and the display above the doors shows numbers only. On 13 the only text is the number plate on each door.

## Three things in the row I changed, and why

1. **The scare.** "The lights go out, and when they come back it's inside the lift with us" is ep11's bus beat: the grey passenger came up the bus in the dark, closer each time the lights came back. None of the three uses lights-out. That also keeps this story's flash risk close to nothing.
2. **The last line.** "We never ride ALONE" is a now-we-always ending, like ep11's "And I count SIX too". "The button for THIRTEEN is still there" is a kept-object ending, and lookout (the trees) and moving-day (the box we kept) both end on that shape. Each pitch gets its own last line instead.
3. **"Carpeted like ours."** It's fine as set dressing, but the script never calls 13 a copy of our floor, because copies are mirror-maze's lane. Only A keeps the carpet. B and C give the hallway a different wrongness: it runs further back than the tower is wide.

## Cleared against

- **Batch 1's template:** no "At the PLACE, rule" opener, no knowing elder, no night-shift or one-summer narrator, no pale grey blank face, no "pressed FLAT", no wet click, no "not X, it was Y" cadence, no ritual done back, no later-night return, no "it was lonely/kind all along", no "Last NIGHT," closing opener, no on-screen age, no spoken "it didn't touch me", and no ending where I gesture and it answers.
- **The other batch-2 lanes:** no mirrors, copies or reflections (mirror-maze), no giant, forest, lamp or guiding it home (lookout), and no boxes, moving or Mum (moving-day). No inside-joke, question or mid-scare opener.
- **Words:** nothing `checks.py` blocks or flags, nothing claimed as real, and no real place, building or lift-company names. Note 9 applies to every line, so the script carries none of its tells.
- **Mr. Okafor:** an adult, never a stereotype. There's no accent, and no line is about his voice being loud. He's never in real peril. The Attendant is never nearer to him than to me, and it never touches either of us.

## A. "Between Us"

**In one line:** the lift keeps stopping on a floor our tower doesn't have, and the thing in the uniform waits one door closer each time, until the morning Mr. Okafor and I ride down in silence together and it ends up standing in the space between us.

**First line** (the row's own): `DING. / Floor THIRTEEN. / Our building / doesn't HAVE one.`

**Who and where:** I live on 16, and Mr. Okafor lives below me in 12B. When I ride down alone, the lift passes the floor the panel skips.

**The plants** (both in by about 15 s)
1. `Mr. Okafor / from TWELVE B / never says HELLO.` `He takes / the STAIRS. / All the way DOWN.` The picture shows him walking past me at the lift and on to the stair door.
2. `My HEADPHONES / are always IN.` The picture is me in the lift with my eyes on the display.

**The middle: one door closer.** On 13 the hallway has the same orange carpet as every floor, four shut doors a side, and every door plate reads 13.
1. Monday, alone. DING. It stands at the far end, by the fourth door. One button is lit, and it's on me. The doors close on their own.
2. Thursday. DING. It's one door closer, by the third. That's close enough to see the arms are too long and the folded gloves sit at its knees.
3. The next Tuesday, by the second door. That's close enough to see its buttons have no numbers. Its light goes from my face down to my headphones and back.

**Together:** one morning the stairwell is shut for painting. The picture shows a ladder and a paint tray in the doorway, with no sign. The lift stops on 12 and Mr. Okafor gets in. No hello. My headphones stay in. He presses the lobby button, and the lift goes UP. DING. It's at the doors now, so close its sleeves nearly brush them. The doors won't close. Its one light goes from him to me. `HIM. / ME.`

**The scare, between us:** a second DING with the doors still open. The music drops away first. The doorway is empty. Then a cold glow lights the side of his face. `It was IN / the lift, / standing / BETWEEN us.` `Every button / LIT.` The wide from the hallway side shows three figures in a lift, and the middle one is wrong. It was never seen stepping in. It's there on the cut.

**How we find the way out, by accident:** Mr. Okafor mutters, "I should've taken the STAIRS." One button goes dark. I pull my headphones out and say, "Me TOO." Another goes dark. We look at each other past its shoulder.

**The talk, nine buttons and nine turns.** Each turn darkens one button, in this order:
1. He says, "I should've / taken the STAIRS."
2. I say, "Me TOO."
3. I say, "Say something ELSE."
4. He says, "I don't TALK / to people."
5. I say, "Me NEITHER. / I don't know / ANYONE here."
6. He says, "My wife / did the TALKING."
7. He says, "I still water / her TOMATOES."
8. I say, "I've never seen / a tomato / GROW."
9. He says, "Come and LOOK, / then."

On "then", cut to the two of us. Cut back, and the space between us is empty. The doors close, and the lift goes down.

**The twist:** `It only comes / when NOBODY / in the lift / is TALKING.` `Even if / there are / TWO of you.` Both plants re-read at once: he takes the stairs, I wear headphones, and neither of us had said a word to anyone in this building.

**The warm turn, shown:**
- The lobby doors open on morning light, and we walk out side by side with my headphones round my neck.
- Then his balcony on 12 in low evening sun. It's all tomato pots. He rests his hand on one bigger, older pot, and that's hers (the line says only "He showed me / which pot / was HERS"). There's a cherry tomato in my palm, and we're both leaning on the rail.

**Last line:** `Then, from INSIDE, / his oven TIMER / went OFF.` Hold 0.25 s, then hard cut to the first shot. The timer is the same synthesized bell as the lift, so the warm DING becomes the scary one.

**Counts to match the picture:** four doors a side. It stands by the fourth, third and second door, then at the lift doors. That's nine buttons, nine turns and nine dark.

**Length:** about 600 words, about 2:20 with the held beats.

**Why it's not a template or another lane:** the opener is a sound, nobody knows more than anyone else, the scare is the gap between two silent people rather than ep11's lights-out, and it ends on a kitchen-timer bell, not a ritual, a return or a kind monster. Its weak spot is that "one door closer" is the most familiar dread beat in the lessons.

## B. "Going Up"

**In one line:** I live on 12, and when I press the lobby button the lift goes up instead, to a 13th floor our tower doesn't have. The thing in the uniform is always waiting right outside the doors, and each time the doors stay open longer.

**First line:** `DING. / I pressed LOBBY. / The lift / went UP.` then `From TWELVE / to a FLOOR / we don't HAVE.`

**Who and where:** I'm in 12A. Mr. Okafor is next door in 12B. Our balconies sit side by side with a low wall between them. Every trip to 13 starts on 12 and goes up one floor.

**The plants**
1. **The balcony wall** (about 12 s): `Mr. Okafor / next door / has never / said HELLO.` `But most EVENINGS, / over the BALCONY wall, / I hear him / TALKING.` `To his TOMATOES.` The picture shows him bent over his pots with his mouth going, while I'm on my side pretending not to hear. He talks, just never to people.
2. **The phone ride** (about 25 s): `Wednesday, / I was on / the PHONE / the whole way DOWN,` `arguing with / my LANDLORD / about the HEATING.` `The lift / didn't stop / ONCE.` The picture is me mid-rant with the phone screen dark, while the display counts down past 12 to the ground.

**The middle: it holds me there longer each time.** It's right outside the doors from the very first DING, and it never comes nearer. What grows is how long the doors stay open, and its one lit button watches what I do. Each time it's somewhere new along the doors, and it's never seen getting there. The hallway behind it runs further back than the tower is wide.
1. Monday (the cold open). It stands just left of the doors. Its top-left button is lit, on me. The doors close on their own after a breath, and the lift goes down like nothing happened.
2. Thursday, with my headphones in. DING. It's just right of the doors. The doors stay open. I press CLOSE, and its light slides to its bottom corner, like it's watching my finger. They close on my third press, and we see all three.
3. Saturday. DING. It's straight in front of the doors. I press CLOSE over and over before they give. `I didn't tell ANYONE.` `Who would I TELL?`

**Together:** the next Monday morning, Mr. Okafor and I open our front doors at the same moment. We wait for the lift without a word and stand in opposite corners. I press the lobby button, and the lift goes up. From the way his face goes stiff, he's been here before too, and never told anyone either. DING. It's right in front of the doors with all four buttons lit. The doors don't even try to close.

**The scare, the doors close on it:** he presses CLOSE. Nothing. He presses it again. The music drops out. The doors start to slide, then bump something and roll back open. On the bump it's IN the doorway, right in the gap. `Four LIGHTS, / a FOOT / from our faces.` The doors bump its shoulders, open, and bump them again. That's a soft rubber bump and the motor's whine, both synthesized, never a knock. His hand finds my sleeve.

**How we find the way out, I work it out:** `The one ride / I TALKED / the whole way, / it never CAME.` `So I said, / "TALK to me."` `One light / went OUT.`

**The talk, four buttons and four turns.** The 2x2 head reads at phone size, and four is a count anyone can follow. In order:
1. I say, "TALK / to me."
2. He stares at the dark button and says, "About WHAT?"
3. I say, "ANYTHING. / I don't know / ANYONE / in this city."
4. He answers, in my telling: `He said / the tomatoes / were his WIFE'S.` `He's kept them going / four YEARS / without her.`

The last light goes out. The doors slide shut, and nothing's in the way.

**The twist:** `It only comes / when NOBODY / in the lift / is TALKING.` That's both plants at once. He talks, but only to tomatoes. I talked once, and the lift went straight down.

**The warm turn, in pictures:**
- The lobby doors open on daylight, and the two of us step out together.
- That evening a tomato comes over the balcony wall, then another, then his whole head, still talking. It's a two-shot from my side in warm porch light, both of us leaning on the low wall.
- The next morning in the lift we're both talking. He's waving a tomato around, I'm laughing, and the display runs 12 to 14 with no 13 between them.

**Last line:** `We rode it / up to TWENTY / and back DOWN / before either of us / NOTICED.` Hold 0.25 s, then hard cut to the first shot's DING.

**Counts to match the picture:** four buttons. One is lit at a time on the three solo trips, all four are lit on the ride together, then four turns and four dark. There are three presses on Thursday. The display skips 13 on the last ride.

**Length:** about 590 words, about 2:15 to 2:20.

**Why it's not a template or another lane:** the dread is the doors being held open, not something creeping nearer in the dark. The rule is solved from a clue the viewer saw, not told by anyone. It ends on two people forgetting to get off, a shape no story on the channel has used. It has nothing reflected and nobody copied or guided, and its face never redraws the way moving-day's box does. Only which of its four lights is on changes.

## C. "Doors Closing"

**In one line:** each time the lift stops on a 13th floor our tower doesn't have, a little more of the world's sound goes missing, until the morning Mr. Okafor rides with me and the only sound left is the DING.

**First line:** `DING. / Then my MUSIC / CUT OUT.` then `The screen / said THIRTEEN. / Our building / SKIPS thirteen.`

**Who and where:** my place is on 16, and Mr. Okafor's is 12B. The lift has a recorded voice that says "Doors closing". Like every voice in the film, it's in my narration as reported speech.

**The plants**
1. **The lift's voice** (about 10 s): `Most DAYS, / the only VOICE / I hear / is the LIFT'S.` `It says, / "Doors CLOSING."`
2. **The mailboxes** (about 20 s): `Mr. Okafor / from TWELVE B / gets his MAIL / when I get MINE.` `Six MONTHS, / side by SIDE, / and not / one WORD.` The picture is a lobby wall of plain brass mailboxes with no names or numbers, and the two of us not looking at each other.

**The middle: the sound drains away.** Each stop on 13 takes one more sound with it, and the mix carries the escalation. The hallway is shut doors, all numbered 13, and it runs too far back. The Attendant is somewhere new each DING.
1. Monday. DING. My music cuts out. It's at the far end, one button lit, on me. The doors close, and the lift doesn't say "Doors closing".
2. Thursday. DING. The lift's hum and its fan go too. It's halfway down the hall with its back to me, facing a door, and its glow is on the paint.
3. The next Monday. DING. I drop my keys, and they hit the floor without a sound. It's right outside the doors now, facing me, too close.

**Together:** Wednesday, the lift stops on 12 and Mr. Okafor gets in. No hello. The lift says "Doors closing", then it goes UP. DING. Then nothing at all. `I couldn't hear / my own BREATHING.` His face says he's heard this silence before. The hall is empty, and the doors stay open.

**The scare, the panel:** I press CLOSE. Nothing, not even the click. I press it again. DING, the only sound left. `My finger / stopped / an INCH / from a BUTTON.` `A LIT one.` `The panel / was wearing / a COLLAR.` It's inside the lift, standing between me and the lift's own panel, every button lit and every one on me, with its folded gloves right under my hand. It was never seen arriving. It's there on the DING.

**How we find the way out, by sound:** I whisper, "Mr. OKAFOR?" And I can hear it. The hum comes back, just a little, and one button goes dark.

**The talk, nine buttons and nine turns.** Each turn darkens one button and brings back one more layer of the mix. The hum comes first, then the fan, the cables, a TV through a wall and the traffic outside. My headphones' music comes back last. In order:
1. I say, "Mr. OKAFOR?"
2. He says, "You know / my NAME?"
3. I say, "We get our MAIL / at the same TIME."
4. He says, "I don't know / YOURS."
5. I answer, in my telling: `I told him / my NAME.`
6. He says, "Who do you / KNOW here?"
7. I say, "NOBODY. / Not in / this whole CITY."
8. He says, "My WIFE / knew everyone / in this BUILDING."
9. He says, "She grew TOMATOES / for HALF of them."

The last button goes dark on her tomatoes. `And the lift / said, / "Doors CLOSING."` The space in front of the panel is empty, and the doors close.

**The twist:** `It only comes / when NOBODY / is TALKING.` Both plants re-read: the lift's voice was the only one I heard, and the two of us had stood at those mailboxes for six months without a word.

**What the camera shows for the warmth:**
- The next morning at the mailboxes, he's there first, already talking as I walk up. The shot carries it: his mouth going, my face laughing, no line needed under it.
- I open my mailbox. He doesn't look over, but the corner of his mouth goes up.

**Last line:** `Today / there was / a TOMATO / in my MAILBOX.` The picture is the open brass door with a red tomato inside and the lift behind us. Hold 0.25 s, then hard cut to the first shot's DING.

**Counts to match the picture:** three solo stops, each removing the sound its line names. That's my music, then the hum and fan, then the keys. The ride together is total silence. That's nine buttons, nine turns and nine dark, with one layer of the mix back on each turn and "Doors closing" back last.

**Length:** about 620 words, the longest talk of the three, about 2:25.

**Why it's not a template or another lane:** silence is its engine, which is the nearest thing here to ep12's sound rule, but nothing has to be answered or copied, nobody explains anything, and it ends on a tomato in a mailbox. The name beat is me saying his name. It's never a copy mouthing mine (mirror-maze), and nothing gets guided anywhere.

## My pick: B, "Going Up"

- **The cleanest logic.** Four buttons and four turns are easy to count and easy to match to the picture. The way out is worked out from a clue the viewer saw at 25 s (the phone ride), so it rewards a re-watch and gives the packager a fair pin question.
- **The scare uses the creature's own trait.** It stands too close to the doors, so the doors close on it and bounce off. A square of four cold lights in a brass gap reads at phone size, and no batch-1 story had anything like it.
- **No lights-out,** so the flash pre-check on this story should be short.
- **The friendship has the most room to show.** Next-door neighbours, a shared balcony wall, a tomato coming over it, then his whole head, still talking.
- **A new ending shape:** two people so busy talking they ride past their floor. None of ep11 to ep13 and none of the other batch-2 rows ends like that.
- **The cheapest build:** one lift car, one door gap, a short hall, two balconies and a lobby.

**The risk:** a 2x2 head might read as a toy. The creature test still decides that. If it does read that way, the head goes to 3x3 and the talk grows to nine turns, borrowing A's lines 3 to 8 in spirit. That adds about 15 s, which I'd pay for by cutting trip 3. C is the runner-up for its scare, and A is the safe fallback closest to the row.
