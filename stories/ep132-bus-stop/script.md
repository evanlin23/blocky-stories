# ep132-bus-stop: the script (writer, 2026-11-06, batch 14)

- **Slug:** `ep132-bus-stop` (batch 14, upload order 4 of 6)
- **Pitch:** 5 in the batch 14 pitch round, picked by the panel (6.8). Engine: the hidden participant, a rule-logic comedy flip (the rule needed one of us to be the driver). Opening shape `cost` (the fare). Lane: experiment (comedy). Length arm: ultra (20 to 45 s).
- **Title:** The Bus Hasn't Come In 41 DAYS 🚏
- **Panel note built in (the logic hole):** no bus is parked unseen for 41 days. The bus sits inside the bus depot across the street, behind its big shut roll-up door, in frame in every wide (L06). The old driver retired 41 days ago (L05), and the new one is a stranger to all of us. He comes to the stop every morning because the rule binds him too: the bus can't leave the depot until two strangers talk at the stop, and he's far too shy to go first (L15, L16). Everyone else gives up and walks (L07).
- **Batch 14 constraints, applied to every shot:**
  1. Flash-safe by design. It's one sunny morning with no light change. The depot door is shut, then (after a cut) up. It never rolls on screen.
  2. Two faces at most in any shot. The bench is framed two faces at a time, and the emotional line (L09) is on a single close-up of the driver facing camera. Nothing is held near a face.
  3. Nothing small has to read. The bus and the depot door are huge. The chalk count is big strokes and numerals on the shelter's side wall.
  4. No set moves (standards r1). The depot door is shut in one shot and already up in the next, and the bus is already at the depot mouth (S15, creeping at most half a bus length) or already stopped at the shelter (S17). Nothing big moves on screen.
  5. The rule marker lives in the scene: the chalk count of days on the shelter's side wall, in frame in every bench shot.
- **Everyone is an adult.** I'm 26. The driver (Mr. Tolley) is about 45. The other commuters are adults.
- **Word count:** 159 spoken words in 22 lines (L01 to L22), all `narrator`. No music drop (comedy, one beat).
- **Estimated length:** about 37 s at 290 wpm with 0.15 s gaps and the loop tail.
- **Look:** `"look": {"style": "closer", "flash_safe": true, "polish": true}`. A sunny corner on a bright small-town morning: a glass-and-red bus shelter with a pale yellow side wall, a long bench, the blue roll-up door of the bus depot across the street, a cherry tree, blue sky.

## The big idea
**At our bus stop the fare is two strangers talking, and the bus hasn't come in forty-one days because the shiest stranger on the bench every morning is the new driver, waiting for one of us to say something first.**

- **Why it clicks:** the bus stopped coming the day the old driver retired (L05), the depot door across the street stays shut (L06), the same quiet man is on the bench every morning (L08), and he sighs at us (L08). After L14, he was the other stranger the rule needed.
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: a bus parked behind the shelter (the panel's hole), the driver is me (confusing), the bus is invisible (failed material), the bus comes for a fight (ugly), a talking bench (a prop), the bus goes somewhere strange (ep52, ep115), everyone pretends (ep94), the fare is a secret (E24), a robot driver (rule 1 of the playbook), the driver is my dad (family, not strangers).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas": not ep119 (riders waiting for an answer), not back-row (a count on a bus). Shape `go-first` (changed after standards r1 so the batch has at most two "he waited, I showed up" endings: the warm beat is me going first for the next stranger, never somebody waiting on me).
- **The rule is the clue:** "the fare is two strangers talking" sounds like it's on us. After L14, one of the two was always going to be him.

## The story
- **Rule / hook (L01, 11 words, about 2.3 s):** "At our bus stop, / the fare is two strangers / TALKING." L02: "Nobody here / goes FIRST." (L02 plants the shy driver.)
- **The relatable want or fight:** being late every day, and the awkward silence of strangers at a stop.
- **The marker (the rule meter), in frame by 0 s and in every bench shot:** a chalk count on the shelter's pale yellow side wall, a big "41" with tally strokes beside it.
- **The montage (L02 to L07, about 2 to 11 s):** nobody talks, everybody stares at their shoes, the chalk count, the shut depot door, everyone walking off.
- **A concrete mystery by 15 s:** forty-one days without a bus, and a depot door that never opens (L04 to L06).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** I'm the one who finally talks (L09).
- **The loved one at stake:** none (strangers, a comedy).
- **First real beat (L08 to L11, about 11 to 18 s):** the quiet man next to me sighs, and I say "Nice day".
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** the fare is two strangers talking, and the shy man was one of them. No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L12 to L16, about 18 to 26 s, said plainly):** "He's the DRIVER." over S15: the depot door already up, the bus at its mouth, the man at the wheel waving through the windshield, `laugh`.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** every morning there's somebody new on the bench, so I go first. L01's fare is now something I pay for the next stranger.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L05: the bus stopped the day the old driver retired.
  2. L06: the depot door across the street stays shut.
  3. L02 and L08: nobody goes first, and the same quiet man sighs at us every morning.
- **Ending shape:** `go-first`.
- **Warmth:** strangers, and me becoming the one who goes first (comedy).
- **Sad family reveal?** No.

## Clarity test (cold, two sentences)
At this bus stop the bus only comes after two strangers talk, and nobody had talked for forty-one days, ever since a new driver started. When the narrator finally says "nice day" to the shy man beside him, the man walks across the street into the depot and drives the bus out: he was the new driver, too shy to talk first, and now the narrator is the one who says hi to every new face on the bench.
