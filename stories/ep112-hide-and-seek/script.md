## The big idea
**Our family's hide-and-seek game has never ended because Uncle Pete was never found, and for thirty-one years he's been hiding as our grandma's next-door neighbor, Mr. Glenn, who helps us look for him every reunion.**

- **Engine:** hiding in plain sight. No story's letter or line: our reunion, our salad.
- **Why it clicks:** Mr. Glenn helps look every year (L07), always sits facing the old shed where Pete hid (L08), and brings Pete's famous potato salad that nobody else has the recipe for (L09 to L12). Nobody looks at the guy helping you look (L18).
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: Pete hid in the shed and never came out (a person gone, too dark), Pete is the family dog (a gag with no logic), Pete is a scarecrow (ep108-scarecrow-swap's field), Grandma is hiding from all of us (a sad beat), Pete is me (muddled), Pete went to the moon (no picture), the shed is bigger inside (a door idea), Pete is Grandpa in disguise (two relatives at once), the game counts us and we're one too many (a Keeper's count), Pete hides by standing very still (ep88's man in the window).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not ep82's house in disguise (a person, not a creature), not ep88's still watcher, not a copy or a swap, and the reveal rests on his salad and his own words, never on reading the mustache alone. Shape `plain-sight`. (ep108-scarecrow-swap, four uploads earlier, is a grandpa swapped with a straw scarecrow for a job, set up with Grandma, on a farm at dusk, ending on Grandma preferring the straw one. This is one relative posing as another person to win a family game, at a reunion, ending on the next one hiding.)
- **The rule is the clue:** "Uncle Pete's still hiding" reads as a mystery or a vanishing. After L14 he's been next door the whole time.

## The story
- **Premise:** Thirty-one years ago our whole family played hide-and-seek at Grandma's, and Grandma found everyone by dinner except Uncle Pete. The game isn't over until everyone's found, so every reunion we look again, with the help of Grandma's neighbor, Mr. Glenn.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.8 s):** "My family's game / of hide-and-SEEK / started thirty-one years ago. / Uncle Pete's still HIDING."
- **The relatable want or fight:** family reunions, and the relative who always disappears.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag top-left with an eye glyph and a game clock, years and days: 31 : 204, ticking a day at L05. At L14 the eye glyph flips to a tick and the clock freezes. At L19 it resets to 0 : 0 with a new eye.
- **The beat:** tasting Mr. Glenn's potato salad (L10 to L12).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** thirty-one years, still hiding (L01), and the salad nobody else can make (L11, L12, about 17 to 20 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** the first to taste the salad and think.
- **The loved one at stake:** Uncle Pete, named in L01, and Grandma, named at L02 in frame counting.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** the game ends when everyone's found, and nobody said you had to find him behind something (L14).
- **Twist and reveal (L14 to L17, about 21 to 26 s):** said plainly (L14: "Found you, Uncle PETE.") over one picture (S15: the big crooked mustache dropping into the potato salad, Pete's face under it, caught and grinning).
- **How the last seconds turn over the first line:** Pete's "it" now and counting at the shed, and I'm hiding: next year I'll be at the reunion in a mustache, which runs straight into L01 as my game now.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L08: Mr. Glenn always sits facing the old shed.
  2. L11, L12: his salad is Pete's famous one, and nobody else has the recipe.
- **Ending shape:** the found one becomes the seeker, and I'm the next one hiding.
- **Warmth:** family play, a family that's kept a game going for thirty-one years.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. Comedy.
