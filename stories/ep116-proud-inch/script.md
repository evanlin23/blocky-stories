## The big idea
**In a town where saying you're proud of someone makes you grow an inch, my quiet grandpa is nine feet tall and has never once said it to me, because the person he says it to is anyone in town who fixes something with their own hands, fifty years of them, one pencil mark each on his door frame, and in six years at his counter I've never fixed a single thing myself.**

## The story
- **Premise:** My grandpa Gil runs the town hardware store. He says maybe ten words a day, and he's never once said he's proud of me, yet he's nine feet tall and still growing. I've worked his counter every Saturday for six years, so he must be proud of somebody else. He is.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.8 s):** "Here, every time / you say you're PROUD / of someone, / you grow an INCH."
- **The relatable want or fight:** wanting a quiet grandparent to be proud of you, and never quite trying.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag top-left with a ruler glyph and Grandpa's height in feet and inches (9'0" rising to 9'2"), and a second, smaller row for me (5'9" to 5'10"). - **The loved one at stake:** my grandpa Gil, named "My grandpa, Gil," at L06, seated on his crate, face to camera.

- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** he grows when he says it, and he says it to whoever fixes something themselves, so I fix the stuck back door myself (L37, L38), he says it to my face (L40), and I say it back and grow my first inch (L42, L43). No new magic.
