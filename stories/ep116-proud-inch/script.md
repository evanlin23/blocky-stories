## The big idea
**In a town where saying you're proud of someone makes you grow an inch, my quiet grandpa is nine feet tall and has never once said it to me, and every inch of him is me: he's said it every morning for six years, to my photo by the register, and marked each inch on his door frame beside a drawing of what I did.**

- **Engine:** the secret benefactor: praise said behind your back. No film's giant.
- **Why it clicks:** the inches started the year I started working Saturdays (L11, L14), I hear him talking alone in the dark shop before I come in (L18), and my photo sits by the register (S21b).
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: Grandpa is proud of his rival's shop (a joke, no feeling), Grandpa is shrinking from shame (sad), I'm the one growing without knowing (`about-me`), Grandpa brags about a grandson who isn't me (a sad family reveal), the inches are lies (muddled), he's tall from pride in himself (no stakes), the town measures pride on a wall (no person), my mom is the tall one (moves the love away from the shop), Grandpa grows from praising customers (a twist with no me in it), he can't stop growing and will hit the sky (peril).

- **The rule is the clue:** "every time you say you're proud of someone, you grow an inch" makes nine feet sound like a lot of somebody. After L32 it's one somebody, said many times, never to his face.

## The story
- **Premise:** My grandpa Gil runs the town hardware store. He says maybe ten words a day, and he's never once said he's proud of me, yet he's nine feet tall and still growing. I've worked his counter every Saturday for six years and I'm still five foot nine, so it must be somebody else.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.8 s):** "Here, every time / you say you're PROUD / of someone, / you grow an INCH."
- **The relatable want or fight:** wanting a quiet grandparent to say he's proud of you.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag top-left with a ruler glyph and Grandpa's height in feet and inches (9'0" rising to 9'2"), and a second, smaller row for me (5'9" to 5'10"). It ticks with a soft pencil scratch when anyone grows. Numerals and glyphs only.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L05, about 5 to 11 s):** a bragging dad ducking under a café door, the mail carrier seven foot two beside her tomato plants, a bride's dad dancing on his knees at a wedding.
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** nine feet tall (L07, about 12 s), and never said it to me (L10).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** six years at his counter and I've never grown an inch, because nobody's said it to me and I've never said it to anyone.
- **The loved one at stake:** my grandpa Gil, named "My grandpa, Gil," at L06, seated on his crate, face to camera.
- **First real scare (L16 to L22, about 28 to 39 s):** the shop dark at dawn, Grandpa's voice in the dark, the ceiling creaking, a ceiling tile pushing up over the counter.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** you grow when you *say* it, so I say it to him first (L34 to L37), and then he can say it to my face (L39). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L23 to L33, about 39 to 58 s):** Mrs. Bloom tells me he practices on my photo every morning (L25), and the door frame's pencil marks, each with a drawing of something I did, are the picture (S28 to S31). Said plainly at L32: "Every inch / he's grown / was ME."
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** I say it, I grow my first inch, he says it out loud to me, the ceiling tile pops out, and the last line ("So go on. / SAY it.") runs straight into L01.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**

  2. S21b: my photo propped by the register, the morning I hear him talking in the dark.
- **Ending shape:** `behind-your-back`: the praise was always about me, said where I couldn't hear.
- **Warmth:** pride said out loud, both ways.
