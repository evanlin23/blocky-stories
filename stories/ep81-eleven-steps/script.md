## The big idea
**The eleventh stair is a carpeted box my dad has set at the bottom of our stairs every night for eleven years, because something climbs exactly ten steps every night, and with eleven it stops one short of my door.**

- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the eleventh stair is Dad sleepwalking across a gap (the pitch's own idea, but sleepwalking was ep41's twist, and "the creature is a loved one" was warm-shoes'), the house is breathing in (no stakes), the house is climbing a step a night (no feeling), the stairs measure the distance between two people who don't talk (ep20's "we moved apart"), the house lowers its steps for a proud grandpa's knees (mild), the new step leads to a floor that isn't ours (floor-thirteen), you count eleven because you count yourself (ep43), the extra step is a flat creature holding up a rotten stair (kind all along), each step is a year of our lives (time), the steps belong to whoever waits up and they become a step (a loved one as the creature again).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not forgotten or unseen, not a stand-in, not a reflection, not a swap. It's the canon's "a count that's wrong on purpose" (`stories/lore.md` → Other things a count can do): a person lies to a count to protect someone, and the twist is the lie.
- **The rule is the clue:** "At night, you'll count ELEVEN" first reads as the house growing a step. After the reveal it reads as Dad's step, there because the thing only ever climbs ten. L02 is the plant in plain sight: the creaks are ten, never eleven.

## The story
- **Premise:** By day our stairs have ten steps, at night eleven, and at 2 a.m. they creak ten times with nobody on them. Dad's one house rule is that everyone's upstairs by ten and nobody's on the stairs after. I think it's a Dad thing, so I sit at the top one night to catch the new step appear.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.1 s):** "Our stairs have TEN steps. / At night, / you'll count ELEVEN." L02 tightens it: "And at two A.M., / they CREAK. / TEN times."
- **The relatable want / fight:** a grown son and a dad with a house rule that makes no sense, and a fight about it (L26, L27).
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a cream torn-tape step counter in the top-left corner, our own design: a big plum numeral that reads 10 by day and 11 at night. During L30 to L33 a second small row of plum tick marks counts the creaks up to ten. Frame 1 already reads 11.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L08, first 15 s):** ten by day, I've counted a thousand times (L03), eleven after dark and I can't tell which is new (L04), my friend Luis counting them and driving home at midnight (L05, L06), the ten creaks with nobody on them (L07), the cold step just below the landing every morning (L08).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** eleven steps at night, and ten creaks on empty stairs (L01, L02, L07).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** everyone follows Dad's rule. I sit on the stairs after ten, then move his box.
- **The loved one at stake:** my dad, named plainly at L09 in the same shot as him, and me. What I could lose is clear by 20 s: his trust, and a door with nothing between it and the stairs.
- **The rule broken:** L15, about 28 s in (on the stairs after ten), then L28 (moving the box).
- **First real scare:** L29 to L40 (54 to 67 s): ten counted creaks, the door bowing, a sideways face at the gap under my door.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** it climbs ten, so all that matters is one more step between it and me, anywhere. Dad always sets the box at the bottom, but a step doesn't have to be at the bottom (L50, L51). I shove it out onto the landing in front of my door, and it stands one step short until dawn. No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L43 to L48, 72 to 83 s):** said plainly. "It climbs TEN steps. Every NIGHT. It has never ONCE climbed ELEVEN. That's what Dad's box is FOR."
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** Dad and I take turns setting the box, so the count of eleven is now ours on purpose, and then the sting: it still stops one short, but last night it sat down to wait (L69, L70). The last line ("So if you ever / stay OVER,") runs into L01.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L02 and L07: ten creaks on stairs of eleven.
  2. L08: the step just below the landing is ice cold every morning (where it stood all night).
  3. L10 to L13: the rule (upstairs by ten, nobody on the stairs after), Dad always up last, carpet fluff on his knees.
  4. Unnarrated: frame 1's bottom step is a slightly brighter plum than the other ten, with no wear dip.
- **Ending shape:** two people taking turns at a job, then a cold sting (it sat down), and a loop.
- **Warmth:** a dad's quiet care, shown: he doesn't ask, he sits down beside me on the landing, and now we take turns.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. Dad is fine and in every shot of the ending. No one is gone. Mom is never mentioned.

## World
One place: the staircase of a small two-storey house and the landing at its top. A straight run of ten honey-oak steps with a plum carpet runner held by brass rods, white-painted banister and spindles, cream walls with a few framed pictures (shapes only, no photos of anyone young), a small round window at the turn showing the night sky, an amber plug-in nightlight on the landing. At the top, my door (white, with a 2 cm gap under it), Dad's door at the bottom of the stairs off the hall, the front door at the end of the hall, a hall lamp with a mustard shade.
- **The box:** an oak box exactly one step high and the stairs' width, carpeted in the same plum runner with one brass rod. It sits snug against the first step. By day it lives under the stairs in a little cupboard.
- **No text anywhere.** The counter tag is numerals and tick marks only.
- **What's true underneath (never said beyond the reveal):** every night at 2 a.m. the Climber comes up our stairs, counting. It climbs ten steps, never more, then stands until dawn. With Dad's box at the bottom, ten leaves it on the real ninth step, just below the landing, which is why that step is cold. It never touches anyone and never opens a door. Nobody knows where it comes from.
