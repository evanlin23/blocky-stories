## The big idea
**Every "worst day of your life" the morning radio ever called was only the whole town staying away from whoever it named, so the bad luck was us all along, and last spring, on his birthday, I stayed away from my best friend too.**

- **Engine:** the self-fulfilling prophecy (engine 9). No myth's oracle or film's radio: our kitchen radio, our oranges.
- **Why it clicks:** every bad day in the montage is something nobody did (the baker sold nothing, the bus didn't stop, a woman ate alone, a phone never rang, L05 to L08). On my own named day the line steps back, the bus slows, sees me and goes, and a dozen people watch my oranges roll (L11 to L19). Then the one person who comes near me (Dev) has a perfectly fine day (L34 to L36).
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the radio names the next person to vanish (peril), the radio is read by a Keeper counting the town (a count), the radio names whoever needs us most (ep114's "around one sad person"), the radio's voice is mine from the future (time, a copy), the radio names whoever's thought about most (ep110's rule), the named are lucky and the town is jealous (no warmth), Dev is the voice on the radio (a silhouette-free but muddled trick), the radio names the person who'll save the town (a chosen-one plot), the radio names a person to be forgotten (the crowded forgotten theme), the radio stops naming once someone breaks the rule (new magic).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not the forgotten or unseen theme (everyone sees me, all day, from their windows, they just won't come near), not ep96's card (no double reading of a sentence), not ep98's ritual-is-the-trap (no thing behind the rule), not ep114's town around one person (one friend comes, the town keeps watching). Shape `self-fulfilling`.
- **The rule is the clue:** "you can't go near that person all day" first reads as protection from bad luck. After L37 it's the only bad luck there ever was.

## The story
- **Premise:** In our town, each morning the kitchen radio reads out one name, and nobody may go near that person all day, because a named day is the worst day of your life and bad luck is catching. In twenty-four years it's never said mine. This Saturday morning, it does. Last spring it named my best friend Dev, on his birthday, and I stayed home like everybody else. We haven't talked since.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 3 s):** "When the morning radio / says a NAME, / you can't go near that person / all DAY." L02: "This morning, / it said MINE."
- **The relatable want or fight:** a best friend you let down and never apologized to.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag top-left with a radio-dial glyph lit red and a day clock running from 7:00 to midnight (7:02 at L02, 9:40 at L16, 2:00 at L35, 11:59 at L43). Beside it, a little person glyph inside a ring: an empty ring around me while I'm alone, and a second little person glyph appearing inside it at L28 when Dev kneels down.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L05 to L08, 8 to 19 s):** the named baker selling nothing, the named man whose bus didn't stop, the named woman eating lunch alone on her birthday, the named man whose new phone never rang.
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** the radio says my name (L02, the first frame's dial), and I'm 24 and it's never said it before (L09, about 19 s). The threat to me is in L02.
- **How the narrator is the exception:** never named in twenty-four years, and today, the only one in town nobody will go near.
- **The loved one at stake:** my best friend Dev, named "My best friend, DEV." at L21 over a present-day shot of our photo on my fridge.
- **First real scare (L14 to L19, about 30 to 37 s):** a face in every window on the street, watching me, then my bag tears and a dozen people watch the oranges roll and nobody moves.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** the town's reason for the rule is that a named day is the worst day of your life and bad luck is catching. Dev stays with me all day, and nothing bad happens to either of us (L34 to L36). So the worst day "of your life" was only ever the day nobody came, and mine isn't one (L42, L43). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L32 to L38, about 55 to 69 s):** said plainly (L37, L38: "Every bad day / the radio ever CALLED / was just a whole town / staying AWAY." "The bad day / is US.") over one picture (S37: a high wide of the bright main street at noon, Dev and me on the bench in the middle of a perfect empty ring, a face at every window around us).
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** the next morning the radio names our neighbor Mrs. Odell, and Dev and I are on her porch by seven with coffee, while the rest of the street watches from its windows. They still think it's the rule, which runs straight into L01.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L05 to L08: no bad day in the montage is bad luck. Each one is only something nobody did.
  2. L24, L25: on Dev's birthday I walked past the cafe and saw him at a table with a cake and two plates (one was for me).
  3. L13: the bus slows down for me, sees me, and keeps going (the driver chose it).
- **Ending shape:** the rule broken by two people, then the town still keeping it, which loops into L01.
- **Warmth:** owning up to my best friend, and being forgiven.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. No family is involved.

## Clarity test (cold, two sentences)
In this town nobody may go near whoever the morning radio names, because a named day is supposed to be the worst day of your life and the bad luck spreads, and when it names the narrator, everyone avoids him until his best friend Dev, whom he abandoned on Dev's own named day, comes and spends the whole day with him.
