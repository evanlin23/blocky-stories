# The canon (the stories' hidden world)

The writer keeps this file. Each story is complete on its own, and plants one small connection to what's here (RULES.md → Deep lore and a real twist). Write what's true in this world, even the parts no story has shown yet, so connections stay consistent. Never spell the canon out on screen.

## What's true
(Set by ep20 `photo-strip`, 2026-11-01. Written to fit ep11 to ep13 as they already are.)

- **The Makers built the towns.** Block by block, a long time ago, and then they left. Nobody in any story knows them by that name or any name. They are never shown.
- **The Keepers are the Makers' first tries at people.** They're built from the same blocks as us, but made before the Makers settled on the shape: too tall, too wide, too flat, faces put on wrong. That's why every creature in every story is a person gone slightly wrong in one dimension. The Makers didn't throw them away. They gave each one a place to keep.
- **Every Keeper keeps a count.** Each place the Makers built was built for a number of people: a bus, a house, a hall, a photo. A Keeper's whole job is to keep that count true. When a place is short, the Keeper stands in the gap and counts itself, so the number holds. It doesn't want the space. It just can't leave it empty.
  - ep11: the back-row passenger is the bus's sixth. The driver's "six" was the count, and it was always right.
  - ep12: the keeper under the farmhouse floor knocks to check the family is all still there. The knock is a roll call.
  - ep13: the watchers behind the big tank wait for every one of the shift to bow. The show only ends when the count is complete.
  - ep20: the Third stands in the gap between two people who should be sitting together. The more room they leave, the more of it shows.
- **The mark is a tally.** Short upright strokes, in the colour of whatever Keeper made them, one stroke per person counted, the Keeper included. It's stamped, scratched or pressed into the things a Keeper keeps. It is never letters and never explained. A tally with one stroke too many means a Keeper is still counted there, even when you can't see it.
- **Who keeps the rules.** The rules people follow (never sit in the back row, knock back, wave goodbye, sit in the middle) were made up by people, long ago, to stay on the right side of a count they could feel but couldn't see. Nobody alive remembers why. The Keepers don't make rules. They only count.
- **What's under the towns.** Far below every town is the Bench: the Makers' workbench floor, flat and endless, with unfinished pieces lying on it. Keepers that come up from below (ep12's) come from there. No story has shown it, and none should show more than a glimpse.
- **What the Keepers are to each other.** Kin, all the same first batch. They never meet, because each is tied to its own place. They're never evil. They're also never "kind all along": they're doing a job, and it's eerie because they do it without caring how it looks to us.
- **Pets count** (added with `warm-shoes`, 2026-11-03). A home's count includes the animals that live in it, as a dog counts aboard a boat. A house of two people and a cat is a place built for three.
- **Some animals stand up tall at night** (`warm-shoes`). A house cat can stand up as a far-too-long person after sundown, in whatever shoes it finds that day, and gets small again at its own gate, leaving the shoes behind, warm. It's the animal's own shape, never a Keeper, and it does it for the people it loves. Nobody in any story knows why. Don't explain it.
- **Some of the Makers' work keeps things other than counts.** (Set by `dryer-nine`, 2026-11-03.) A few Maker-built objects sit inside newer buildings doing a job nobody remembers giving them: the town's night is dried in dryer nine at the all-night laundromat, a riveted iron dryer under the same paint as the plastic ones beside it. Morning comes when the load is done. No Keeper is involved, and nobody in any story knows who built it.

- **Batch 7 (scripted 2026-11-03, ep80 to ep84).** What these make true:
  - **A street can have a Keeper, and a model can show it** (`ep80-snow-globe`). The Stander has stood in the middle of one street's road since before its houses, where it can count every door. People can't see it, only the dry spot it leaves in snow and the way cars swerve. Grandpa Gus's snow globe is a Maker-built model of that street (its base is one seamless block of pale old stone, the "older object" thread), and the tiny figure in it is the Stander. Shaking the globe snows on that one street and moves the Stander to wherever the globe's snow settles last, brushing past everyone on the way. It never touches anyone. It only stands and bends to look. "Never shake Grandpa's snow globe" is a people-made rule. Nobody in the story says any of this.
  - **A house can sit in a street's gap** (`ep82-no-door`). The doorless lemon house is a Keeper far too big, sitting in the eighth lot of a street built for eight houses, so the street's count holds. The town's pizza a night is a people-made rule from long ago: a house that gets a delivery is counted as a household. Unfed, it stands and turns toward the nearest lit door. It never walks on screen, never touches anyone, and sits back down when the delivery is in its lot.
  - **Not every strange thing is a Keeper** (`ep81-eleven-steps`, `ep83-free-fries`, `ep84-pick-door`). The Climber on our stairs is unexplained, like the tall night cat: nobody knows why it climbs exactly ten steps, and no story says. The diner hatch and the white room of doors are simply how those places are. None of them carries or makes a tally.

## Other things a count can do
(Added 2026-11-02, because most of the parallel batches used the same shape: a Keeper standing in someone's empty place until they come back. These are all true in the canon, and each makes a different twist.)
- **The count is too high, and the extra one is us.** The narrator, or someone they love, is the stroke that shouldn't be there, and the Keeper's job is to make the number right.
- **Leaving breaks the count.** A Keeper can't stop anyone leaving, but a place built for four goes on counting four. The empty chair is set, the coat is hung, and the person who left starts to fade from photos instead.
- **A count kept for someone who's gone for good.** The Keeper isn't waiting. It's mourning, the only way a counter knows how.
- **A count that's wrong on purpose.** People lie to a Keeper (a name on a list, a ticket bought for nobody) to protect someone. The twist is the lie.
- **Two places count the same person.** Someone belongs to two places at once (two homes, two crews), and two Keepers' tallies both include them.
- **The Keeper is the one being counted.** Something bigger than the Keeper keeps a count too, and the story ends on the Keeper's own stroke.
- **The rule was never about the Keeper.** The town's rule protects people from something else entirely, and the Keeper is just standing nearby, counting.

## The Buhkeeper (recurring host)
(Added 2026-11-03 with `rule-three`, the pilot. Reuse this design exactly in every later episode, so he reads as the same man.)

**Who he is, and how he fits the canon.** The Buhkeeper is the channel's host and avatar (`brand/avatar-800.png`; RULES "The channel is Buhtown"; `docs/research/branding.md` section 6). He is a person, an ordinary adult man, not a Keeper, and "Buhkeeper" is a nickname for the job, keeping Buhtown's rulebook, nothing to do with the creatures. In these notes "Keeper" with a capital K alone still means only the creatures, and he is always "the Buhkeeper". On screen he's named only as "the Buhkeeper", if at all (the pilot never names him), and nobody names the creatures. His town is Buhtown, a bright hill town, and his own Shorts say "Buhtown" once, in line 2, never line 1. Each night at nine he stamps one new rule into the town's huge red rulebook in the record room of the town hall. **He doesn't make the rules.** People do, as the canon says: they drop rules on folded slips through the brass slot in the hall door, and he picks one a night. By morning the whole town follows it, out of long habit (it isn't magic that forces anyone, and a stubborn person can break a rule, as Milo does). The old rules at the front of the book are the ones people made long ago to stay on the right side of a count they could feel but couldn't see, and nobody alive remembers why. The creature Keepers have nothing to do with the book. They don't read it, write in it or keep it. They only count. The Buhkeeper has never seen one, and no episode shows him with one. If a Keeper ever appears in Buhtown, it's only standing in some place's gap, counting, like everywhere else. The red book he holds on the avatar is this same rulebook, drawn hand-sized for the channel picture.

**He never says "Buhtown" aloud** (orchestrator, 2026-11-03, pending the owner's choice: the synthetic voice makes "Buh" plus "town" sound like "butt town"). Spoken lines say "this town" or "the town". BUHTOWN appears on screen only: a caption may read it, and it goes in titles and packages. The pilot's line 2 is spoken "Not in THIS town. Not ONCE."

**The book's own laws (true in every episode):**
- One rule a night, every night. The page number is the rule number, a big ink-blue numeral in each page's top outer corner. Rules are written in the town's own ink-blue glyphs (blocky loops and bars, never letters), and the Buhkeeper reads them aloud for us.
- A rule ends only when a newer rule says so. **A torn page still counts. It just can't be read.** Rules three and four were printed back to back on the same leaf, so tearing out three took four with it: the page after the stub is numbered 5.
- Rule three (torn out by the Buhkeeper on his first night, fourteen years before the pilot): if the book's keeper ever misses a night, the book goes to his little brother, who never gets a night off again. It holds, so the Buhkeeper never misses a night. He can stamp anywhere, as long as he stamps.
- The town's habit of never asking who tore out rule three is a rule the Buhkeeper stamped on his first night (a page far into the book, its number never shown). When anyone asks, everyone in earshot stops and turns to face the hall.
- Rule four is on the back of the torn page. Nobody has read it, the Buhkeeper included. He folded the page shut at the end of the pilot. It's the next episode's door, if the host continues.
- The lectern is one seamless block of pale old stone, older than the brick hall (the "older object inside a newer room" thread). The book itself is the town's, made by people.
