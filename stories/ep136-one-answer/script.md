## The big idea
**An echo that's never wrong answered me twice about opening the door to my brother tonight, a brother who lives alone but said "we". One answer came too fast and one came when nobody gets two, so one of them wasn't my echo.**

- It holds up as a film because the rule gives the viewer the tools. The echo always says your name first and always answers on three (L12), and you only ever get one answer (L01, the post). The two messages each break a different piece of that rule.
- Ten pitched, this one kept: the echo tells the truth in your voice (kept); the echo as a lost person (stand-in, dropped); the canyon answers as whoever misses you (killed ep109, never); a second echo is a future self (ep124, dropped); a canyon that keeps what you shout (a material, dropped); and five others for the same rule (the echo lies on purpose, you hear others' questions, a tally of shouts, a hiker who never came back, a radio in the canyon) dropped as plain or as near ep109 and ep124.
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas": not ep109 (the radio names who to avoid), not ep124 (a call from future me), not ep38 (who you miss).

## The story (the lane's engine)
1. **The rule, then me and my worry (L01 to L03, L13 to L16):** one shouted question off the Rim, answered on three in your own voice, never wrong. My name's Eli. Jonah is coming tonight at eight, and he lives alone, but on the phone he said "we". The mystery lands by about 12 s.
2. **Proof it works for three people (L03b to L12):** so I'd ask the echo, and I've seen it work: Mrs. Avila's bakery, Kofi's job, Mr. Bell's car. It always says your name first, always on three.
3. **My turn (L12b, L17, L20, L21, drop, L18, L19, L22):** I shout, "Who's coming with Jonah?" **Message 1** comes back before I've finished shouting, before the count, with no name first: "Don't open the door tonight. The other one knows your name." The creepy detail is that it names "the other one", someone who shouldn't exist.
4. **Message 2, from the same echo (L23, L23b, L24 to L26):** waiting in my truck as the canyon goes quiet at dusk, I whisper, "What do I do?" On three exactly: "ELI. Open the door. Jonah's SCARED." It says the opposite and raises the stakes: my brother is frightened, and he needs me to open the door tonight.
5. **No answer (L27 to L32):** the post says one answer. It's eight, and somebody is knocking on my window and saying my name. "The FIRST came too fast. The SECOND shouldn't have come at ALL. Which echo do I trust?"
- **The marker (the rule meter):** the post at the railing painted with a huge cream "1". It's in frame in every railing shot, and through the windshield in every truck shot.
- **How the narrator is the exception:** everyone else's echo answered once, on three, with their name. Mine broke the rule both ways.
- **The loved one at stake:** my big brother Jonah.
- **The loop:** L32's question leads back into L01's rule ("At the Rim, your echo answers ONE question"), which is exactly the rule the two answers can't both keep.

## Clarity test (cold, two sentences)
At a canyon overlook your echo answers one question, always after three seconds and always starting with your name, so Eli asks who's coming with his brother Jonah, who lives alone but said "we". He gets two opposite answers, one too fast and without his name saying don't open the door, and one on time but a second answer, which shouldn't happen, saying open it because Jonah is scared, and now somebody is knocking.
