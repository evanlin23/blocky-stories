## The big idea
**In our town, if you miss your old house you slowly turn its colors and never see it on yourself, so the one going sky blue with yellow stripes, for the forty days I spent putting the old house back for Mom, is me, the seventeen-year-old who dodges every hug.**

- **Why it clicks:** the rule's fine print is said in the first fifteen seconds as plain world-building (you never see it on yourself, it rubs off on what you touch, you're always a little cold, and a hug from home washes it out), and each line is me: blue fingerprints on the new white fridge that I blame on Mom (L10), a yellow stripe on my own pillowcase (L12), three sweaters in July (L13), Mr. Pratt asking every day if I'm settling in (L14), and every hug dodged (L08, L11). Even the care is a clue: the sky-blue swing and the yellow stripy curtains I put back are the old house's colors, the ones I miss.
- **The misdirect is love:** Mom keeps saying the new place isn't home yet, so every morning I check her hands for blue, and I spend the summer putting the old house back for her.
- **Why the film never shows me blue until the reveal:** the rule says you never see it on yourself, and the story is told from my eyes. My own shots show me as I see myself. Only the things I touch (the fridge, the pillowcase) carry the color, and the reveal shot is the first one from Mom's eyes. That's the fine print doing the work, stated in L01, so a rewatch is fair.
