## The big idea
**I think my birthday keeps restarting because it went wrong, but in a real do-over only I would remember and everyone else would do the day the same, and everyone is doing it better: there's no do-over at all. My whole block, led by Mom, is rehearsing my surprise party on me, resetting the clocks and making the rain with a garden hose every time I nearly catch on.**

- **Engine:** the staged world (premise-engines engine 12), kept human and handmade, as the table asks. No film's set, show or crew.
- **Why it clicks ("oh, THAT's why"):** line 1 carries the clue. In this town only the person whose day went wrong remembers a do-over (L03), so everybody else should do today exactly the same every time. They don't: Pearl trips, then steps over the mat, then holds the door. Mom spills her coffee, then catches it. The rain falls only on our window because it comes from Mr. Okafor's hose. Pearl's hands sparkle more each time because she's been glittering the cake. And nobody says happy birthday because it's a surprise.

## The story
- **Premise:** In this town, when your day goes wrong you get it again, and only you remember. I work the morning counter at the corner bakery, today is my birthday, nobody has said so, and today has now happened five times. Each time, everybody does it a little better.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.4 s):** "Today has happened FIVE times. / Everyone's getting BETTER at it." L02 and L03 give the rule in under 3 s.
- **The relatable want or fight:** wanting someone, anyone, Mom most of all, to remember my birthday.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a calendar page inset in the top-left corner, our own design: a big 12 and a small badge with the count of todays (x5 in S01, x1, x2, x3 in the montage, x4 at L20, x5 from L25).
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L04 to L06, 6 to 12 s):** Mr. Lund's dropped wedding cake and his second Saturday (L04), Mrs. Dunn's burned toast and her second Monday (L05), the street that never notices (L06).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** line 1 (five todays, everyone improving), then my birthday nobody mentions (L08, L09, by 15 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** a do-over is supposed to happen only to the one whose day went wrong. Everyone else seems to be in it with me.
- **The loved one at stake:** my mom, named at L16 in the same shot as her.
- **The rule broken:** L20, L21: I say it out loud. Is everyone pretending?
- **First real scare:** L22 to L25 (about 35 to 40 s): the whole bakery freezes, every head turns to Mom, she checks her watch and tells everyone "from the top", a rehearsal's reset cue, and next morning it's today again.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** in a real do-over only you remember and everyone else does the day the same. Everyone here did it better, so they remembered (L33 to L35). No new magic: the rule just can't explain what I've been seeing.
- **Twist and reveal (L26 to L43, 41 to 72 s):** said plainly over one picture (S37, the overhead of the whole block on tape marks around the cake, Mom with her clipboard, Mr. Okafor's ladder at the edge): "It isn't a DO-over." "It's a REHEARSAL." "My whole block / has been practicing / my surprise party / ON me."
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** I sneak back in, and tomorrow I'll act surprised. "Today happens AGAIN. / And I'll be / BETTER at it." runs into L01: now "everyone" includes me.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L11, S11: the rain falls only on the bakery window. The street behind it is dry and sunny.
  2. S13 to S15: Pearl's hands have a few specks of glitter, then more, then sparkle.
  3. L17, L18: Mom spills her coffee the first time and catches it the third. She remembers.
  4. L03: only you remember a do-over, which is why the improving is wrong.
- **Ending shape:** a warm secret kept, and a loop with me joining in.
