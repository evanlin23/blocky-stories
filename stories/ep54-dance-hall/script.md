## The story

- **Premise:** In a town where everyone is born with half a glowing glyph on the back of one hand and a match out there with the rest, I play the records for Aunt Ros's Tuesday ballroom class in the old upstairs dance hall, in white gloves, and never dance. Ros has looked for her other half for forty years. This autumn her half is fading, she dances every demonstration with her arms round nobody, and the floor starts creaking behind her in a stride far too long.
- **Rule:** "In this town, you're born with half a mark. Your match has the rest." Halves glow gold on the back of a hand, and when two halves touch they glow whole.
- **Twist:** the rule never says which side of the hand. My half is on my palm, hidden every Tuesday under my record gloves, and Ros's match has been carrying her records for years. A match was never only a love match. It's whoever you fit with. The first line turns over: "your match has the rest" first reads as romance and by the end means family.
- **Where the twist is planted:**
  - Plant 1, the gloves: L09 ("I play the RECORDS / in my record GLOVES. / I NEVER dance."), and the careful wording at L06 ("The backs / of MY hands / are BLANK.").
  - Plant 2, the collar: L18 to L19, when Ros straightens my collar after class her half glows bright for a second. She blames the lighting (L20).
  - Rewatch reward, not narrated: under L13, my gloved hand rests on the record player lid as she dances past, and a faint gold shows through the cotton at the palm for about 0.8 s.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** L71 repeats L01 word for word over the two of us in hold. Now "your match" is my aunt and "the rest" was on my palm, so the line the hook sold as romance is about kin. The sting (L73) lands cold: the place in the middle of the floor still gets filled whenever I'm late.
- **The first person taken:** Ros, at about 44 s (L24 to L27). Gone off-screen, her dance shoes left side by side in the middle of the floor, toes toward where her partner would stand, the record still turning. She went to the station to meet a stranger whose half might match, an ordinary reason. Back by midnight (L34), unhurt. The Keeper never takes or touches anyone.
- **Ending shape:** two in dance hold in the middle of the floor, one whole glyph glowing between her hand and my palm, then a cold low shot of Ros warming up alone before class, her partner's spot empty, and a creak too far from the last.
- **Warmth:** kinship. We're each other's match. Shown as the two of us dancing the demonstration badly while the class laughs and claps, and her straightening my collar with our halves whole.

## World

An old upstairs dance hall above a row of shops, on Tuesday nights in autumn. A narrow wooden stair comes up from a street door. The hall is long and high: a honey maple sprung floor worn pale in a ring where couples circle, primrose plaster walls with cream pilasters, three tall arched windows of indigo night, a row of bentwood chairs along the walls, and one big brass chandelier of glass drops hanging low over the middle of the floor. At the far end on a small table stands an old wooden record player with a brass-cornered lid and a crate of plain-sleeved records.

- **No text anywhere.** Record labels are plain gold discs with a drawn ring, sleeves are plain card, no posters, no name on the hall or the street door. The bus stop has a shelter and a plain post with a coloured disc, no route number or name.
- **The opener** is a sunny bus stop on a bright street of painted shopfronts with blank signboards (colour only).
- **What's true underneath (never said):** the demonstration dance in this hall is a place built for two. For years Ros has danced it with nobody in her partner's spot, so the count is short by one, and the hall's Keeper stands in the gap. Each week it stands nearer that spot, until it stands right over it. When I step in, the place is filled by a person and it's gone. It carries no glyph, because a Keeper carries none of the town's markers. It never touches anyone, and it doesn't care about Ros. It only counts.
