## The story

- **Premise:** My sister Pip and I run a stall off the back of our car at the Sunday car-boot sale. In this town your coat changes colour when you change your mind, and mine has been white my whole life, which I put down to knowing my own mind. Pip is offered a job abroad, and her coat drifts through every colour for a week, then sticks on grey. On our stall everything turns to face her empty chair, and one foggy dawn her coat lies on the table without her, still changing colour by itself.
- **Rule:** in this town, your coat changes colour when you change your mind. Nobody in the story questions it or explains it.
- **Twist:** a coat only changes when you change your mind, and you can't change a mind you never made up. My coat was white because I'd never decided anything. Every time Pip asked what I thought, I said "whatever you want", and she was waiting on me. The first line first reads as a strange law I'm proudly immune to. After the climax it reads as an accusation: mine never changed because there was never anything there to change.
- **Where the twist is planted:**
  - Plant 1, "whatever you want": I say it every night (L17) and my coat doesn't even shimmer (L18). Said again at the bus shelter (L40), the same flat white.
  - Plant 2, the white stallholder in the montage (L07 to L08): white all morning while he prices one teapot, yellow the moment he picks a price. White is the colour of not deciding, shown in the first 15 s.
  - Rewatch reward, not narrated: in L09 and L10 my coat is the exact cream-white of the stallholder's in L07.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** in L69 to L75 the chair is folded away, I remember what sits in an empty one, and the first line comes back with "So I NEVER / change MINE." Having made my mind up once, I hold it shut like the boot. The rule that was a joke at 0:02 is now something I'm afraid of.
- **Ending shape:** one chair at the stall, one coloured coat, the second chair folded flat in the boot. (The coach going over the hill comes just before it, at L68.)
- **Warmth:** encouragement. I tell her to go, then show it: I load her bag myself and drive her to the coach.
- **The loophole the climax turns:** the rule's own logic. A coat changes when its wearer changes their mind, and a mind never made up can't change. Pip's coat is stuck on grey because she's waiting for my answer. I make my mind up out loud ("Go. I'll be fine."), my coat fills with colour for the first time, and hers settles. No new magic.

## World

A sloping grass field outside town on Sunday mornings, churned to mud along the two rows where the cars park. Thirty-odd cars back onto the rows with their boots open, each with a pasting table or a blanket of second-hand things in front: lamps, teapots, odd shoes, a sofa on the grass, crates of plates, a radio. A hedge and a five-bar gate on the low side. Beyond the gate a narrow lane climbs a hill, and halfway up it stands a small wooden bus shelter with a bench. Fog lies in the dip of the field at dawn and lifts by mid-morning.

- **Our stall:** a faded cream estate car, tailgate up, a folding pasting table in front, two green canvas camp chairs side by side behind the table (Pip's on the left as we face the stall), a steel flask, two mugs.
- **What's true underneath (never said):** our stall was built for a count of two, the two of us. When Pip stops sitting at it, the count goes short, and the Tilted, this stall's Keeper, sits in the gap so the number holds. It doesn't want her chair. It just can't leave it empty. When she sits in it again it's gone. Once I fold the second chair away there's no seat for it, as long as I never unfold it.
