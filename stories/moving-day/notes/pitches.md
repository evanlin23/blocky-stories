Each pitch gives its opening lines as caption chunks split with ` / `. CAPS are the key words. Cues in square brackets are for the picture, never read.

## What all three keep (row 4)

- **Cast:** me, an adult, and my mum. We've moved into a smaller house because the old one was sold. Nothing more is said about why. Mum is tired and hasn't laughed in months. She thinks I'm just tired, and she explains nothing.
- **The Boxes:** a tall figure built only from our taped brown moving boxes, with a black marker face on its front box. It's the same box every time, with the same line weight, but the drawing is new each time we see it. It's never seen moving. Each morning it's in a different room, standing very still among the other boxes. Its colours are cardboard brown, black marker and packing-tape shine. No box has a word on it.
- **First line:** mid-scare.
- **Warmth:** home. Mum laughs again and we unpack together the old way, shown in the picture.
- **Twist:** everything it moves goes where it lived in the OLD house, and it's built from the old house's boxes. The 3 a.m. scare over Mum's bed is it tucking the old quilt round her.

## A. "Where it lived"

4. Mum's line about the mug, at L12 (about 24 s). She means the old kitchen, and she's too tired to hear herself.

**Strange mornings.** Each adds one new picture:

**The twist:** I work it out and say it plainly. In the old house Mum's room was the cold one, and she slept under that quilt all winter. Her mug lived on the old kitchen windowsill, and the photos hung up high, under the old house's tall ceilings. "Everything / it MOVED / was where / it LIVED / BEFORE."

**The warm turn, on screen:** the next morning Mum shuffles into the front room in the old quilt and looks up at the photos, half of them bent over onto the ceiling. It's a held two-shot in low gold sun, with the mug on the sill catching it. Her shoulders go first, then her face, from grin to squeeze, with a tremble and a lean. She laughs till she has to sit on a box. The score turns to the major. The words are few: "Mum looked UP / at the PHOTOS." **The Boxes at the end:** when the last box is empty, the figure is gone. Mum opens the hall cupboard and finds a neat pile of folded boxes. On top sits one box still made up and taped shut, with a new face already drawn on it, a smile. Nobody asked for it and nobody drew one first.

- Mum sitting on a box needs the seated pose checked against the box.

**The rule the viewer learns by 5 s:** it only changes when nobody's looking. Then the gap shrinks. The first face came overnight, this one took ten seconds, and the next takes a blink. The wording never says "each time".

**Strange mornings.** Each adds one new picture and a shorter gap:

2. (the cold open) It's in the kitchen. The stare came in the ten seconds I spent at the kettle. Mum's mug is out on the bathroom windowsill, and she says, "That's where I always KEPT it."

**Risks:**

- It's the biggest build of the three. The front room exists in two states, plus a flower pattern drawn on box faces. It's still one set.py, and the creature is still one model, with its boxes reused as the room.
- The drawn flowers must read at phone size without crawling on pans. Flash pre-check the room reveal at the final's frame steps.
- My watering eyes need tears, which faces.py has, and blink false for the held-open stare.
- The box sofa sagging under Mum's lean is a soft prop move in animate(). Nothing falls and nobody drops.
- "Out of ITSELF" can read as the Boxes giving themselves up for us, a cousin of "kind all along". The lines say only what's in the room and give no motive.

## My pick: B, "Looking away"

- **It has the scariest middle.** The rule is clear by 5 s, and the gap keeps shrinking: overnight, then ten seconds, then a blink. So the viewer is watching the face with me, and every morning is a real escalation instead of one more strange thing.
- **The scare and the reveal are one cut.** Over Mum's bed, I hold my eyes open until I can't. When I blink, the arms are on the quilt. The reaching arms are on screen for about five seconds, and one cut undoes them.
- **The twist is shown in one picture.** The old front room in cardboard explains the mug, the photos and the quilt without a line of explaining.
- **Mum's laugh is earned.** It comes from a plant she set up herself at about 19 s, and it plays in the picture.
- **It's the furthest from "kind all along".** The Boxes are never thanked, never befriended and never there to reply. They end as a room, and their last face is a window.
- **It stays out of every other lane.** No other story in either batch has a blink rule or a rebuilt room. The only other face that changes is floor-thirteen's panel of lit buttons, and a marker drawing on cardboard is nothing like it.
- **The cost:** it's the biggest set build. The front room in two states is worth it, because it's the twist and the warm turn at once.

If the build time isn't there, A is the safe fallback. It's the cheapest, it follows the row most closely and it keeps the working title as its first line. I'd keep C's "Thanks, love" out of this batch, because it brushes mirror-maze's lane.

B's opener isn't the working title. B keeps "taped from the INSIDE" at L06, so the title still fits the story, and the upload title is the packager's call anyway.

## Redo pitches (writer, 2026-11-02): rule first

Held because L01 wasn't the world's rule. Same set (the small low house, hall, kitchen, front room, the stack by the door, the hall cupboard), same cast (me, an adult, and Mum), and the best old scenes (photos up at the ceiling, the quilt at 3 a.m., "ladder height", tape from the inside). Ten big ideas, each as its line 1 and its "wait, what".

1. **"In this town, a house grows to fit the tallest one living in it."** Our old house was always a ladder taller than any of us. The new one fits me, until a gap opens over my head each morning, and the photos ride up the stretching walls. Reveal: the old house never broke the rule. Something tall has always lived with us, and it packed itself, taped from the inside, and came along. **Kept.**
2. "In this town, houses shrink when nobody laughs in them." Clear stakes (the ceiling coming down on Mum), but the box figure would have to hold the ceiling up off her bed, which is "kind all along". The second reading (my laughs were fake) is small. Dropped.
3. "In this town, anything you pack in a box shrinks to fit it." Reveal: we're inside a box, the old house packed with us in it. A great opening picture (an armchair shrinking into a shoebox), but the mechanics need explaining, which is ep20's confusion. Dropped.
4. "In this town, you can pack away a feeling." Mum packed her laugh when the old house sold. Reveal: the box taped from the inside is mine, and the figure is everything I packed to look strong. Moving, but abstract, and a figure made of feelings is hard to read on a phone. Dropped.
5. "In this town, every house keeps a copy of whoever lived there last." Reveal: the copy in our new house is of me. Edges toward "gone for good" for a living narrator, and it's `double`, close to mirror-maze. Dropped.
6. "In this town, a house puts things back where its last owner kept them." Reveal: this small house was Mum's before I was born, and the mug, the quilt and the photos are her old places here. Neat, but the ladder line and the tall figure stop making sense. Dropped.
7. "In this town, when a house is sold, it keeps one thing of yours." Reveal: it kept Mum's laugh, and the figure is carrying it to her. Courier-kind, too close to "kind all along". Dropped.
8. "In this town, a house is only as tall as the people in it stand." Mum stoops with sadness and the ceiling sinks. Reveal hinges on posture, which doesn't read at phone size. Dropped.

**Why 1.** Every old scene becomes a plant of one idea: the ladder line (the old house's height was never ours), the photos at the ceiling (the walls stretch as the house grows to fit it), the figure folded along the ceiling (always taller than the house has caught up to), the tape from the inside (it packed itself), Mum never looking up (an old habit, from a house where something lived up there). The rule is visible in the first frame (my hair touching our ceiling) and funny in the montage (tall hats are rude at dinner). It says the reveal plainly: "The old house never broke the rule. It was always as tall as the tallest one living in it. That was never us." The feeling: Mum laughs for the first time since the sale, right under it, and closes the cupboard on its box without looking up. Shape `came-with`, not `stand-in`. No Keeper and no tally, because the canon ties a Keeper to one place and this thing moves with a family.
