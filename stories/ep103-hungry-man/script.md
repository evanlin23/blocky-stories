## The big idea
**There is no Supper Man. The first winter without Grandma, Grandpa stopped eating and sent every casserole back, so my mom made up a stranger who needed his supper and the whole street has left him dinner every Friday since, and called it a rule, so Grandpa never has to say thank you. The tall shape in the long coat collecting the plates is Grandpa, and when a plate's missing, he knocks to check you're all right.**

- **Engine:** the cover story (engine 11). The scary rule is a kindness in disguise. No film's figure or town.
- **Why it clicks:** 43 porches, 42 plates (L03, L04), and the one porch with no plate is Grandpa's (L12, L13), the only door the Supper Man has never knocked on (L14). Plates come back washed and stacked in threes (L09), the way Grandma did (L51, L52). The Supper Man showed up the same winter Grandpa sent the casseroles back (L19 to L22). The long coat three porches down (L11) is the coat at our door (L39).
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the Supper Man is a real giant who guards the street (a monster), the plates feed the street's Keeper (canon as twist), he's Grandma (sad and spooky), the plates go to the town's dogs (a gag), I'm the Supper Man sleepwalking (ep86), the rule was made to stop a feud (no feeling), the food goes to a family who can't afford it (a lecture), Grandpa cooks for everyone (a muddle about who leaves what), the Supper Man is from the past (time), the street is fattening something (horror cliche).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not a stand-in, not ep82's fed house (no creature, no disguise as a building), not ep83's someone cooks for you (the street cooks openly, the secret is who eats), not ep12's guard knock (this knock is a man checking on his neighbors). ## The story
- **Premise:** Every Friday, every porch on our street leaves a plate out for the Supper Man, and if you forget, he knocks all night until you open the door. By morning the plates are empty, washed and stacked in threes. My grandpa across the street is the only porch with no plate, and nobody has ever heard a knock there. Last Friday, with Mom at work, I didn't put ours out.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 3.4 s):** "Every Friday, you have to leave / the Supper Man a PLATE. / Forget, and he KNOCKS." The rule and its or-else in one breath.
- **The relatable want or fight:** a proud grandparent who won't let anyone help him.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small round cream plate tag in the top-left with a count of plates out on the street tonight over the porches: 42/43. It drops to 41/43 at L26 when ours is missing, and reads 43/43 at L62.

- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** the count, 42 plates for 43 porches, from frame 1, and the porch with no plate (L12 to L14, about 22 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** I'm the one who thinks the rule is silly, and the one who skips it.
- **The loved one at stake:** Grandpa, named "my GRANDPA'S" porch at L12 in the same shot, and Mom, named at L22.
- **The rule broken:** I don't put our plate out (L24 to L26).
- **First real scare:** L28 to L37 (about 52 to 68 s): something on our steps, three slow knocks, again at midnight, at one, a long coat and an empty basket through the window, my name said through the door.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** the knock is the rule's threat, and it's the rule's whole point: he knocks because he's checking (L53 to L58). No new magic.

Now there are two tall shapes on our street on Fridays, and if your plate's not out, we both knock, which runs into L01.

A street leaves plates out every Friday for "the Supper Man", who knocks all night if you forget, and when the narrator skips it, the knocker at his door is his own grandpa. ## World

- **Grandpa's house:** across the street from ours, a gray-green house with a bare porch, a rocking chair, a lace curtain, Grandma's flower boxes gone a bit wild.
- **Our house:** a cream house with a blue door, a brass knocker, a front window with blinds, our kitchen with a small table.
- **The plates:** ordinary dinner plates, covered with foil or a dish towel, and in the morning stacked in neat piles of three on each step.
- **The basket:** Grandma's big wicker basket with a red checked cloth.
- **No text anywhere:** the plate tag carries numerals.
- **What's true underneath (never said):** nothing supernatural at all. The Supper Man is a story a street tells so a proud man can eat.
