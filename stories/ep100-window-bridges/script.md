## The big idea
**In this town every friendship builds a little bridge between two windows, and burning it makes you both forget the other one ever lived: Eli and I never fought. Every year, the night before his boat ships out, we stand in the middle of our bridge and burn it together so neither of us has to miss the other, and every spring we meet for the first time and are best friends again in a week.**

## The story
- **Premise:** Here every friend you make builds a bridge between your windows, one plank at a time. If you burn one, you both forget the other ever lived, and nobody tells you who you burned. In April I finally meet Eli, the guy across the alley, and by the end of the week he's my best friend, like the brother I never had. Our bridge grows thirty planks in a week, when bridges take years. He already knows how I take my coffee, my dog jumps on him like she missed him, and my mom calls him Eli before I've said his name. Three burnt bridges lie in the alley under ours, and all three ran from his window to mine.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.2 s):** "This is the FOURTH bridge / between my window / and HIS." L02 tightens it: "The other three / are lying in the ALLEY. / BURNT."
- **The relatable want:** a best friend you click with at once, and not wanting to lose him.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** our bridge itself, plus a plank counter in the top-right corner: a small teal plank glyph with our bridge's plank count (3 at L09, 30 at L12, 64 by L24, then 65, 66 a plank a night from L58, past 200 at L61). Frame 1 is the fourth bridge, close, its newest plank at my window's sill, Eli's teal shutters at the far end.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L08, 4 to 19 s):** bridges between windows all over the alley (L03), one plank at a time (L04), Mrs. Vega's sixty bridges like a web (L05), two old guys' bridge so old it turned to stone (L06), burning one (L07), nobody telling (L08).
- **A concrete mystery by 15 s:** three burnt bridges lying in the alley (L02, at 2 s), and our bridge growing thirty planks in a week (L12, 26 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** bridges take years. Ours took a week (L12, L13).
- **The loved ones at stake:** my best friend, Eli (named at L09 at his window across the alley, waving), plainly a friend: "like the brother I never had" (L11). My mom (named at L18 at our table), who knows and has promised not to say.
- **The rule broken:** nobody breaks it. The rule's own silence (L08: nobody tells you who you burned) keeps it hidden.
- **The eerie middle (L25 to L33, 48 to 62 s):** the cold nights come and the three burnt bridges lift off the cobbles, black and cracked, swaying up toward our windows a little higher every night. Nobody else's burnt bridges ever move. At 3 a.m. the oldest one is resting on my sill.
- **Twist and reveal (L44 to L48, 81 to 93 s):** said plainly. "We never FOUGHT. / Every year, / the night before / Eli's boat goes OUT, / we stand in the MIDDLE / and burn it TOGETHER, / so neither of us / has to MISS the other. / And every SPRING, / when he comes HOME, / we meet / for the first TIME." The picture under it: the two soot handprints side by side on the unburnt middle plank.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic (L49 to L61):** Eli pockets the matches ("So I'd have to MISS you?" "GOOD."). The rule says burn it and you forget. It never says a bridge has to stop at a window. So we keep it, he ships out, I miss him all winter, and every night our bridge grows one more plank out past his window, over the roofs and out over the sea toward his boat. No new magic: a bridge grows with the friendship, and it's still growing.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** in April I'll walk him down to the alley and tell him: "This is the fourth bridge between my window and his." The opening line becomes the first thing I'll say to him when he comes home remembering me, for the first time in four years.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L14 to L18: he knows how I take my coffee, my dog acts like she missed him, Mom calls him Eli before I've said his name, sets him a plate like she always has, then goes quiet.
  2. L22, L23: a box of matches on his windowsill, and he doesn't even light candles.
  3. L24: all three burnt bridges ran from his window to mine.
  4. Unnarrated: in S01 and S12 a coil of rope and a sea bag hang on the hook inside Eli's window, and in S09 his forearms are sunburned in April.
- **Ending shape:** a turn of the rule done on purpose, a goodbye that keeps him, and a loop that becomes a welcome.
- **Warmth:** a best friend, shown: coffee passed across the bridge, cards on the bridge with Eli cheating, the bridge out over the sea at dusk.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. Nobody is ill, gone or hurt. Eli works the boats every winter and comes home every spring. Missing him is the warm choice the story ends on.

## World
One alley in a bright old seaside town: cobbles, tall narrow apartment houses in terracotta and ochre render with teal shutters, flower boxes, laundry lines, and dozens of little bridges of planks and rope strung between facing windows at every height, some new and pale, some grey with age, one at the far end turned to smooth stone. The alley opens at its end onto a glittering sea and the town's roofs stepping down to it. My window is on the third floor, Eli's faces it across the alley, three meters away.
- **The burnt bridges:** three, lying across the cobbles under ours, charred black and cracked, each with one unburnt plank in its middle bearing two soot handprints side by side and a date cut into it (shown as notches and a numeral 9 and the month glyph, never readable text beyond the narration).
- **At night (L25 to L33):** the three burnt bridges lift off the cobbles and sway upward toward our windows, never fast, never seen moving: each shot finds them a little higher.
- **The fourth bridge in winter (L58 to L61):** it grows past Eli's window, plank by plank, up over the roofs and out over the sea, a thin line of planks catching the dusk.
- **No text anywhere.** The plank counter is a glyph and numerals. The ticket (L42) is a plain card with a boat glyph.
- **What's true underneath (never said beyond the reveal):** a bridge grows as fast as the friendship, and a burnt one remembers what the two people don't. Burnt bridges between two people who keep finding each other rise toward their windows when the same night comes round again. Others remember, but the town's habit is never to tell.
