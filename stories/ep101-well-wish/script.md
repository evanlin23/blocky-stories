## The big idea
**I wished at the town well that I'd never lose my big brother, and the well, which grants every wish word for word, made it so he physically can't get more than one block from me. The way out is in the words: you can only lose what you don't mean to let go of.**

- **Engine:** the literal wish (engine 6). No film's wish, genie or line: our well, our wording, our loophole.
- **Why it clicks:** every montage wish (L04 to L07) is granted by its exact words, which teaches the rule before it's mine. "Lose" is the word that matters, and it's planted in L01, L11 and L29. Wes never asking why (L18) pays off when he says he heard me (L38).
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: I wished he'd always be there and he's stuck on our porch (no loophole), the wish was his not mine (a muddle), the well grants the opposite (no rule), he wished to never lose me back (a swap), the wish ends when I forget it (crowded memory theme), the well wants a coin back (a fetch quest), he's fading the farther he goes (ep31's fading), I wish him away and he forgets me (sad), the well grants it to whoever says it next (a gag), my wish moved the town to follow him (too big to film).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not a stand-in, not forgotten, not time, not ep52's lost things coming home (nothing travels here, a person is held at a fixed distance), not ep100's on-purpose forgetting. Shape `literal-wording`.
- **The rule is the clue:** "I'd never LOSE him" first reads as love. Then it's a leash. After L33 it's a promise kept the only way that frees him.

## The story
- **Premise:** Our town well grants wishes word for word. Three years ago, the night before my big brother Wes moved out, I wished I'd never lose him. Since then he can't get more than one block from me. This week he got his dream job across the country, and I heard him turning it down.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 3.0 s):** "I wished I'd never lose my BROTHER. / Now he can't get / one BLOCK away." Rule and price in one breath, aimed at me.
- **The relatable want or fight:** wanting your big brother to stay, and knowing you're holding him back.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag in the top-left: a road glyph and Wes's distance from me in blocks, a thin bar that fills toward a red notch at 1. It sits at 0.3 or so all story, hits the notch whenever he tries to leave, and at L34 to L36 runs past it and turns into a climbing mile count (600 by L41).
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L02 to L07, 3 to 16 s):** the well's coin drop (L02), Mrs. Kemp's summer yard in the snow (L04, L05), Mr. Hobb's bus waiting at his corner (L06, L07).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** the meter's red notch at one block, and Wes's car stopping dead at the corner (L13, about 25 s). The notch is on screen from frame 1.
- **How the narrator is the exception:** everyone else's wish was for themselves. Mine was for someone else.
- **The loved one at stake:** my big brother Wes, named at L08 in the same shot as him.
- **The rule broken:** I go back to the well to undo it, and a wish can't be undone (L25 to L27).
- **First real scare:** L13 to L16 (about 25 to 33 s): his car stops at one block, then rolls back on its own and parks itself at our curb, his own feet walk him home.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** the wish says I'd never lose him. You only lose what you don't mean to let go of. So I let him go, on purpose (L28 to L33). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L28 to L35, about 50 to 66 s):** said plainly (L30) and shown in one picture (S35: his car passing the green town-line post, the meter bar sailing past its red notch).
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** his text says he heard my first wish three years ago and always knew (L38). He gave up three years so I wouldn't feel bad. Then he's back on our porch on Sunday, 600 miles, because he wanted to be. L42 runs into L01 as the thing he teases me about.

  2. L18: he never asked me why. (He already knew.)
  3. L24: on the phone he says his brother needs him close, not that he's stuck.
- **Ending shape:** a loophole in the wish's own words, then a loop as a family joke.
- **Warmth:** letting go so he can choose to come back.
