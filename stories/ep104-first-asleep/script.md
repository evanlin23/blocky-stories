## The big idea
**Whoever in town falls asleep first, everyone dreams their dream, and for three years the whole town has had the same nightmare of a giant in a blue cap coming up every driveway, because the first one asleep every night is our dog, and the monster is our mail carrier.**

- **Engine:** the shared dream, whose dream is it (engine 22). Comedy lane. No film's dream or line.
- **Why it clicks:** the giant has a cap and a bag full of paper (L04): mail. Mr. Bixby says it looks a little like him (L07). The dream comes out of our chimney, from the dog basket (L09 to L11). Every dog's oldest enemy.

## The story
- **Premise:** In our town, the first person asleep each night picks the dream for everyone, and their dream rises out of their chimney like lavender smoke. For three years, everyone has had the same nightmare. I stay up to see whose it is.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.6 s):** "If you fall asleep FIRST here, / the whole town / has your NIGHTMARE."
- **The relatable want or fight:** your dog versus the mail carrier.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag in the top-left with a moon glyph and a nights count: 1,095 (three years of the same nightmare). It ticks to 1,096 at L10 and resets to a heart-shaped 1 at L17. In every night shot, the lavender dream smoke curling from one chimney is the rule itself.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L02 to L07, 3 to 15 s):** the giant on the driveway (L03, L04), the whole town waking up screaming at six (L05), Mr. Bixby with bags under his eyes (L06, L07).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** whose chimney the smoke comes from (L08 to L10).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** I'm the one who stays up to find out.
- **The loved one at stake:** our dog Waffles, named "Our dog, WAFFLES" at L12 in the same shot, and the whole town's sleep.
- **The rule broken / the scare:** the giant itself (L03, L04, about 5 to 9 s), shown first.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** if the dog picks the dream, change the dog's mind about the mail carrier (L15, L16). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L12 to L14, about 22 to 27 s):** said plainly (L13: "The monster / is Mr. BIXBY.") over one picture (S13: Waffles in his basket, growling in his sleep, the lavender smoke rising up the chimney, and in the smoke the giant's blue cap).
- **How the last seconds turn over the first line:** Mr. Bixby gives Waffles a treat, then another, and the whole town dreams about a big red ball. Waffles is still first every night, and last night the whole town woke up screaming about our VACUUM (his nightmare, shared), which runs straight into L01.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L04: a bag full of PAPER (mail).
  2. L07: Mr. Bixby says the giant looks a little like him.
- **Ending shape:** a comic reveal, then a loop that threatens a new nightmare.
- **Warmth:** comedy, a dog and his mail carrier make peace.
- **Sad family reveal?** No.

## Clarity test (cold, two sentences)
In this town, whoever falls asleep first makes everyone dream their dream, and for three years everyone has dreamed of a giant mail carrier because the first one asleep is the narrator's dog, who hates the mail carrier. The mail carrier gives the dog treats, the town dreams of a red ball, and now the dog's having nightmares about the vacuum.

## The opening recipe (playbook v1), point by point
1. Line 1 is a condition (`if`) with its price. 12 words, about 2.6 s.
2. Frame 1 is a close-up of one chimney at night, lavender smoke curling out of it, the moon tag at 1,095, and the town's other chimneys dark. No wide.
3. A sound hit on "NIGHTMARE": a low soft boom and a swell.
4. The danger is in line 1: the nightmare is everyone's.
5. L02 to L07 show the rule at work, one to three shots per line, none over 2 s.
6. The title is the rule's danger, never the dog.
7. The whole short is under 45 s, so the twist lands by about 20 s and the rest is the sting.

## World
- **The town:** a sunny small town of pastel houses, every one with a brick chimney, a long hill of driveways and mailboxes, a little white mail truck.
- **Our living room:** a fireplace, a plaid dog basket beside it, a couch, a window to the street.
- **The dream:** a moonlit driveway under a purple sky, a streetlight behind, a porch with a low roof, a mailbox.

## The creature (the dream giant)
`docs/research/creature-playbook.md` section 2, applied while writing:
- **Wrong silhouette (rule 1):** a man whose arms hang past his knees and whose neck is too long, so his capped head bends forward under the porch roof. At a glance it is a too-tall stooped person, not a robot, mascot, snowman, appliance, box, dummy or costume. It's only ever in the dream.
- **What's stretched (rule 2):** legs and arms about 1.8 times the cast's, the neck long, shoulders narrow, hands at the end of long sleeves below the knees. Built from `kit.box` in `set.py`.
- **Scale proof in the reveal shot (rules 3 and 13):** S03b: low camera at a sleeper's chest height, framed head to knees, the porch roof's edge pressing his cap down, the mailbox at the height of his knee.
- **The one lit identity cue (rule 12):** the light-blue cap brim and the brown mailbag strap, caught by the streetlight. The rest of his front is in shadow.
- **Light (rule 5):** from behind, the streetlight at the end of the driveway giving a hard rim on cap and shoulders, and a cold lavender bounce from below off the dream smoke. No warm front light on him.
- **Eyes and mouth (rules 7, 8, 15):** `eyes: "none"` in shadow under the cap, two tiny `kit.emit` glints only, no mouth.
- **Where it is in each beat:** L01 none. L03 S03a his long shadow on the driveway, S03b the full reveal at the porch. L04 S04a his hand hanging past his knee, S04b the bag of paper. Then only as a shape inside the dream smoke at L13. Never on screen after L13. Mr. Bixby himself is an ordinary-sized man and never stretched.
