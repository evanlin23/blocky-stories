## The big idea
**Grandma's two diaries are a pair, so whatever's written in one appears in the other: for a week I thought my sister's diary was answering me, and she thought her diary was answering her, and the "ghost" on each side was the other one of us.**

- **Engine:** two people haunting each other without knowing (a two-way reversal through paired objects), our own version. No film's plot, place or people.
- **Why it clicks ("oh, THAT's why"):** the diary knows things only family would know (it's my sister). It writes "right as she wrote it" (live). It says "I can hear you" (our rooms share a wall) and "I know you're in the house" (we both were). It begs "please leave me alone" and "I'm going to find you" (she's as scared as I am). Grandma said they only work as a set. Then she's at her desk, terrified, writing "Who ARE you?" while the same words crawl across mine.
- Twelve pitched, this one kept. Dropped: it's her ten years from now (time, the owner's door example), the house writes it (ep82's house that is somebody), I sleep-write the answers (ep86's sleepwalking), she knows it's me and pranks me (no fear on her side, so no click), a thing in the diary she keeps on the page (a protective-sibling twist, ep81's shape), the diary answers whoever reads it about themselves (no reveal), the replies are from the diary's last owner (a ghost, sad), it answers with tomorrow's entry (time), she stopped writing a year ago and the diary writes for her (sad), the diary is a phone line to a twin house (a copy, ep62), the answers come from Grandma (sad), the diary is ranking us (no picture).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not forgotten, not a stand-in, not a reflection, not time, not a copy. Two people, one ink, each other's ghost.
- **The rule is the clue:** "My sister's diary answers back" first reads as a haunted diary. After the reveal, it's literal: the answers were my sister.

## The story
- **Premise:** Years ago Grandma gave my big sister Nina and me matching diaries, lilac and blue, and said they only work as a set. Mine stayed blank in my drawer. Nina's 19, I'm 17, and we don't really talk. Last week my blank diary started filling with Nina's handwriting as she wrote. I wrote back under her line, "Who is this?", and it answered.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 2.4 s):** "My sister's diary / answers BACK. / And tonight / it wrote about YOU." L02 turns it: "Well. / About ME."
- **The relatable want or fight:** snooping on a sibling, and two siblings who only slam doors and fight over the car.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** the open blue diary as an inset in the top-right corner, our own design, with lilac ink crawling across it whenever it writes and a small pen glyph that ticks a count of answers (1, 2, 3...). At L48 a second inset, the lilac diary, appears beside it with the same words crawling in both. Frame 1 is the blue diary itself, close, ink crawling.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L15, 3 to 24 s):** the two diaries and Grandma's "only as a set" (L03 to L05), mine blank for years (L06), door slams and the car (L09), then the ink crawling in her hand (L10 to L13) with her days in it: she hates her job (L14), she dented the car (L15).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** my blank diary filling with Nina's handwriting, live (L10, L11, at 15 to 17 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** nobody else knows the diaries work. I'm the one reading.
- **The loved one at stake:** my big sister, Nina, named at L07 in the same shot as her slamming her door across the hall.
- **The rule broken:** L19, about 28 s: I write back.
- **First real scare:** L32 to L38 (45 to 56 s): "I can HEAR you", "I know you're in the house", "please leave me alone", "I'm going to find you", and a lamp clicks on down the hall.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** whatever you write in one shows in the other, so the one thing I'd never say out loud (L60, "I miss my SISTER") is already in hers, and hers is already in mine (L63). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L46 to L57, 67 to 86 s):** said plainly. "Grandma's diaries are a PAIR. / Whatever you write in one / shows up in the OTHER." "It was ME. And mine was HER. / We were each other's GHOST."
