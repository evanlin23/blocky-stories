## The big idea

**The man warning you never to ask is the one who tore out rule three: it hands the town's rulebook to his little brother for good if he ever misses a night, so he hid it, stamped the "never ask" rule himself, and hasn't missed a night in fourteen years.**

The "wait, what" is double: the eerie town habit in the first ten seconds is the narrator's own rule, and his fourteen years of missed birthdays were him keeping his brother free. On a rewatch, line 1 is a man protecting his own secret, every dodge in the montage glances at his window, and the folded page has been in his pocket since his first shot.

### Ten pitches (kept the one in bold)
1. Rule three is the first line itself, passed by mouth since the page went. (A neat paradox, no feeling.)
2. Whoever asks who tore it out goes back and tears it out. (A time loop, too hard to follow in 75 s.)
3. Rule three made the rules binding. It's gone, so the town keeps every rule by choice. (A thought, not a film.)
4. Rule three said the book's keeper never leaves the hall. Mom tore it out years ago and he was free all along. (Close second. The town's habit of not asking had no reason.)
5. He is rule three: the page was pasted into him. (Silly on screen.)
6. Rule three said "there is no three", and the town skips the number everywhere. (A gag for an ultra-short, parked in ideas.)
7. Whoever asks gets torn out of the town, a ragged paper gap where their house was. (Strong picture, but a person gone is the big beat, and no warm turn.)
8. Rule three says someone must never know rule three, and that someone is him. (Too abstract.)
9. Torn pages can't be crossed out, so tearing a rule out makes it permanent. (Kept as the book's logic: "a torn page still counts".)
10. **The narrator tore it out himself to hide a rule that would hand the book to his little brother, and the town's habit of not asking is a rule he stamped.**

## The story

- **Premise:** I keep this town's rulebook. Every night at nine I stamp one new rule into a huge red book at the town hall, picked from slips people drop through the door, and by morning everyone follows it. Rule three is the only page torn out, and nobody in town ever asks who tore it. My little brother, Milo, is sick of me missing everything for the book, and he says that if I skip Mom's birthday again he'll stand up at the party and ask.
- **Rule (line 1):** "Rule three is missing, and everyone in town still obeys it." (opening type `cost`, per docs/research/opening-variety.md, 2026-11-03). Line 2 carries the warning: "Don't ask who tore it. Not in this town."
- **Twist:** I tore it out, fourteen years ago, on my first night. It said that if I ever miss a night, the book goes to Milo, and he never gets a night off again. A torn page still counts. It just can't be read to him. The town's habit of never asking is a rule I stamped too. Milo asks anyway, I tell him, and the loophole is in rule three's own words: it never said where I stamp. So we carry the book to Mom's together, and I stamp at her table at nine.
- **Where it's planted:**
  - Plant 1, frame 1 (L01): an ink-blue thumbprint on the torn edge of the stub. L08 shows my right thumb, ink-blue from the stamp, in close-up.
  - Plant 2, L07: the corner of a folded cream page sticks out of my coat's breast pocket. It's there in every shot of me until L42, when I unfold it.
  - Plant 3, L03 and L04: as the cook and the mailman dodge, each glances up the hill at the hall's arched window. They're looking at the answer.
  - Plant 4, L13: "In fourteen YEARS, I've never MISSED a NIGHT."
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** first time, "Never ask who tore out rule three" is folk dread about a missing page. By L35 we know the warning is mine and the page is in my pocket. L45, "So do me a FAVOR.", runs straight into L01, so the loop plays as one sentence: a man asking you to keep his secret.
- **Ending shape:** warm and funny at Mom's table (the book among the cake plates), then a cold sting alone at home: the torn page turned over for the first time, rule four on the back, my face falling. Loop.
- **Warmth:** two brothers on the same side. Shown: the two of us carrying a door-sized book down the hill in the dark, Milo walking backward at the front.
- **The sad-family check:** no sad reveal. Mom is at her own party, Milo is fine, and the ending is a joke rule and a sting.

It's on every shot from frame 1. At L43 it flips over, like a page, to show a whole "4", and holds through L45. At the loop cut it's the torn "3" again. (Builder: a post overlay of our own design. - **What the town does:** follows every rule in the book from the morning after it's stamped, without fuss. People made every rule in it (the slips). When anyone asks who tore out rule three, everybody in earshot stops and turns to face the hall. That habit is rule 36,512, my first night's rule, never shown by number.
