## The big idea
**Something far too tall has stood in the middle of our road since before the street was built, Grandpa's snow globe is the only place you can see it, and every shake of the globe moves it to somebody's door.**

- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: our street is inside a bigger globe on someone's shelf (the pitch's own idea, but it is closing-time's reveal in other clothes), each flake is a neighbor's wish (a fun montage, no "wait, what"), a shake shuffles who wakes up in which house (no feeling), the snow covers our tracks from a thing that follows prints (falls apart: snow shows prints), the figures are neighbors glued in so nobody ever moves away (makes Grandpa a jailer), Grandpa came out of the globe (odd, no stakes), the globe is the street's last snow and he's rationing it (mild), the snow is Grandpa's mood (abstract), a shake freezes the street (too close to the premise bank's pause remote), the snow brings back the street as it was (the owner's "door to the past").
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not being forgotten or unseen, not a stand-in, not a reflection, not altitude time, not a count of people. Closest is ep62 (something only visible in glass), but there the glass copies my brother and swaps with him. Here the globe is a map of a thing that has always been on our street.
- **The rule is the clue:** "Our whole street feels it" first reads as the snow and the stumble. After the reveal it reads as "feels IT": the stumble is the thing brushing past everyone on its way to its new spot (L56).

## The story
- **Premise:** On our street, Grandpa Gus's snow globe makes it snow on our street and only ours, and everyone stumbles at once when it's shaken. After every snow, one person-sized dry spot stays in the middle of the road, and every car swerves around it. My driving test is tomorrow, I'm scared, and I shake it for a snow day.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 1.2 s):** "NEVER shake / Grandpa's snow GLOBE." L02 tightens it: "Our whole STREET / feels it."
- **The relatable want:** a snow day to get out of something you're scared of (a driving test with Grandpa, who's teaching me in his truck). Then a fight: Grandpa won't speak to me after the second shake.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** the globe's own minimap. A round inset in the top-left corner of every shot shows the globe from above: the tiny white street, nine tiny roofs, and the tiny figure as a dark blue dot. The dot sits in the middle of the road until L22, then moves with each shake (Mrs. Ortiz's gate, our mailbox, our porch) and goes back at L61. At L72 the dot turns its tiny face mark toward our roof. Builder: a post overlay like `rule-three`'s tag, or a camera-parented disc in the set.
- **The 3 to 5 example montage (L03 to L10, first 18 s):** snow on our street while the next street stays sunny (L03, L04), the whole street stumbling at once (L05), the mailman dropping his bag (L06), Mrs. Ortiz sitting down in her flowerbed (L07), and the dry spot every car swerves around (L08 to L10).
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** the person-sized dry spot in the middle of a white road (L08, L09, at 11 to 16 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** everyone obeys the ban. I break it, three times.
- **The loved one at stake:** my grandpa, Gus, named plainly at L11 in the same shot as him at the window. At L45 to L48 the thing bends to the glass an inch from his sleeping face.
- **The rule broken:** L18, about 32 s in.
- **First real scare:** L22 to L25 (37 to 43 s): the tiny figure has moved, and Mrs. Ortiz's white yard has one dry spot by her gate.
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** a shake moves it, so a shake can steer it. Grandpa puts his hands over mine and we tip the globe slowly toward the road, and the tiny figure slides back to the middle (L59 to L64). No new magic, just the rule used on purpose.
- **Twist and reveal (L49 to L56, 87 to 99 s):** said plainly. "Something has always STOOD in the middle of our road. The globe is the only place you can SEE it. And every SHAKE MOVES it." Then the three details click in order: the dry spot, the swerving, the stumble.
- **How the last 15 seconds turn over the first line:** I pass, Grandpa lets me dust the globe, and then the sting: nobody touched it last night, and the little figure turned to face our house (L71, L72). "Never shake it" no longer keeps it still. The last line ("So no matter what / you want TOMORROW,") runs straight into L01.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L08 to L10: the dry spot is "about the size of a person", and every car swerves around it. Paid off at L54, L55.
  2. L13, L14: the tiny figure in the middle of the tiny road "was a snowman". Frame 1 already shows it there, and the minimap dot is on the same spot from frame 1.
  3. L15: Grandpa tips his hat to the empty road every morning. Paid off at L67, when he tips it after I drive around the middle.
  4. Unnarrated: in S01 the snow on the real street, out of focus behind the globe, has the same bare spot as the globe's tiny road.
- **Ending shape:** a lesson passed on (the truck keys, the duster), then a cold turn in the glass, and a loop.
- **Warmth:** trust. Grandpa doesn't yell. He puts his hands over mine on the globe, and in the morning he hands me his keys. Shown, never said.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. Nobody is gone, hurt or revealed to be anyone else. Grandpa is well and in every shot of the ending.

## World
One street and the front of one house, in bright snow. Grandpa's front room: a big front window with a deep white sill, the globe on it, his green armchair beside the window, a wood-panel wall, a rug. Through the window, our street: nine painted houses in a row each side (mint, butter, coral, sky blue, lilac), white picket gates, mailboxes on posts with no lettering, Mrs. Ortiz's coral house straight across with a flowerbed and a gate, Grandpa's old pine-green pickup at the curb. At the corner the next street is visible, dry and sunny under a clear sky (it never snows there). In the middle of the road, after every snow, one bare dry patch about 0.6 m by 0.5 m. The crosswalk at the corner is painted in a curve around it.
- **The globe:** a glass dome 18 cm across on a heavy base. Inside, our street exactly, tiny and white: nine roofs each side, tiny gates, a tiny green truck, and in the middle of the tiny road one tiny figure (see below). The base is the lore plant.
- **No text anywhere.** Mailboxes, the truck and the test car are plain. The driving test is shown as a cone course in a lot at the corner, with an examiner holding a plain clipboard.
- **What's true underneath (never said beyond the reveal):** the Stander has stood in the middle of the road since before the houses. A shake of the globe moves it to wherever the globe's snow settles last, and on the way it brushes past everyone on the street, which is the stumble. It never touches, takes or harms anyone. It stands, and bends to look.

## The creature: the Stander

- **On the real street:** unseen. Only what it leaves: the dry spot, cars swerving, the stumble. At L42 to L48 snow settles on it and shows it clearly: a person built of our blocks but far too tall (about 2.7 m, its flat hat brim touching the porch roof), with long arms hanging past its knees, outlined in white snow on its hat, shoulders, arms and the top of its face. Inside the snow it's empty air, so the porch light and the window show faintly through the gaps.
