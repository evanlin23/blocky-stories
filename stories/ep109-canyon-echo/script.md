## The big idea
**The canyon answers your name in the voice of whoever misses you most, and mine comes back in my own voice, so I think nobody misses me, but my dad and I sound exactly the same: it was him all along.**

- **Engine:** the answering echo. No myth's character, no film's line: our canyon, our rule.
- **Why it clicks:** the echo comes back a beat too soon (L07), so it was never my echo. Mom mixes us up on the phone (L13). "Bud" is the one word only Dad uses (L10, L11).
- Ten pitched, this one kept. Dropped: the echo is my future self (time), the echo is a Keeper copying voices (a copy, and live hits do voice copiers), the echo is my late mom (a sad reveal, and a death), the canyon is a giant's mouth (a monster with no feeling), it answers in a stranger's voice who turns out to be my birth dad (narrows the audience), the echo answers as my dog (ep104's comedy shape), the echo lies (no rule logic), everyone hears the same voice (no turn), the canyon misses me itself (muddled), my brother and I sound the same (Dad is the clearer stakes).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not a copy or a reflection (nothing imitates anyone, the voice is Dad's own), not ep62's thing practicing being my brother, not the forgotten theme (I'm missed, not forgotten). Shape `same-voice`.
- **The rule is the clue:** "It answers as whoever MISSES you most" first reads as a test I fail. After L12 it says Dad misses me most, in a voice I thought was mine.

## The story
- **Premise:** At a canyon overlook out west, if you shout your own name, the canyon answers in the voice of whoever misses you most. I moved out here in May and Dad hasn't called once. When I shout mine, it comes back in my own voice.
- **Rule / hook (L01, about 3.0 s):** "Never shout your NAME / at the canyon. / It answers as whoever / MISSES you most."
- **The relatable want or fight:** wondering if your dad misses you after you move away, when he never calls.
- **The marker (the rule meter), on screen by frame 1 and in every shot:** a small cream tag in the top-left with a sound-wave ring glyph that pulses once for each shout and once for each answer. A tiny voice glyph inside the ring changes per answer (a heart for the mom, a paw for the dog, a face for mine). At L12 its face glyph gets a little mustache line.
- **The example montage (L02, L03):** a guy hears his mom, a woman hears her old dog bark.
- **A concrete mystery on screen by 15 s:** my own voice coming back, a beat too soon (L06, L07, about 11 to 13 s).
- **How the narrator is the exception:** the only one who hears himself.
- **The loved one at stake:** my dad, named "Dad" at L04 in the same shot as his porch on my phone's screen.
- **The rule broken / the beat:** I shout anyway (L05).
- **The climax turns the rule's own logic:** the rule says "whoever misses you most", never "someone who sounds different from you" (L12, L13). No new magic.
- **Twist and reveal (L10 to L13, about 17 to 25 s):** said plainly (L12: "It wasn't my voice. / It was HIS.") over one picture (S12: my face at the rail, the wave ring pulsing, cut against Dad on his porch at the same moment, hand cupped by his mouth as if calling).
- **How the last seconds turn over the first line:** I call him. This Sunday he drives up and shouts his own name, and it answers in mine. The rule's warning becomes an invitation, which runs straight into L01.
- **The plants a rewatch catches:**
  1. L07: the echo comes back "a beat too SOON".
  2. L13: "Mom still mixes us up / on the PHONE." (said before the reveal is pinned down, in the same breath as it)
- **Ending shape:** a misheard answer heard right, and the same test passed both ways.
- **Warmth:** a dad and his grown son who miss each other and finally call.
- **Sad family reveal?** No. A sting of feeling, then warm.
