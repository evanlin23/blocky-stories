## The big idea
**The sun doesn't rise: every night it comes down to sleep in our backyard, and every morning someone has to wake it and winch it back into the sky, and for thirty years that someone has been my dad.**

- **Engine:** the hidden machinery of the world (the sky is run by hand by someone you know), our own version. No film's plot, place or people.
- **Why it clicks ("oh, THAT's why"):** Dad is up before anyone, sunburned in November, with warm work gloves on the counter. Our yard is the only one without frost (the sun sleeps on it). The sun comes up right over our shed (the winch is in it). Dad watches it rise "like he's checking it's straight". The town's rule exists because whoever sees the sun on the grass gets the early alarm for good, and Dad never wanted that for me. The dark mornings are the sun with nobody to wake it.
- Twelve pitched, this one kept. Dropped: a crew hangs the morning like a stage set (closing-time's "we live in a display"), get up early and you meet yourself (ep62's copy), the extra hour is borrowed from tomorrow (time, ep33's lane), the house has a second family in that hour (confusing), the early hour is when the town is repainted (a world-is-fake reveal), your alarm is set by the person you'll be (time), Dad lives an extra hour each day (time, sad), the early alarm calls you to a test you always fail (no picture), the town is dreamed and the early hour is the dreamer waking (the owner's coma example), the early hour belongs to the town's animals (cute, no stakes), the sun is a Keeper counting houses (stand-in), the early alarm is the house yawning (whimsy).
- Checked against "Claimed big ideas" and every twist: not forgotten or unseen, not a stand-in, not a reflection, not time, not a display. The closest is ep81 (a dad's nightly secret), but there the dad protects me from a creature. Here the dad does a job for the whole town, and the climax is the two of us doing it together.
- **The rule is the clue:** "do NOT get up" first reads as protection from something dangerous. After the reveal it's protection from a job: see it once and the sun's alarm is yours for life.

## The story
- **Who raised the late suns:** Dad, alone, crawling out with his bad back, slower each day (said at L53a, L53b, planted at L29a: fresh sunburn, wrecked on the couch every afternoon).
- **Why the whole street keeps the rule:** anyone who sees the sun on the grass gets the alarm for good (L55, L55a), so nobody gets up.
- **Premise:** In our town, if your alarm rings an hour early, you lie still until it's light. My dad, Frank, is always up first, sunburned in November, and our yard never frosts. On Monday his back goes out. On Tuesday my alarm rings at five, and the sun doesn't come up until nine.
